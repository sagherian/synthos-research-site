The Overview

Apple sells about half a trillion dollars a year of phones, computers, tablets, watches and headphones, and then sells services to the people who own them — cloud storage, music, television, advertising, payments, and a commission on apps. Roughly half its revenue is the iPhone. About a quarter is services, and that quarter carries a much higher profit margin than the hardware.

For three years the standard criticism of Apple was that it had stopped growing. Sales actually fell in the year to September 2023, rose 2% the next year and 6% the year after. Experts we track said so repeatedly and forcefully. That criticism is now out of date. In each of the three quarters Apple has reported so far in its 2026 financial year, sales have grown between 15% and 17% compared with a year earlier — driven by more expensive iPhone models, laptops, and services. Its profit margin on every dollar of sales has also risen every quarter, and in the quarter reported on 30 July it passed 50% for the first time in the company's history.

So the business is doing well. The problem is what you pay for it. The shares have risen 53% in a year while the wider market rose 24%. At $309 you are paying about 35 times what analysts expect the company to earn next year, for a company those same analysts expect to grow its earnings around 10% a year after that. Put simply: the good news is already in the price, and then some.

Three things in the official filings deserve a plain warning. First, some of that record margin came from tariff refunds — money Apple got back after a court ruling — and refunds do not happen twice. Second, Apple says the cost of memory chips is rising and it expects that to get worse. Third, European regulators are running an investigation in which the maximum fine is 10% of Apple's worldwide sales, which would be more than $41 billion, and separate American court cases could change how much Apple can charge app developers and whether it keeps a very profitable search-related payment. Those cases affect the most profitable part of the company.

One more thing you should know: Apple is changing chief executive on 1 September 2026, four weeks from now. Tim Cook becomes chairman and John Ternus, the hardware engineering chief, takes over. This was announced in April.

Financially the company is close to unbreakable. It holds about $132 billion of cash and investments against roughly $99 billion of borrowings, so it owes nothing on balance — it is about $34 billion in the black. It generated $99 billion of spare cash last year and spent $89 billion of it buying back its own shares.

Our estimate of what the shares are worth is $326, against a price of $309. That is a 5% gap, and it is below what Wall Street thinks ($341). It is not enough to buy for. If you own it, keep it. If you do not, there is no urgency here.

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Now the honest part. Three separate deductions have to be made.

Deduction one — the tariff refunds are in the margin and they do not recur. The 10-Q, verbatim: "Products gross margin and gross margin percentage increased during the third quarter and first nine months of 2026 compared to the same periods in 2025 primarily due to a different mix of products and tariff refunds, partially offset by higher costs, including memory." The mechanism is disclosed too: "on February 20, 2026, the U.S. Supreme Court issued a ruling striking down certain tariffs previously imposed under the International Emergency Economic Powers Act of 1977. The Company has applied for a refund of tariffs paid… and has recognized any refunds received as a reduction of products cost of sales." The size is not disclosed. Compare the FY2025 10-K, where tariffs ran the other way: "Products gross margin percentage decreased during 2025 compared to 2024 primarily due to a different mix of products and tariff costs." So the year-over-year margin comparison is flattered at both ends — a tariff cost in the base, a tariff refund in the current period.

Deduction two — the company is warning about the cost line, in the strongest language it uses. From the 10-Q's macroeconomic discussion: "The Company is experiencing a period of supply constraints and increasing costs for components driven by factors such as industry supply-demand imbalances for components, including advanced semiconductors, storage (NAND) and memory (DRAM). The Company expects these trends to intensify." And on margins generally: "the Company believes, in general, gross margins will be subject to volatility and downward pressure." This is not boilerplate risk language; it is a forward statement placed in the macroeconomic section of the MD&A, and it is corroborated independently by the knowledge base (odd_lots, 2026-02-16, bearish, conviction 70: "Memory shortage is destroying downstream demand — smartphone/PC cuts, delayed premium launches; meaningful margin impact shows up in second half of 2026").

Deduction three — the FY2025 earnings comparison everyone quotes is wrong. Reported net income grew from $93.736B (FY2024) to $112.010B (FY2025), which reads as +19.5%, and diluted EPS from $6.08 to $7.46, or +22.7%. Both are artefacts. The 10-K discloses that in Q4 FY2024 Apple "recorded a one-time income tax charge of $10.2 billion, net" following the European Court of Justice's confirmation of the Irish State Aid Decision — "$15.8 billion payable to Ireland via release of amounts held in escrow, partially offset by a U.S. foreign tax credit of $4.8 billion and a decrease in unrecognized tax benefits of $823 million" — and that FY2025's effective tax rate was lower than FY2024's "due to a $10.7 billion year-over-year decrease in the provision for income taxes related to the State Aid Decision." Normalising FY2024 net income to $103.936B, FY2025 growth was +7.8%, and normalised diluted EPS growth was +10.6%, not +22.7%. This is visible in the quarterly data too: Q4 FY2024 shows net income of $14.736B on $29.591B of operating income, with a $14.874B tax provision.

What survives all three deductions. Revenue growth of 15.7/16.6/16.4% is a volume-and-mix fact, not an accounting one. Operating margin at 32.62% against 29.99% is a 263 basis point expansion that the tariff refund alone is unlikely to explain in full. Services growth is led by advertising and cloud, which are structural rather than cyclical. 6b. The return bridge (why the multiple moves)

Expected return over 12-24 months decomposes as: EPS growth (+10.1% a year, consensus) + shareholder yield (+2.2%) + multiple drift.

Our base case assumes modest multiple COMPRESSION — 35.1x FY2026 to 30.5x FY2028 — and that assumption is doing most of the work. The mechanics are worth stating precisely, because the guardrail against confusing rolldown with re-rating applies directly here: at a constant $309.15, the forward multiple falls mechanically from 35.1x to 29.0x simply by rolling from FY2026 to FY2028 estimates. That is not compression. Compression is the market paying a lower multiple at each date. Our base of $326 pays 30.5x FY2028 earnings in roughly two years' time against 35.1x FY2026 earnings today — genuine, modest de-rating of about 13%, which offsets most of the 21% cumulative EPS growth and leaves +5.5%.

Why compression rather than a hold? Three reasons, all forward. Growth maturation: consensus itself takes revenue growth from 14.7% to 7.1% over the same window, and multiples follow growth rates. Margin normalisation: a 50% gross margin achieved partly through non-recurring tariff refunds, with the company warning about component costs, is unlikely to be the run-rate. And risk normalisation in the wrong direction: the regulatory tail is getting nearer, not further away, with the Supreme Court and the European Commission both live inside the window.

If instead the multiple simply HOLDS at 35.1x, applied to FY2028E $10.6768, the price is $374.8 — +21.2%. That is close to the bull case and it is the entire bull case. Note what this means: essentially all of the return above the mid-single digits is a multiple assumption, not an earnings assumption. That is the definition of a fragile leg, and it is exactly the leg the most rigorous bear in the knowledge base attacks — not the earnings, but the mechanism setting the multiple.

11. Verdict, kill-criteria and flip conditions

Hold.

What is genuinely good here, stated plainly, because the house bias runs against mega-caps and it should not run past the evidence. Revenue grew 15.7%, 16.6% and 16.4% year over year in the three FY2026 quarters reported, after three years at −2.8%, +2.0% and +6.4%. Gross margin has expanded every single quarter for five quarters and crossed 50% for the first time in the quarter reported five days before this dive. Operating margin reached 32.62%. Greater China reversed a full-year decline. Services is 26.2% of revenue, compounding 13.5%, now led by advertising and cloud rather than App Store commissions. Free cash flow has exceeded $98 billion in each of the last four years on capital expenditure of 3.06% of revenue. The balance sheet holds $33.8 billion of net CASH — a fact no screen in the market displays correctly — and gross debt fell $14.4 billion in nine months. And the knowledge-base lane, 44 independent sources deep with zero company-management claims, has flipped to 26 bullish against 7 bearish since 2026-04-01. The "mature cash cow" thesis that dominated 2025 is dead, and this dive says so against the grain of its own historical claim base.

Why that is still not a Buy. At $309.15 you pay 35.1x FY2026 and 32.2x FY2027 consensus earnings for a company whose own consensus decelerates revenue growth from 14.7% to 9.1% to 7.1% and compounds EPS 10.1% a year through FY2028. Our base fair value of $326 is +5.5%, 4.4% below the street, and the bear-to-bull span is symmetric at −25.6% / +26.1%. Three concrete cautions the filings supply: part of the record margin is tariff refunds that cannot recur; the company says component cost pressure will "intensify" and that gross margins face "volatility and downward pressure"; and the EU can, per the 10-Q, fine up to 10% of annual worldwide net sales — over $41 billion — in a live investigation, while the App Store commission sits at the Supreme Court and the search-licensing revenue is exposed to remedies on appeal. All of that regulatory exposure lands in Services, the highest-margin quarter of the business. Add a chief executive change 28 days away, a stock up 53.3% in twelve months against the index's 24.3%, no insider buying and one real insider sale at $311.02, and the picture is a good business at a full price with a symmetric payoff. That is the definition of Hold.

Pre-registered KILL criteria — what would move this to Avoid:

  • A final adverse determination in the EU Digital Markets Act Article 6(4) investigation, or any fine sized against worldwide net sales rather than a fixed amount.
  • A Supreme Court outcome, or a remanded District Court injunction, that eliminates the commission on purchases made outside an app. The 10-Q's own risk-factor language: "If the Company is ultimately unsuccessful in defending its commission structure… the Company's business, results of operations, and financial condition could be materially and adversely affected."
  • Remedies that end the search-licensing arrangements, which the 10-Q flags explicitly as a revenue stream at risk on appeal.
  • Consolidated gross margin below 48% for two consecutive quarters, which would confirm that the 50.06% print was tariff refunds plus mix rather than a new baseline, and that the memory cost warning has landed.
  • FY2027 consensus EPS revised DOWN below ~$9.00, breaking the earnings leg the entire valuation rests on.
  • Greater China resuming its decline after the Q3 FY2026 reversal.
  • A visible execution stumble in the leadership transition — departures of senior product or operations leadership in the two quarters after 2026-09-01.

Pre-registered FLIP TO BUY (upsize) conditions:

  • A price below ~$278 (the 200-day average) with the FY2027 earnings path intact — that is 30.5x FY2026E and 29.0x FY2027E, where the base case works without any multiple assumption.
  • FY2027 consensus EPS revised UP through $10.00 while gross margin holds above 49% without tariff refunds. This is the single cleanest positive test and one knowledge-base source has pre-registered it.
  • A disclosed artificial-intelligence monetisation arrangement at the $20-30B scale the knowledge base identifies as the only genuine re-rating trigger.
  • Wearables returning to growth after −7.1% and −3.6% years, which would evidence that the new product categories are reaching material revenue rather than merely being announced.
  • Resolution of the EU and App Store matters on terms that preserve the commission structure, removing the largest unpriced tail.
  • Any open-market insider purchase, of which there is currently none.
