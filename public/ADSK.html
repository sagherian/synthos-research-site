The Overview

If a building, a bridge or a machine part is being designed anywhere in the world, there is a good chance it is being drawn in Autodesk software. AutoCAD is forty years old and still brings in $1.8 billion a year. The bigger and faster-growing business is now the platform sold to architecture, engineering and construction firms — $3.6 billion, growing 22%.

The economics are about as good as business gets. Autodesk keeps 91 cents of gross profit on every revenue dollar, and customers pay their subscriptions in advance — so the company collects the money roughly 193 days before it has to deliver anything.

Last year sales grew 17.5%, the fastest in six years. In the most recent quarter they grew 18.4% and operating profit grew 60%, because the company had spent the previous year cutting costs — two separate restructuring programmes, both named in its annual report.

Two things happened very recently that no standard data feed has picked up.

Yesterday, Autodesk completed the purchase of a company called MaintainX. We cannot tell you what it paid — no document we hold says. Three weeks ago it set up a $2 billion short-term borrowing facility, explicitly to help pay for it. So a company that had slightly more cash than debt has just borrowed to buy something we cannot size.

Now the number that matters most and gets least attention. Autodesk pays its staff $788 million a year in shares — about 11 cents of every revenue dollar, the highest of the twelve companies in this batch. Accounting treats this as a non-cash expense, so it gets added back to "free cash flow" and excluded from "adjusted earnings."

That is why you will see two very different numbers for the same year: profit per share of $6.86 under standard accounting, or $11.13 "adjusted." And free cash flow of $2.41 billion, or $1.62 billion if you count the shares as the cost they actually are.

We report both, every time. The company is genuinely cheap on the adjusted number and merely reasonable on the honest one.

Our fair value estimate is $270, about 14% above the price of $237.19. Analysts say $310.20. The stock is 27% below its high and has fallen 20% over the past year while the market rose 24%.

One encouraging detail: in June, a director bought 2,000 shares of his own accord at $189.20 — doubling his holding. The stock is 25% higher now.

  • Downside Risk 6/10. Wonderful economics, a just-borrowed acquisition of unstated size, and heavy share-based pay.
  • Growth Quality 8/10. 17.5% revenue growth accelerating, with real operating leverage arriving.
  • Exponential Potential 5/10. A real platform runway, and an unmeasured technology fork.

Putting a number on it: our fair-value estimate is $270 against a current price of $237.19 — real upside if our numbers are right.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, plus an independent, public-math cross-check further down the page (“Check our number”) so you can judge it for yourself.

2. What the payload does not carry: two restructurings and an acquisition that closed yesterday

The restructurings

The 10-K filed 2026-03-03 names TWO separate plans and the payload contains no restructuring line item at all:

> "...restructuring plan (the '2026 Plan'), to support Autodesk's initiatives to optimize its go-to-market organization and, at the same time, to reallocate resources to Autodesk's strategic pr[iorities]..."

> "...restructuring plan (the 'January 2026 Plan') that marks the final phase of our sales and marketing optimization program..."

> "...restructuring plans initiated during the fourth fiscal quarter ended January 31, 2026 ('January 2026 Plan') and during the first fiscal quarter ended April 30, 2025 ('2026 Plan')..."

So: a plan begun in the quarter ended 2025-04-30 and a second plan begun in the quarter ended 2026-01-31, described as the final phase. That dating explains the comparison precisely: the April 2025 quarter — the base for the 60.1% operating income growth just reported — was the quarter in which the first plan was initiated, and the January 2026 quarter carried the second.

The consequence for this dive is stated rather than smoothed: the 28.0% first-quarter operating margin cannot be cleanly separated from the charges that depressed the comparison, because the payload isolates no restructuring expense and the extract does not carry the charge tables. The margin improvement is real in direction and unquantifiable in composition here, and one quarter is one quarter.

The acquisition, completed one day before this dive

From the 8-K filed 2026-08-03:

> "On August 3, 2026, Autodesk, Inc. completed the previously announced acquisition of MaintainX Inc. ('MaintainX'), pursuant to the Agreement and Plan of Merger, dated as of May 28, 2026, by and among the Company, Matterhorn Acquisition Corp., a Delaware corporation and a wholly-owned subsidiary of the Company, MaintainX and Shareholder Representative Services LLC..."

That is the entire disclosure. No consideration, no share count, no expected revenue contribution, no goodwill allocation.

And from the 8-K filed 2026-07-13, three weeks earlier:

> "On July 13, 2026, Autodesk, Inc. established an unsecured commercial paper program... the Company may issue, from time to time, unsecured commercial paper notes with varying maturities not in excess of 365 days from the date of issue... with the maximum aggregate face or principal amount of Notes outstanding at any one time not exceeding $2.0 billion... The Company expects to use the proceeds of the Notes for general corporate purposes, including to partially finance the transactions..."

Three things follow.

First, the acquisition is debt-financed at least in part, and the facility is $2.0 billion. Autodesk held approximately $114 million of NET CASH at 2026-01-31 on the corrected basis; drawing materially on a $2.0 billion facility moves it to net debt.
