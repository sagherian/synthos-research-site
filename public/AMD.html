The Overview

AMD designs computer chips. Three kinds matter. It makes the processors that run servers in data centres and the ones inside laptops and desktops. It makes the graphics chips that video games run on and, far more importantly now, the accelerator chips that artificial-intelligence systems are trained and run on. And it makes specialised chips embedded inside industrial equipment, cars and medical devices. It does not own factories; it designs the chips and pays an outside manufacturer to build them.

The business is doing extremely well. Sales grew a third last year, to $34.6 billion. In the first three months of 2026 they grew another 38% compared with the same period a year earlier. The data-centre part, which is where the artificial-intelligence money is, grew 57%. The company generated $3 billion of cash from operations in that single quarter. It has more cash than debt. No single customer is big enough to matter on its own if it left.

Here is the thing almost nobody looking at this stock can see. Last October and again in February, AMD signed two enormous agreements to supply accelerator chips to two very large customers. As part of those agreements, it handed each customer a right to buy up to 160 million AMD shares for one cent each. Together that is 320 million shares. The company currently has about 1.65 billion shares. So those two rights, if fully used, represent almost a fifth of the entire company, given away to win the business.

Those rights only become usable if the customers actually buy the chips and if the share price hits certain levels. In other words, they turn into real shares in exactly the situation everyone is excited about. If AMD's data-centre business booms as forecast, existing shareholders own about 16% less of it than they think. If it disappoints, the rights stay worthless, but then so does the forecast. Neither the quoted value of the company nor any of the published profit-per-share forecasts include those 320 million shares. Only the quarterly report filed with the regulator mentions them.

The share price has tripled in a year. It rose another 7% today. And the company announces its latest quarterly results today, after the market closed — meaning by the time you read this, the number is public and this document does not know it.

Our estimate of what the shares are worth is about $455. They cost $518.58. That is not a reason to sell a company this good; it is a reason not to buy it on a day it has already jumped 7% into an announcement.

1. What the business is — and what the vendor got wrong about it

AMD designs and sells processors and accelerators. It is fabless: it owns no leading-edge manufacturing. The profile records 31,000 employees, chief executive Lisa T. Su, NASDAQ listing, and an IPO date of 1972-09-07.

The 10-K filed 2026-02-04 is explicit that there are three reportable segments, restructured in Q1'25:

> "Beginning in the first quarter of fiscal year 2025, we combined the Client and Gaming segments into one reportable segment to align with how we manage our business. All prior period segment data were retrospectively adjusted."

And, critically, on the sub-lines:

> "Each of the Client and Gaming businesses do not qualify as a reportable operating segment, however, the Company continues to separately disclose revenue for each business."

That single sentence is the origin of the vendor's segment defect, and it is worth stating in full before anything else.

The correction. The vendor reports four FY2025 segments; the 10-K shows three; we use the 10-K. seg_prod lists "Client and Gaming" $14.550B, "Data Center" $16.635B, "Embedded" $3.454B and "Gaming" $3.910B, which sums to $38.549B against reported FY2025 revenue of $34.639B — an overstatement of exactly $3.910B. Gaming is not a fourth segment; it is a separately disclosed sub-line inside Client and Gaming, and the vendor has counted it twice. The 10-K settles it precisely: "Client and Gaming net revenue of $14.6 billion in 2025 increased by 51%… Client net revenue of $10.6 billion… Gaming net revenue of $3.9 billion."

2. The single most important thing about this company's numbers — 320 million invisible shares

There are 320 million shares of AMD that exist as a contractual right, are 19.4% of the diluted share count, and appear in no consensus estimate, no quoted market capitalisation, and nowhere in the vendor payload. They appear only in the 10-Q filed 2026-05-06:

> "In October 2025 and February 2026, the Company issued warrants to [two counterparties]… Each warrant provides the holder the right to purchase up to an aggregate of 160 million shares of the Company's common stock at an exercise price of $0.01 per share. The warrants vest in tranches based on AMD Instinct GPU purchase milestones achieved by [the counterparties], their affiliates, or indirectly through authorized third parties, and achievement of specified Company stock price targets… As of March 28, 2026… none of the warrant shares had vested or become exercisable."

The 10-K and the 10-Q together record the commercial context: an October 2025 product purchase agreement to deploy 6 gigawatts of AMD GPUs, first gigawatt on the MI450 series; and a February 2026 amended master purchase agreement for a further 6 gigawatts, first gigawatt on custom MI450-based GPUs plus 6th-generation EPYC CPUs. The warrants were issued concurrent with those agreements. They are, in plain terms, the price paid for the two largest commercial commitments in the company's history.

The structural point, which is subtler than the arithmetic and more important. The warrants vest against accelerator purchase milestones and specified stock-price targets. They therefore do not dilute in the bear case — if the deployments do not happen and the stock does not rise, the warrants expire worthless and the reported share count is correct. They dilute only in the bull case. This means the consensus estimate series and the share count on which it is divided are internally inconsistent: the estimates describe a world in which the denominator they use is wrong. Every forward multiple quoted on this stock, by anyone, is understated by roughly 13-17% in the scenario that justifies the multiple.

We use the fully diluted basis as the primary valuation frame in Section 6 and report the undiluted figure alongside it. Neither is more "correct"; only one is honest about what the buyer receives if the thesis works.

Two secondary basis notes belong here.

Estimates are non-GAAP; reported EPS is GAAP; the gap is purchase accounting. FY2025 epsAvg was $3.9621 against reported GAAP diluted EPS of $2.65 — a gap of $1.31 per share, or roughly $2.14B. The 10-K's All Other note gives the reason directly: $2.3B of amortisation of acquisition-related intangibles, which at the ~13-15% effective tax rate the filings disclose is about $2.0B, or $1.22 per share. Notably, the $1.6B of stock-based compensation is evidently not added back in the FY2025 bridge — AMD's non-GAAP measure expenses it, which is to the company's credit. The FY2024 bridge does not reconcile the same way: epsAvg $3.3149 against GAAP $1.00, a gap of $3.78B against $2.4B of amortisation, implying stock compensation and restructuring were also excluded that year. The non-GAAP definition is therefore not stable across the estimate series, which is a reason to treat the multi-year EPS path with care (Section 8).

Q2'25 was not a broken quarter; it was two offsetting one-offs. The quarter ended 2025-06-28 printed gross margin of 39.8% and an operating loss of −$134M, against roughly 50% margins in the quarters either side — and yet reported net income of +$872M. Both anomalies are explained in the filings:

  • The 10-K: "During the second quarter of fiscal year 2025, the Company recorded approximately $800 million of inventory and related charges on AMD Instinct MI308 Data Center GPU products due to new U.S. export restrictions on certain semiconductors to China… During the fourth quarter of fiscal year 2025, we began shipping products and reversed approximately $360 million." Adding $800M back to Q2'25 gross profit of $3.059B gives $3.859B on revenue of $7.685B — 50.2% gross margin, exactly in line with Q1'25's 50.2%. The operating loss becomes +$666M.
  • The 10-Q filed 2025-11-05 supplies the other half: for the nine months to 2025-09-27 the company recorded an income tax benefit of $558M, "including a discrete tax benefit of $853 million related to the release of uncertain tax positions pertaining to the reasonable cause relief for dual consolidated losses approved by the Internal Revenue Service in April 2025." With Q1'25 and Q3'25 tax provisions of $123M and $153M, the implied Q2'25 tax benefit is approximately $834M — which is what turns a −$134M operating loss into +$872M of net income.

6b. The return bridge (why the multiple moves)

Expected return over the medium horizon decomposes as: EPS growth (very large — consensus FY2026 $7.51 to FY2028 $19.46, a 61% compound rate) + multiple drift (COMPRESSION assumed) + shareholder yield (negative — the buyback does not cover stock compensation and the warrants add up to 19.4% of the count).

The base fair value of $455 is 35x FY2027 consensus non-GAAP EPS of $13.873 on a share count adjusted for partial warrant vesting — a lower multiple than the 37.4x the market pays today, and applied to a smaller per-share number. The justification for compression is not pessimism about the business; it is three things the arithmetic forces: growth maturation (a 59% FY2027 revenue increase cannot be repeated indefinitely and consensus itself has it decelerating to 35% by FY2028), the mechanical arrival of dilution as the milestones are hit, and the fact that a 2.23x FY2027 estimate range is itself a risk premium the market must eventually price.

Almost all of the return available in this name is earnings growth, and none of it is yield. That is the correct shape for a company at this stage, but it means the entire investment case is a forecast, with no valuation floor, no dividend and no meaningful buyback beneath it. The one leg the thesis cannot afford is multiple expansion, and we do not assume it. The bull case at $700 assumes reality lands near the top of the analyst range and the multiple holds around 42x — both, not either. That is the fragile combination.

Watch.

Data Center grew 57% in the quarter with segment operating margin recovering to 27.6%. Gross margin has climbed from 44.9% to 52.8% in three years. The independent knowledge base is unanimous in its admiration for the management. This is one of the best businesses in this batch and nothing below contradicts that.

Why the verdict is nevertheless Watch. Four things, in order of weight.

One: 19.4% of the equity is invisible. The 320 million penny warrants appear only in the 10-Q. They are in no consensus estimate, no quoted market capitalisation and nowhere in the vendor file. They vest on accelerator milestones and stock-price targets — they arrive in the success case and stay away in the failure case, which means every published forward multiple is understated by 13-17% precisely when it matters. Fully diluted, the stock is 43.1x FY2027 consensus, not 37.4x, and the effective market capitalisation is $1.022 trillion, not $845.6B.

Two: the price already occupies the optimistic half of an enormous range. Twenty-nine analysts put FY2027 EPS between $8.58 and $19.14. The price is 60.4x the low and 27.1x the high. Base fair value of $455 is 12.3% below spot, and the asymmetry runs −55.7% to bear against +35.0% to bull — roughly 1.6:1 against.

Three: the entry is the worst part of the case. Four: the knowledge base's independent lane doubts the specific thing the price requires. Fifty-one non-management assessments across fourteen sources, and every one that addresses competitive position names the same two gaps and, in the most credible case, a falsifiable ceiling of single-digit percentage GPU share. Liking the company is not the same as underwriting the multiple.

What Watch means here, precisely. Do not initiate at $518.58 on 2026-08-04. Read the print. Pre-registered KILL criteria — what would move this to Avoid:

  • H2'26 guidance implying materially less than ~$14.3B of quarterly revenue, which would break the FY2026 consensus of $50.217B and, by extension, the FY2027 path that carries the multiple.
  • A warrant tranche vesting while the stock is flat or down — dilution arriving without the growth that was supposed to pay for it. This is the specific compound failure the structure creates.
  • Gross margin falling below ~50% on a non-charge basis, which would validate the independent-analyst claim that accelerator economics are structurally inferior and that mix shift is margin-destructive.
  • Data Center segment operating margin failing to hold the Q1'26 recovery to 27.6%, i.e. returning toward the FY2025 level of 21.6% while revenue grows — buying share with margin.
