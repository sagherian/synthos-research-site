The Overview

When a company builds a large artificial-intelligence system, it buys thousands of specialised chips. Those chips are useless unless they can talk to each other extremely fast. The equipment that connects them — the switches and the software that routes traffic between them — is what Arista makes.

That has turned out to be a very good place to be. In the three months to June, Arista sold $3.036 billion of equipment and services, 37.7% more than a year earlier and 12.1% more than the previous quarter. It was the first time the company has passed three billion dollars in a quarter.

Two things about those results are unusual. First, the profit margin went up, not down: the company kept 45.4 cents of operating profit per dollar of sales against 44.7 cents a year earlier, and nearly 50 cents on the measure it prefers. Almost every other hardware company in this group has seen margins fall as artificial-intelligence sales grew, because that equipment is usually assembled from expensive bought-in parts. Arista's did the opposite, which is what pricing power looks like.

Second, the company has no debt at all — not a dollar — and holds $10.7 billion in cash and short-term investments. It could not get into financial difficulty if it tried.

The problem is the price. The shares closed today at $190.55, their highest of the year, having risen 62% over twelve months. That is about 60 times what the company earned over the past year, and about 22 times its annual sales. Those are prices you pay for something that will keep growing very fast for a long time.

Two odd details worth knowing. The average analyst price target is $186.67 — below today's closing price — even though 39 of the 52 analysts covering the company rate it a buy and none rates it a sell. That combination means the targets simply have not been updated since this morning's results, and will be raised. Separately, our own data provider's records do not yet include this quarter at all, so everything above comes from the company's own filing.

One more thing, and we report it because it is unusual: our knowledge base — which holds nearly 52,000 recorded views from investors, analysts and industry figures — contains not a single claim about Arista Networks. Not one. For a company worth $240 billion at the centre of the artificial-intelligence build, that is a striking silence, and it means we have no outside opinion to test our own against.

We think the shares are worth about $195 against $190.55 — a 2% gap. That is not enough of a gap to buy on the day a stock closes at its high.

  • Downside Risk 8/10. No financial risk at all; a great deal of valuation risk, plus heavy inventory and concentrated customers.
  • Growth Quality 9/10. Growth that is accelerating at a fifty-percent operating margin. Close to the best there is.
  • Exponential Potential 8/10. A real position in the bottleneck of the AI build — sold into a competitive category.

Putting a number on it: our fair-value estimate is $195 against a current price of $190.55 — real upside if our numbers are right.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, plus an independent, public-math cross-check further down the page (“Check our number”) so you can judge it for yourself.

The single most important line is the margin, and it needs to be read against the rest of this batch. Arista's operating margin EXPANDED — 70 basis points on GAAP and 110 on non-GAAP — while revenue grew 37.7%. The divergence is the whole quality argument: Arista is selling something it prices, not something it assembles.

> "For the third quarter of 2026, we expect: Revenue of approximately $3.3 billion; Non-GAAP operating margin of 48 - 49%."

$3.3 billion is an 8.7% sequential step on a quarter that already grew 12.1% sequentially, at a margin only 90-190 basis points below the one just delivered.

The product disclosure in the same release is the substance behind the margin, and we quote it because it is the only description of the competitive position available in this entire file:

> "Arista introduced the 7060XE7 Series, a next-generation 1.6-terabit portfolio for AI fabrics — The new Etherlink platforms deliver up to 100 Tbps of system bandwidth and support Linear Pluggable Optics, cutting interconnect power consumption by approximately 60% compared to traditional pluggable optics."

> "Arista detailed new innovations for scale-up, scale-out, and scale-across AI fabrics — The company outlined techniques including multi-planar leaf-spine designs, the open Multipath Reliable Connection (MRC) protocol, and segment routing over IPv6 (SRv6), aimed at maximizing AI cluster utilization and resilience."

Power and cluster utilisation are the two binding constraints on artificial-intelligence infrastructure economics, and this company is selling directly into both. A 60% reduction in interconnect power consumption is a quantified, checkable claim about the value proposition, and it is the kind of thing that supports a 49.9% operating margin.

The release also records recognition as a Leader in the 2026 Gartner Magic Quadrant for Enterprise Wired and Wireless LAN, which points at a materially larger and more fragmented customer base than the hyperscalers — the enterprise campus. That is optionality this file cannot size.
