The Overview

Every piece of electronics has to be joined to every other piece. Amphenol makes the joining parts: the plugs, sockets, cables, antennas and sensors that carry power and data between circuit boards, between machines, and around a building. It is not a glamorous business and it is not a monopoly — but it is enormous, and it has one property that matters right now.

When a company builds a datacentre full of artificial-intelligence computers, it needs a very large number of very high-quality connections. Racks of processors have to talk to each other at extraordinary speed, and the cable and connector between them is a real engineering problem, not a commodity. That work is Amphenol's, and it now provides about 36 cents of every revenue dollar the company earns.

The result has been an unusual year. Sales went from $15.2 billion in 2024 to $23.1 billion in 2025 — up 52%. The obvious suspicion is that a company that buys a lot of other companies simply bought the growth. So we checked, and the annual report tells you: of that 52%, 38 percentage points came from selling more of its own products, and only about 13 came from acquisitions. That is a much better result than the headline, and it is the single most important fact in this report.

Three things sit on the other side of the ledger.

First, the company borrowed a great deal of money. In January 2026 it bought a business from CommScope for about $10.5 billion in cash, its largest deal ever, paid for with new bonds and bank loans. Its net debt went from about $4.4 billion to about $14.1 billion in six months. Most stock screens you might look at will still show the old, small number, because the last annual balance sheet is dated 31 December 2025 and predates the deal. That is not a small difference.

Second, when a company buys businesses for more than the value of their physical assets, the difference sits on the balance sheet as "goodwill". Amphenol now has $17.6 billion of goodwill and another $5.3 billion of other intangible assets — together about half of everything it owns. Strip those out and the company's book value is negative $7.2 billion. That does not mean anything is wrong; it means the value is in the businesses working, not in the things you could sell.

Third, the price. The shares are $171.37, which is 2.8% below their highest point of the last year and up 64% over twelve months. Last year the company earned $3.34 a share. Analysts expect about $5.24 this year and $6.37 next year, so you are paying about 33 times this year's expected profit. Our own estimate of fair value is $178. The average analyst says $192. When our number is only 4% above the price and below the crowd's, and the shares are sitting at their high, the correct answer is usually to wait.

  • Downside Risk 6/10. A very diversified, very well-run business — no customer over 10% of sales, seven separate end markets — attached to a tripled debt load, half its assets in goodwill, and a share price with no discount in it.
  • Growth Quality 8/10. 38% organic growth in 2025 and 30% in the June quarter, with profit margins rising in all three divisions. The mark is not a 9 because that organic rate is already slowing — 38%, then 33%, then 30%.
  • Exponential Potential 7/10. Putting a number on it: our fair-value estimate is $178 against a current price of $171.37 — real upside if our numbers are right.

1. What the business is, and how the disclosure actually works

Amphenol describes itself in the profile as a global designer, manufacturer and distributor of electrical, electronic and fibre-optic connectors, interconnect systems, antennas, sensors and sensor-based products. Chief executive Richard Adam Norwitt. Approximately 170,000 employees worldwide as of 2025-12-31, of which approximately 15,000 are in the United States; less than 10% of the U.S. workforce is unionised. Approximately 6,400 employees support research, development and engineering. NYSE-listed, Class A Common Stock, $.001 par value, CIK 0000820313, CUSIP 032095101, IPO 1991-11-08.

The organisational structure is itself the disclosure. The 10-K states that Amphenol runs a "flat organizational structure" with more than 140 general managers running unique, independent businesses, each with income-statement and balance-sheet responsibility, grouped into three divisions that are the reportable segments. This is a federation, not a monolith, and it is why the acquisition programme works: acquired businesses are bolted on as new general-manager units rather than absorbed.

The three reportable segments (from the 10-K and the 2026-07-31 10-Q)

  • Communications Solutions — connector and interconnect systems including high-speed, radio-frequency, power and fibre-optic products; coaxial, fibre-optic and high-speed cable; antennas. Serves IT datacom, mobile devices, industrial, communications networks, automotive, commercial aerospace and defense. This is where CommScope and Andrew went.
  • Harsh Environment Solutions — ruggedised interconnect products, connectors, specialty cable, printed circuits and printed-circuit assemblies for industrial, defense, commercial aerospace, automotive, communications networks and IT datacom. This is where Trexon went.
  • Interconnect and Sensor Systems — sensors, sensor-based systems, connectors and value-add interconnect systems for automotive, industrial, IT datacom, communications networks, defense and commercial aerospace.

The segment table, and the two things the vendor gets wrong

seg_prod is a genuine segment table — unlike several names in this batch, it is not a product table masquerading as one. The 10-K is explicit that no product breakdown exists: "As the Company is not organized by product or group of products, it is impracticable to disclose net sales by product or group of products." So the vendor has the right disclosure. It has two problems with it.

Problem one: the fiscal 2025 line is on a superseded taxonomy label. The vendor's third segment for fiscal 2025 is called "Interconnect Products And Assemblies" — which is the pre-2022 legacy label, the one that appears in the vendor's own fiscal 2010 through fiscal 2021 rows alongside "Cable Products And Solutions". 6b. The return bridge (why the multiple moves)

Expected return over the next twelve months decomposes as: EPS growth (+21.5%, from FY2026E $5.238 to FY2027E $6.366) + multiple drift (COMPRESSION, from 32.7x to 28x on the respective forward year, −14.4%) + shareholder yield (+0.58% dividend, with the gross buyback of roughly 0.37% largely absorbed by option dilution) ≈ +4% to +8%.

Our base case assumes multiple COMPRESSION, and we should be explicit that this is not a rolldown artefact. Today the market pays 32.7x on the current fiscal year's estimate. In twelve months, if it pays 28x on the then-current year's estimate, that is a genuine de-rating of about 14%, not the mechanical effect of the calendar advancing. We assume it because growth decelerates hard on the consensus path itself: revenue +52.1%, then +17.3%, then +13.0%; EPS +56.8%, then +21.5%, then +14.1%. A multiple appropriate to 57% earnings growth is not appropriate to 14%, and the market normally begins discounting the deceleration a year ahead of it. The additional reason specific to this name: the debt-funded balance sheet. A company at 0.3x net leverage deserves a different multiple from the same company at 1.5x with negative tangible book, and the market repriced from the former to the latter over the same six months in which the earnings were rising.

Almost all of the expected return is earnings growth, and the multiple is fighting it. If the multiple simply HELD at 32.7x on FY2027E the price would be $208 (+21.5%); if it compressed to 24x the price would be $153 (−10.7%). The entire spread between a good and a bad outcome sits in a multiple we do not control and have no edge on — and the knowledge base offers no help whatsoever in forming a view about it.

The bull case at $229 does not require multiple expansion at all — it holds today's 32.7x and applies it to the fiscal 2027 consensus high of $6.99. That is a deliberately conservative construction: the bull case is an earnings beat, not a re-rating, and Amphenol has beaten six consecutive quarters.
