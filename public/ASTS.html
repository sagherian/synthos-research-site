The Overview

AST SpaceMobile is building a network of large satellites designed to connect directly to ordinary mobile phones — no special equipment, no dish, just your existing handset getting a signal from space anywhere on Earth. If it works, it solves a genuinely enormous problem. Roughly half the planet has patchy mobile coverage or none.

The problem is the gap between that idea and this company's current financial position. Last year it took in about $71 million of revenue. The stock market values the business at about $25.8 billion. That is roughly 364 dollars of value for every dollar of sales — an extraordinary number by any standard.

It is also burning cash at an enormous rate: about $1.1 billion of cash went out the door last year, and to cover it the company raised $2.6 billion in loans and $1.3 billion by selling new shares. It does hold $2.3 billion of cash today, but it owes $2.2 billion, so almost all of that cash is borrowed. The number of shares has more than tripled in three years, which means existing owners' slice keeps getting smaller.

And the recent operating results have gone backwards. The most recent quarter brought in $14.7 million of revenue when analysts expected $39 million — a miss of 62%. Only seven analysts follow the company at all, and their forecasts for next year's earnings range from a loss of $2.30 per share to a profit of $2.46. That range is so wide it effectively means nobody knows.

One more thing, and it is the piece that tips the judgement. We track a panel of independent experts. None of them has said anything about this company. But several have talked about this exact market — satellites connecting directly to phones — and when they name a company, they name a competitor: one that has spent $17 billion acquiring the radio spectrum to do it and whose connectivity revenue one expert expects to grow fivefold by 2028.

The idea is real. The price assumes it has already succeeded, and the experts we follow are backing someone else.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, We don’t publish a reverse-DCF cross-check for pre-profit companies — negative or missing earnings break that math — so take this number on our modeling alone.

1. Data integrity and the share-count warning — read before any per-share number

  • Market capitalisation of $25,845,783,143 divided by a price of $63.52 implies 406.9M shares.
  • The most recent quarter (Q1 2026) reports weighted average diluted shares of 290,689,457.
  • That is a 40% discrepancy.

The most likely explanation is a multi-class capital structure in which the weighted-average diluted count used for earnings per share excludes classes of stock included in the market-capitalisation calculation, combined with continuous issuance during the period (shares rose from 223.97M to 290.69M across the four quarters of 2025 alone). We cannot resolve it from this file. Consequently: every per-share figure in this dive is approximate, and the scenario analysis in section 6 uses the market-implied 406.9M count — the conservative choice, since it produces lower per-share values. 4a. What today's price assumes (the inversion)

At $63.52 — 363x FY25 revenue and 14.0x FY28E revenue — the price embeds the following falsifiable claims:

  • Revenue reaches $158.9M in FY26 (consensus, 8 analysts). Q1 delivered $14.7M. The remaining three quarters must average $48.1M each — a 227% step up from the quarter just reported, which itself missed its estimate by 62%. This is the most fragile assumption in the price and it is testable in six days.
  • Revenue reaches $684.2M by FY27 and $1.833B by FY28 — a 26-fold increase from FY25 in three years, requiring commercial service at scale across multiple carrier markets.
  • The company reaches GAAP profitability in FY28 (consensus EPS +$0.600), i.e. within thirty months, from a business currently losing $191M per quarter.
  • No further material dilution. The share count has risen 4.7-fold in three years and the free-cash-flow burn is $1.14B a year against $97.1M of true net cash. The price assumes the constellation is funded — and the balance sheet says it is funded for roughly two years.
  • This company, rather than a competing system, captures the direct-to-device opportunity. Our knowledge base contains three claims underwriting a competitor with $17B of acquired spectrum and none naming this one.

4b. The return bridge (why the multiple moves)

Expected return ≈ revenue growth + multiple drift + dilution. We use revenue rather than earnings because there are none, and rather than free cash flow because it is negative.

  • Revenue growth: enormous on consensus (+124% compound to FY30), and entirely unproven — four revenue misses in five quarters.
  • Multiple drift: our base case assumes very heavy COMPRESSION, from 363x FY25 revenue to 8x FY28E revenue, and this is not a bearish assumption — it is arithmetic. A company at 363x sales must de-rate as revenue arrives; the only question is whether revenue arrives fast enough that the price still rises. In our base case it does not. 8x forward sales is a normal multiple for a capital-intensive, capital-hungry infrastructure operator, and applying it to the FY28 consensus produces $36 per share.
  • Dilution: strongly NEGATIVE. With a $1.14B annual burn and $97.1M of true net cash, further equity issuance is near-certain within the horizon. Each of the last three years saw net stock issuance of $64.6M, $551.9M and $1,259.3M. The share count is the third variable in this equation and it has only ever moved one way.

The bridge reads: extraordinary revenue growth, overwhelmed by necessary multiple compression and further dilution. The reason this is an Avoid rather than a Hold is that even granting consensus revenue in full, the fair value is 43% below spot. The bull case does not require us to be wrong about the technology; it requires the market to keep paying an extreme multiple while revenue arrives.

4c. Variant perception (where we differ, what would surprise)

  • Our variant perception is on the multiple and on the competitive outcome, not on the technology. 5. What would change our mind

We want to be explicit about the falsification path, because an Avoid on a genuinely visionary company should carry a clear route back.

This becomes a Watch on any two of:

1. Two consecutive quarters of revenue at or above consensus, establishing that the ramp is real and that the estimate misses were timing rather than capability.

2. A funding solution that does not dilute — a strategic partner, a customer prepayment structure, or cash generation sufficient to cover the burn.

3. A tracked source naming this company — any independent expert conviction at all would move this from zero to something.

4. A price below ~$36, our base case, where consensus FY28 revenue at a normal infrastructure multiple is no longer pre-paid.

It becomes a Stage-In only on all of the above, plus disclosure of a commercial service milestone with recurring revenue behind it.

6. Verdict, and what this verdict is not

Avoid. At $63.52 the enterprise trades at 363 times FY25 revenue and 14.0 times FY28 consensus revenue — consensus that rests on eight analysts and requires a 26-fold revenue increase in three years, from a company that has missed its revenue estimate in four of the last five quarters, including by 62% in the most recent one, and whose EPS missed by 159%. Free cash flow is -$1.136B annually against $97.1M of true net cash, the $2.34B cash balance being funded by $2.24B of debt raised alongside $1.26B of new equity in FY25 alone. The share count has risen 4.7-fold in three years. The vendor's credit grade is D+. And the knowledge base contains zero claims naming this company while carrying three claims underwriting a competing operator with $17B of acquired spectrum. Our base fair value of $36 — which grants consensus revenue in full — is 43% below spot.

What this verdict is NOT. It is not a claim that the technology will fail; we have no engineering data and we say so. It is not a claim that direct-to-device connectivity is a bad market — our own tracked panel is bullish on it, at conviction 55 to 70, across four separate claims. It is not a short recommendation; a stock with a beta of 2.677, an $8-to-$94 outcome range and a binary technical catalyst is dangerous in both directions.

It is a statement that the security offers no margin of safety for an outcome that even its own analysts cannot bound within $4.76 per share of earnings. When the range of expert opinion on next year's profitability spans a loss of $2.30 to a gain of $2.46, the correct position size is zero unless the price compensates for that uncertainty. At 364 times sales, it does not.
