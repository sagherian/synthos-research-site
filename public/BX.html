The Overview

Blackstone manages $1.35 trillion of other people's money in property, company buyouts, lending and hedge funds. It charges an annual fee on those assets and takes a share of the profits when investments are sold well. It is the biggest firm of its kind in the world.

The most recent quarter looked strong. The recurring-fee profit measure rose 22%, the cash-earnings measure rose 26%, and investors put in $68 billion of new money — about $262 billion over the past year. The firm holds $228 billion of committed money it has not yet invested, which is useful when asset prices fall.

And yet the shares are down 19.5% over the past year, while the American stock market rose 24%. That is a 44-point gap, and it is worth understanding why before assuming the market is wrong.

Two things in the company's own numbers explain a lot of it.

First, the recurring-profit figure did not really grow for recurring reasons. Of the $324 million by which it rose, $321 million came from performance fees crystallised on funds whose value is an internal estimate rather than a market price. The genuinely recurring part — the annual management fee — grew about 11%, half as fast.

Second, the biggest of the four divisions is credit — lending money to companies — at $469 billion, up 15%. But the profit that division actually produced fell 6% in the quarter and 17% for the year so far, and the money it returned to investors from selling loans fell 95%. The credit funds returned 3.9% after fees over the past year, which is not what private lending was sold on.

That matters because the sharpest people in our research library have been warning about exactly this. Of the thirteen expert claims we hold about Blackstone, seven are negative and six of those were made this year, from five different sources. One of them names Blackstone directly as a "shadow bank" facing withdrawals and forced sales. Notably, the one source that was positive about Blackstone in October 2025 changed its mind in February 2026.

The shares are $137.18. On the cash-earnings measure the company is expected to produce, they trade at about 18.5 times next year's estimate and pay a 3.8% dividend. Our estimate of fair value is $150, about 9% higher. The average analyst says $145 and the middle analyst says $138.50 — essentially today's price.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. 1. What the business is, and why the GAAP income statement should be ignored

Blackstone Inc., 345 Park Avenue, New York; chairman and chief executive Stephen A. Schwarzman; 5,285 employees; NYSE-listed since 2007. Four reportable segments: Real Estate, Private Equity, Credit & Insurance and Multi-Asset Investing.

The GAAP income statement is not a description of this business, and the vendor payload reproduces it without saying so. Look at the reported revenue series:

Revenue tripled in one year and then fell by more than half the next. That is not a business changing; it is the performance-allocation line — unrealised carried interest marked to the value of the funds — running through the top line. In the twelve months to 2026-06-30 the filing shows performance allocations of $5,675.9M against management and advisory fees of $8,550.4M, so roughly 35% of GAAP revenue is a mark rather than a fee.

Blackstone reports three non-GAAP measures instead and the whole industry values it on them:

  • Fee Related Earnings (FRE) — management fees plus fee-related performance revenues, less the compensation and operating expense attributable to them. Intended to be the recurring core.
  • Distributable Earnings (DE) — FRE plus realised performance revenues and realised principal investment income, net of the associated compensation. This is what funds the dividend.
  • Total Segment Distributable Earnings — DE before certain corporate items.

We use DE for valuation, FRE for quality, and we print the GAAP figure alongside so that no reader mistakes one for the other.
