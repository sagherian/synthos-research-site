The Overview

The Cigna Group does two things. The bigger one, called Evernorth, is a pharmacy benefit manager — it sits between drug companies, insurers and employers, negotiating prices and running mail-order and specialty pharmacies. That business is 85% of Cigna's revenue. The smaller one, Cigna Healthcare, sells medical insurance to employers.

The shares cost $273.98. Analysts expect $33.50 of earnings in 2027, so you are paying about eight times. For comparison, the average large US company trades near twenty times. Cigna generated $8.4 billion of free cash last year against a $72 billion market value — a yield above 11%.

On the surface that looks like a bargain. Three things complicate it.

First, the bigger business is going backwards on profit. Last quarter Evernorth's revenue rose 6% and its pre-tax profit fell 2%. Cigna's own release says the group's profit growth came from the smaller business. A company where 85% of revenue produces shrinking profit is not obviously cheap.

Second, and this is the decisive one: Cigna has stopped buying back its own shares. It spent $7.0 billion on buybacks in 2024 and $3.6 billion in 2025. So far this year, through 29 July, it has spent $250 million. That is a 93% reduction, by a company whose shares are at eight times earnings. When the most informed buyer of a stock — the company itself — withdraws at what looks like a cheap price, that is worth taking seriously, and the filings do not explain it.

Third, there is a live argument that the pharmacy benefit business should not exist in its current form. Our knowledge base contains claims from four separate independent commentators making versions of this point — that these companies "game an opaque system and face antitrust risk", that separating drug formularies from them "would collapse their rent-extraction model", and that reform on rebate transparency is gaining political momentum. None of them mentions Cigna by name. All of them describe the business that produces 85% of Cigna's revenue.

Against all that: the company beat expectations again five days ago and raised its full-year forecast. It pays a 2.2% dividend. Its shares barely move — historically a 10% market fall has moved Cigna about 3%. And of 39 analysts, none says sell, and the most pessimistic target of $302 is 10% above today's price.

Our estimate of fair value is $308, about 12% above the price, with a bear case of $228 and a bull case of $395.

That is a decent risk-reward and we are still not buying it today. We want to see the pharmacy business stop shrinking, or the company start buying its own shares again. Either would change the answer.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, plus an independent, public-math cross-check further down the page (“Check our number”) so you can judge it for yourself.

Annualised, $250 million over roughly seven months is about $430 million a year, against $3,621 million last year and $7,034 million the year before — a reduction of roughly 88% to 94% depending on the comparison. It is 0.6% of a $72.4 billion market capitalisation.

Three readings, and we cannot distinguish them from this archive. Capital may be committed elsewhere — the release states the outlook "includes the impact of expected future share repurchases", so more is planned but unquantified. Cash may be constrained by working capital in a business where net medical costs payable rose 13.4% year on year. Or management may not think the stock is cheap at nine times earnings.

What can be said without speculation is this. Cigna generated $8,389 million of free cash flow in FY2025 on a market capitalisation of $72,396 million — an 11.6% yield — and in the seven months to 29 July 2026 it returned approximately $250 million of that through buyback and roughly $940 million through dividends. A company that can retire 11% of its equity a year from free cash flow, at nine times earnings, and chooses to retire 0.6%, has made a capital-allocation decision that the filings do not explain.

This is the reason the verdict is Watch rather than Buy — Tactical. In the same batch, this house issued a Buy on a name where the treasury and four insiders were all buying. The symmetrical discipline requires that the withdrawal of the best-informed buyer count against the arithmetic here, and it does. The upgrade condition is written as a number: a buyback restarting above $1 billion a quarter.

4a. What today's price assumes (the inversion)

At $273.98 — 9.0x FY2026 consensus adjusted EPS and 8.2x FY2027 — the price embeds:

  • Adjusted EPS of at least $30.45 in FY2026. (Company guidance, raised 2026-07-30; consensus $30.473.) The first half delivered $15.57 ($7.79 plus $7.78), leaving $14.88 — a modest sequential decline that the September consensus of $7.50 is consistent with. This is the least demanding assumption in the price.
  • Adjusted EPS of $33.500 in FY2027, up 9.9%. (Consensus, 15 analysts.) On revenue growth of 4.3%, that needs margin expansion — from a group whose largest segment's pre-tax adjusted income is currently declining 2%. This is the most fragile assumption in the price.
  • Evernorth's pre-tax profit stops falling. (Our derivation; the release supplies the MINUS 2%.) Evernorth is 85% of revenue. If its pre-tax adjusted income declines at 2% a year, Cigna Healthcare has to grow roughly 12% a year for the group to hit the FY2027 consensus — and Cigna Healthcare's medical care ratio is rising.
  • The market keeps paying 8-9x forward earnings. (Our number.) Cigna traded in the low-to-mid teens for most of the decade to 2024. At 7.5x FY2027E the stock is $251; at 11x it is $369. The band is wide because the multiple, not the estimate, carries the risk.
