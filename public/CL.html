The Overview

Colgate makes toothpaste. Also toothbrushes, soap, body wash, dish liquid, and — through Hill's — the therapeutic pet food your vet sells you. About three quarters of it is the personal and home care side; a quarter is pet food.

It is a very good business. Gross margins are above 60%. It sells things people buy every month regardless of the economy. Its shares move less than almost anything else in this batch — a beta of 0.32 means when the market falls 10%, Colgate has historically fallen about 3%.

Last quarter's results, published four days ago, need two readings.

The headline: sales up 4.9%. The reality: the dollar weakened, and currency alone accounted for 2.4 percentage points of that. Prices rose 1.6%. The actual number of things sold rose 0.9%. Strip out currency and acquisitions and the business grew 2.4%.

Underneath that average is a split. In Latin America, sales grew 5.3% excluding currency. In Asia, 5.2%. In North America, they fell 3.0% — and the number of units sold fell 3.9%. Colgate's own explanation is blunt: toothpaste sales are declining in the United States. In its core category, in its home market, this company is shrinking.

Two more things you should know that the data feed does not contain.

First, Colgate is in the middle of a restructuring. It announced one in July 2025 costing $200-300 million. On 30 April this year, the board roughly doubled it to $350-550 million, running until the end of 2028, mostly redundancy payments. Those costs are excluded from the "adjusted" earnings every quarter. When a company excludes the same category of cost for three years running and then makes it bigger, it is worth asking whether it is really an exception.

Second, Colgate bought a French skin-care brand called Filorga a few years ago. In the last quarter of 2025 it wrote almost all of that value off — $919 million before tax — because the business, particularly in China, did not work. That is why Colgate reported a loss in that quarter.

The shares cost $92.54 and are up 10.8% over a year while the market rose 24.3%. Analysts expect $4.07 of earnings in 2027, so you are paying about 22.7 times. Our estimate of fair value is $94 — about 1.6% above the price. The average analyst says $98. Of 45 analysts, 24 say hold.

There is nothing wrong here and nothing compelling. That is Hold, and we would rather say it than dress it up.

  • Downside Risk 4/10. Beta 0.32, low leverage, recession-proof products — with a book value of $0.71 a share and a shrinking home market.
  • Growth Quality 4/10. 2.4% organic, half the reported growth is currency, North America is negative.
  • Exponential Potential 2/10. Toothpaste. The one growth bet was written down to nothing.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, plus an independent, public-math cross-check further down the page (“Check our number”) so you can judge it for yourself.

North America is shrinking and the company says why. Net sales fell 3.0% to $891M, driven by volume down 3.9% partially offset by price up 0.9%, with foreign exchange flat — so organic sales also fell 3.0%. A 3.9% volume decline in the United States, in toothpaste, is the single most important fact in this file. Against that, North American operating margin rose 120 basis points to 21.6% on funding-the-growth savings — so the segment is defending profit by holding price and cutting cost while losing units, which works until it does not.

Latin America is carrying the company, and 8.4 points of its 13.7% growth was currency. Reported net sales rose 13.7% on volume +2.6%, price +2.8% and foreign exchange +8.4% — organic +5.3%, led by Mexico, Brazil and Argentina. A currency reversal in Latin America is the most mechanically dangerous thing that could happen to Colgate's reported numbers.

Three things follow and each is stated as an observation rather than a criticism.

The programme roughly doubled in size nine months after approval. From a $250M midpoint to a $450M midpoint. A restructuring that is enlarged 80% before its first year is complete is either evidence of an ambitious management team finding more savings, or evidence that the first estimate was wrong. The disclosure supports the first reading and does not exclude the second.

It runs to the end of 2028, so charges will be excluded from adjusted earnings in at least eleven more quarters. This house's standing position is that a cost incurred every quarter for fourteen quarters is a cost of doing business, and the GAAP figures — operating profit MINUS 6%, EPS MINUS 5.5% — are reported here alongside the adjusted ones rather than beneath them.

Europe, Middle East and Africa takes the largest regional share at 25-30%, which is consistent with a region where the company is buying volume with a 1.2% price cut.

And it is not the first. The 10-K refers to the prior "2022 Global Productivity Initiative" whose charges ran through FY2024. Colgate has been running a restructuring programme continuously since 2022.

3. The Filorga write-down and what it says about the growth strategy

In the fourth quarter of 2025 Colgate reported a net LOSS of $37M. The inc_q block contains this and offers no explanation. The 10-K does:

> "In the fourth quarter of 2025, we recorded a non-cash charge of $794 aftertax ($919 pretax) to adjust the carrying values of goodwill and intangible assets related to the skin health business. Given lower than expected category growth rates and weaker than expected performance, particularly in China, we have lowered our outlook for the skin health business, primarily Filorga."

And in more detail:

> "the Company concluded that the carrying value of the Filorga trademark and customer relationships exceeded their estimated fair values and recorded impairment charges of $244 and $93, respectively, reducing their combined carrying values to an immaterial amount as of December 31, 2025. After adjusting the carrying values of the Filorga trademark and customer relationship intangible assets, the Company completed a quantitative impairment test for goodwill and recorded a goodwill impairment charge of $582 in the skin health reporting unit, reducing the carrying value of goodwill to $51 as of December 31, 2025."

That is a complete write-off of an acquired brand's intangible value — $244M of trademark and $93M of customer relationships taken to "an immaterial amount", plus $582M of goodwill. The consequences run through the FY2025 income statement: GAAP operating profit fell 23% to $3,306M from $4,268M; GAAP diluted EPS fell to $2.63 from $3.51, of which $0.98 was the impairment; and on a non-GAAP basis operating profit was $4,347M against $4,353M — essentially flat.

The reason this matters beyond the accounting: skin health was Colgate's one attempt to buy into a faster-growing adjacent category. It has been written to a $51M goodwill stub. The remaining growth options in this company are Hill's Pet Nutrition, whose volumes are falling, and the Prime100 fresh-pet-food business bought for AU$471M ($301M) on 2025-04-30, which contributed 0.6 percentage points to Hill's volume this quarter. The exponential score of 2 is a direct consequence of those two sentences.
