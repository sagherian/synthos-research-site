The Overview

Cummins makes the engines that go in heavy trucks. That has been its business since 1919. It also makes the components that clean up diesel exhaust, it runs a large network of distributors that sell and service all of it, and — this is the part that now matters most — it builds large generators.

Something changed about the generator business. Data centres for artificial intelligence need enormous amounts of electricity, more than the grid can reliably supply in the places they are being built. So the operators install their own generators. Cummins makes those. In the most recent quarter that division's sales grew 19% and its profit margin went up while three of the company's other four divisions saw margins go down. In June, Cummins signed a deal to supply generators to a computing data centre in Texas, with deliveries scheduled every year from now through 2030.

Our own research files contain exactly two expert claims that mention Cummins. Both are from the same analyst, both are from September 2025, and both say the same thing: this is now an artificial-intelligence infrastructure company, not just a truck-engine company. Eleven months later, the company's own filing says exactly that. The shares are up 79% over that period. We record that the call was right and we record that it came from a single source, which is a real limitation.

This morning the company reported. Earnings per share came in at $6.73 when analysts expected $7.21 — a miss. The stock immediately fell 8%, then kept falling to 11% down. But in the same announcement the company raised its forecast for the full year, and raised its dividend for the seventeenth year running. By the close the shares had recovered almost all of the loss and finished down 2%.

So what do you do? Our estimate of fair value is $650, which is 2% above the $635.94 price. If things go badly the shares could fall to about $440. If they go well, about $855. That is roughly one dollar of possible gain for every dollar of possible loss — not a good enough ratio to buy.

There is also a specific danger with a date on it. Emissions rules for American trucks change for the 2027 model year, and the company said this morning that it is changing its launch plans in response. What normally happens is that truck buyers rush to buy before the new rules — which flatters 2026 — and then buy very little in 2027. Analysts' 2027 revenue forecasts do not appear to allow for that.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, plus an independent, public-math cross-check further down the page (“Check our number”) so you can judge it for yourself.

We are not buying it at $635.94, and the reasons are four numbers.

First, the payoff ratio is 1.11:1. Base $650 (+2.2%), bear $440 (−30.8%), bull $855 (+34.4%). A bar of 2:1 exists precisely so that a correct thesis at the wrong price does not become a position.

Second, the multiple has done roughly 40% of the work already. Cummins spent most of the last decade at 11-15x forward earnings. It is now 18.6x FY2027. The re-rating from cyclical to structural has happened. What remains is the earnings.

Third, the company disclosed a dated hazard this morning that consensus does not appear to model. The Model Year 2027 North American emissions transition, a "measured production ramp while maintaining select legacy product availability in 2027", against a consensus FY2027 revenue line growing 8.3%. Every prior emissions step-change pulled demand into the preceding year.

Fourth, the shareholder yield is only about 2.45% — a 1.38% dividend and roughly 1.07% of buyback — so there is very little cushion under a return that depends on both the earnings and the multiple.
