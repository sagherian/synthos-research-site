The Overview

Digital Realty owns data centres — the buildings full of servers that cloud companies and artificial-intelligence workloads run on. It is the second-largest listed owner of them in the world.

Business is very good. Three numbers say why.

Existing tenants are renewing at 25.4% higher rents. When a landlord can raise rents a quarter on renewal, it means demand exceeds supply. That is the single most informative number in this file.

The order book is at a record. Digital Realty has signed leases that have not yet started worth $1.4 billion of annual rent at its own share. Against total revenue of about $6.1 billion, that is a large chunk of next year already contracted. It takes about nine months from signing to the tenant moving in, so today's bookings are 2027's revenue.

And profit per share is growing about 14% a year. Management has raised its forecast for this year twice.

Now the problem, and it is a real one.

Building data centres costs an enormous amount. Last year Digital Realty spent $3.18 billion improving and building property, against $2.41 billion of cash generated by operations. So it spent $770 million more than it earned — and it also paid out $1.73 billion of dividends. That gap is filled by selling shares and borrowing. The number of shares has risen about 10% in two years.

That is not necessarily bad. It is how development companies grow, and if the new buildings earn good returns it creates value. But it means the per-share growth is partly bought.

One warning about the data we were given. The file reports Digital Realty's free cash flow last year as positive $2.4 billion. The actual filed figure is negative $769 million. The discrepancy is that the data file records last year's construction spending as zero. It is not zero; it is $3.18 billion, disclosed on the face of the cash-flow statement. This is the largest single error we found across the twelve companies in this batch.

The shares cost $193.885. Management expects about $8.18 of "core" cash earnings per share this year, so you are paying about 24 times. The dividend is 2.52% and is comfortably covered.

Our estimate of fair value is $209, about 8% above the price. The average analyst says $218.

We are not buying it today because 24 times already pays for most of the good news, and because a business that has not generated positive free cash flow in four years deserves a discount rather than a premium for its growth rate.

  • Downside Risk 6/10. Improving credit metrics and a record backlog — against four years of negative free cash flow and 5% annual dilution.
  • Growth Quality 7/10. 13.9% per-share growth and 25.4% renewal spreads, partly equity-funded.
  • Exponential Potential 6/10. The demand curve is genuinely exponential. The asset is concrete and power.

Putting a number on it: our fair-value estimate is $209 against a current price of $193.88 — real upside if our numbers are right.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, plus an independent, public-math cross-check further down the page (“Check our number”) so you can judge it for yourself.

Three observations. The renewal spread is the most informative number in this file. A landlord re-signing existing tenants at 25.4% higher cash rents is telling you that replacement supply is scarce and that the in-place rent roll is materially below market. That is a pricing statement, not a demand statement, and it is the hardest thing for a competitor to erode.

The backlog is a revenue floor. $1.4 billion of annualised base rent at Digital Realty's share, signed but not commenced, against FY2025 revenue of $6,113 million, with a nine-month average lag. Most of the 2027 revenue increment is already contracted.

8. Verdict, kill-criteria and flip conditions

Watch.

The business deserves better than a Watch and the price does not.

What is genuinely excellent, and all of it is filing-verified. Core FFO per share excluding net promote of $2.13, up 13.9%. A record backlog of $1.9 billion of annualised base rent at 100% share, $1.4 billion at Digital Realty's share, against $6.1 billion of annual revenue and a nine-month signing-to-commencement lag — so most of the 2027 revenue increment is already contracted. Renewal leases signed at rental rates 25.4% higher on a cash basis and 32.0% higher on a GAAP basis, which is the clearest evidence of landlord pricing power anywhere in this batch. Quarterly bookings of $208 million at share, including more than $100 million from the 0-1 megawatt plus interconnection category for the first time. Full-year guidance raised twice. Fixed charge coverage improved from 4.7x to 5.2x.

What holds it at Watch, in three specifics.

First, the price. 23.7 times the midpoint of management's own Core FFO guidance is a growth multiple, and our base of $209 — 23.0x an extrapolated 2027 figure — is only 7.8% above spot with 1.75:1 asymmetry. At 20x the stock is $182, 6.1% below today.

Second, and this is the substantive objection: the growth consumes more cash than it produces. On the filed numbers, free cash flow was MINUS $769 million in FY2025, MINUS $570 million in FY2024, MINUS $1,891 million in FY2023 and MINUS $984 million in FY2022 — four consecutive negative years — while dividends rose from $1,451 million to $1,728 million. The difference is funded with equity: weighted diluted shares rose 10.2% in two years, and the June quarter designated 12,310,249 new shares for a single acquisition. That is a legitimate way to build a development business and it is not a legitimate reason to pay a premium multiple.

Third, the renewal spread that makes the current numbers look so good is partly a vintage effect. +25.4% cash reflects leases signed in a looser market rolling into a tight one. As those vintages exhaust, the spread compresses even if market rents keep rising, and nothing in this archive says how much of the rent roll is still below market.

Why not Hold. Because there is a specific, dated, watchable event — the 2026-10-22 print — with three numbers that would change the answer, and because a business compounding per-share cash earnings at 13.9% with a record contracted backlog belongs on a watchlist rather than in the dead file.

Why not Buy. Because 7.8% of upside on 1.75:1 asymmetry, at 23.7x, on a business with four consecutive years of negative free cash flow, is not a position.

Single biggest risk: that growth this good requires more capital than the business generates.
