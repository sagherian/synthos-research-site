The Overview

FedEx moves packages. It is one of two large parcel networks in America and it flies its own aeroplanes.

Something unusual has happened and it makes this company hard to price today. On 1 June, FedEx split itself in two. It gave shareholders shares in a separate company containing its trucking business — FedEx Freight — and kept only a fifth of it, which it has promised to sell within two years. So the FedEx you can buy today is smaller than the FedEx in any set of accounts published so far.

At the same time, and this is the awkward part, FedEx changed the date its financial year ends. It used to end on 31 May. Now it ends on 31 December. To get from one to the other, FedEx will publish a strange seven-month "transition" report covering June to December this year. The practical effect is that the earnings forecasts sitting in every data feed are labelled with dates — "year ending May 2027", "year ending May 2028" — for years that will never happen.

And it reorganised its reporting so the old "Federal Express" division is now two divisions.

None of that is in the data file we were given. Nor is the most useful thing FedEx has done recently: it took the $4.1 billion of cash the trucking business paid it on the way out and used it to buy back $4.86 billion of its own bonds for less than $4.15 billion — because those bonds were long-dated and trading below face value. That is a straightforward 15% gain and it cut FedEx's borrowings by about a fifth.

The last full year was good. Revenue grew 8%. The express division's profit margin improved from 6.5% to 7.2%, which sounds small but is worth roughly $600 million a year. A cost-cutting programme beat its $1 billion target.

There is one detail worth noticing. The trucking business FedEx just spun off used to be its best business — a 16.7% profit margin. In the final year before the separation, that margin fell to 7.0%. FedEx separated the business on the way down.

The shares cost $313.34 and are up 79% in a year while the market rose 24%. Analysts' price targets range from $160 to $479, which is a polite way of saying nobody is sure.

Our estimate of fair value is $295 — about 6% below today's price. We would rather wait for the first clean set of post-split accounts on 17 September than guess.

One more thing worth stating plainly: our knowledge base of 52,021 expert claims contains nothing about FedEx. Not one investor in it has expressed a view on this company in four years. We are reporting that rather than dressing up something adjacent.

  • Downside Risk 6/10. Debt just cut 19%, pension overfunded — against $17.2bn of leases, a $3.3bn tariff-refund dispute, fourteen class actions and an interim finance chief.
  • Growth Quality 5/10. A good year inside five years of 1.3% total revenue growth.
  • Exponential Potential 3/10. A physical network with a cost programme. Nothing compounds.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, plus an independent, public-math cross-check further down the page (“Check our number”) so you can judge it for yourself.

1. What actually happened, in the order it happened

Everything in this section comes from filings dated after the vendor payload's fundamentals cut-off of 2026-05-31, and none of it is in the payload.

December 2024 — the decision. From the 10-K: "In December 2024, we announced that FedEx's Board of Directors decided to pursue a full separation of FedEx's less-than-truckload freight transportation services business conducted through FedEx Freight, Inc."

13 May 2026 — the declaration. "On May 13, 2026, the FedEx Board of Directors declared a pro rata dividend of 80.1% of the outstanding shares of common stock of FedEx Freight Holding Company, Inc. ... to FedEx's stockholders of record as of the close of business on May 15, 2026."

1 June 2026 — the spin-off. "On June 1, 2026, FedEx stockholders received one share of FedEx Freight common stock for every two shares of FedEx common stock held as of the Record Date. FedEx retained 19.9% of the outstanding shares of FedEx Freight common stock. As a result of the Spin-Off, effective June 1, 2026, FedEx will no longer consolidate the FedEx Freight business and FedEx Freight is no longer a reportable segment." The 10-K adds: "we will divest our ownership interest in FedEx Freight within twenty-four months from the Spin-Off."

1 June 2026 — the fiscal year change. From the 8-K filed 2026-07-21: "Effective June 1, 2026, FedEx Corporation changed its fiscal year end from May 31 to December 31. As a result, FedEx will report operating results covering the seven-month transition period from June 1, 2026 through December 31, 2026 (the 'Transition Period') in a Transition Report on Form 10-K. Following the Transition Period, FedEx will report its operating results on a calendar-year basis, beginning with the fiscal year ending December 31, 2027."

1 June 2026 — the segment realignment. "Following the Spin-Off and beginning the first quarter of the Transition Period, we realigned our internal reporting and management structure, resulting in the identification of two new reportable segments: Express U.S. Domestic and Express International. Prior to this change, these two segments comprised the Federal Express reportable segment. In addition, FedEx Logistics, Inc. ... was moved from 'Corporate, other, and eliminations' to Express International."

8 June 2026 — the dividend reset. "On June 8, 2026, our Board of Directors declared a quarterly cash dividend of $1.22 per share of common stock. The dividend of $292 million was paid on July 7, 2026." Fiscal 2026 dividends paid were $1,374M on roughly 240 million shares — about $5.73 per share. The new rate annualises to $4.88, roughly 15% lower, which is what happens when the spun business begins paying its own dividend.

14 July 2026 — the debt retirement, and it is the most valuable single transaction in this file. From the 8-K filed 2026-07-10, announcing the early results of a cash tender offer opened 25 June: FedEx offered to buy back nineteen series of notes maturing between 2028 and 2065, subject to an Offer Cap of $4,150,000,000 of aggregate purchase price, on a waterfall by acceptance priority. It accepted the full amount tendered in priority levels 1 through 12 and 41.3% of level 13, and rejected levels 14 through 19.

Summing the accepted principal across those thirteen levels gives $4,857 million of face value retired for an aggregate purchase price of at most $4,150 million — a discount of approximately 14.6% to par, because the accepted series are long-dated low-coupon notes (the 2.400% notes due 2031, the 3.250% notes due 2041, the 4.500% notes due 2065) that trade well below face. Against financial debt of $25,714M at 2026-05-31, that is a reduction of roughly 19%.

And the funding source is the spin-off itself: "The Company intends to pay the purchase price for the Notes... with the proceeds of the approximately $4.1 billion dividend received from FedEx Freight Holding Company, Inc. in connection with the spin-off of FedEx Freight on June 1, 2026, together with cash on hand."

20 July 2026 — governance. The board approved an Executive Severance Plan replacing the prior Management Retention Agreements, and disclosed a one-time special cash bonus pool for approximately 1,100 managing directors and above, with the chief executive receiving $1,900,000 and Brie A. Carere $850,000. The stated rationale is worth quoting because it is management's own scorecard on the year: "above-plan adjusted consolidated operating income, structural cost savings that exceeded management's publicly announced target of $1 billion, successful execution of key transformation initiatives, and disciplined capital allocation." Half-weighted as management framing, and it is a board resolution rather than an investor-relations statement, which is a slightly better class of evidence.

Three readings, in order of importance.

First, the core parcel business improved and the improvement is real. Federal Express segment revenue rose 9% to $82,273M and operating income rose 21% to $5,912M, lifting margin 70 basis points. The 10-K attributes the revenue gain to "improved package base yields, increased U.S. domestic package volumes, higher fuel surcharges, favorable exchange rates, and growth in international freight volumes, partially offset by negative impacts from the expiration of our contract with the U.S. Postal Service and global trade policy changes." Two of the five positive drivers — fuel surcharges and exchange rates — are pass-throughs rather than earnings quality; the yield and volume improvements are not.

Second, FedEx separated FedEx Freight in the year its margin halved. From 16.7% to 7.0% is a 970-basis-point collapse on roughly flat revenue (down 1%), taking $873M out of operating income. The spin was announced in December 2024, when Freight was the group's highest-margin business, and completed in June 2026, when it was not. That is not an accusation of bad faith — less-than-truckload is a violently cyclical industry and the timing of a two-year separation process is not chosen to catch a margin peak — but a reader assessing whether the spin created value should know that the asset distributed to shareholders had just lost more than half its profitability.

Third, consolidated operating margin FELL despite everything. 5.8% against 5.9%, because Freight's collapse and $771M of separation and fiscal-year-change costs more than offset the Federal Express improvement.

On the adjustments. The 10-K itemises $1,148M of items affecting operating income in fiscal 2026 — spin-off costs $738M, business optimization $366M, asset impairments $23M, fiscal-year change $33M, offset by a $12M legal credit — and $405M net of items affecting net income, of which the largest single line is a positive $497M mark-to-market retirement plans adjustment. That is why the fourth-quarter adjusted EPS of $6.31 is BELOW the GAAP diluted EPS of $6.60: FedEx books its pension mark-to-market in the May quarter and excludes it from adjusted earnings. Adjusted fiscal 2026 diluted EPS is approximately $20.24 against consensus of $19.86.
