The Overview

Alphabet is the company that owns Google Search, YouTube, Android, Gmail, Google Maps, the Google Cloud business, and a collection of side bets including the self-driving car company. Roughly three-quarters of what it earns still comes from selling advertising space.

Two things happened in the last three months that matter more than anything else, and both are easy to miss.

The first is that the profit figure stopped meaning anything. In the three months to June, Alphabet reported a profit of $112 billion on sales of $119.8 billion — a profit margin of 94%, which no company on earth actually achieves. The reason is that Alphabet owns stakes in private companies, including the rocket company SpaceX, and accounting rules make it run any change in the estimated value of those stakes straight through its profit figure. The company's own quarterly filing says the quarter included $99 billion of such gains. Its actual operating profit — what the businesses earned — was $40.8 billion.

This matters because every stock screen in the world now shows Alphabet on a price-to-earnings ratio of about 19, which looks cheap for a company growing this fast. The real figure, using the profit analysts expect once the paper gains are stripped out, is about 25 times next year's earnings. That is a different investment.

The second thing is that Alphabet has changed how it funds itself. For years it was a machine that generated more cash than it could spend and handed the surplus back by buying its own shares — $45.7 billion of them last year. In the first half of this year it bought back nothing at all. Instead it sold new shares worth $30.5 billion (including $10 billion privately to Warren Buffett's Berkshire Hathaway), sold a special class of preferred shares paying 6.25% for another $19 billion, and borrowed $51.8 billion. It is spending the money on data centres: $80.6 billion in six months, against $39.6 billion in the same six months last year. It has also signed data-centre leases that have not yet started, committing it to $85.2 billion of future payments.

For the first time in memory, Alphabet is spending more cash than it brings in.

The reason it is doing this is genuinely good. Its cloud business grew 82% in the last quarter, and it has started selling the specialist computer chips it designed for itself to other companies. Contracted future cloud orders are, on the figures our tracked sources cite, above $500 billion. If that demand is real, the spending is the right call.

But the shares have already risen 97.6% in a year while the market rose 24%. The good news is in the price. Our estimate of fair value is $402 against a price of $375.35 — about 7% of room. That is not enough to call it a buy.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, plus an independent, public-math cross-check further down the page (“Check our number”) so you can judge it for yourself.

6b. The return bridge (why the multiple moves)

Expected return over 12-24 months decomposes as: clean EPS growth (+18-24% from the current run-rate toward the FY2027 consensus of $14.99) + multiple drift (we assume modest COMPRESSION, from 25.0x to ~23-24x forward) + shareholder yield (~0.23%, dividend only — there is no buyback).

Our base case assumes mild multiple compression, and we should say why rather than dress it as conservatism. Three reasons. First, the mechanical one: 25.0x FY2027E becomes 21.2x FY2028E at a constant price purely by rolldown, and rolldown is not re-rating — but the level the market pays should fall as the earnings base matures and as depreciation from the capital programme compresses incremental margins. Second, the capital structure has changed: a company issuing equity and 6.25% preferred to fund capex is a different security from one retiring 1.5% of its share count a year, and equity issuance at $351-355 is a management signal about value that argues against multiple expansion at $375. Third, free cash flow conversion has collapsed — 1.17% free cash flow yield is a genuinely poor number for a mega-cap, and multiples on companies with negative free cash flow are historically less stable than multiples on companies without.

Almost all of the expected return in the base case is therefore earnings growth, not re-rating. Base fair value of $402 is 26.8x FY2027E — a whisker of expansion, essentially a hold of the multiple — reached by earnings compounding into it. That produces roughly +7.1% plus a 0.23% yield. If the multiple compresses to 22x FY2027E the stock is worth $330, below spot. The thin margin of safety is the verdict.

The bull case at $510 requires 34x FY2027E, roughly the street's high target of $515. That leg needs the market to pay a growth multiple for a company whose consensus models no margin expansion for four years, and it is the fragile leg.

11. Verdict, kill-criteria and flip conditions

Hold.

This is one of the finest businesses in existence, growing faster than it was three years ago, and it is not cheap enough to buy today.

What is genuinely excellent. Revenue accelerating from +8.7% (FY2023) to +24.2% (Q2'26) at a $400B+ scale. Google Cloud growing 82% year over year with operating income up $6.0B. A brand-new revenue line from selling the company's own accelerator systems, first recognised this quarter. Gross margin at a series high of 61.6%. Traffic-acquisition costs falling on favourable mix. $242.5B of liquidity and 296x interest coverage per the 10-Q. Return on invested capital of 15.1%. Forty-one distinct tracked sources discussing it, 258 of them bullish.

What holds it at Hold. The stock has risen 97.6% in twelve months against a market up 24.3%, and the last three months delivered −1.1% — the easy money has been made. The honest multiple is 25.0x FY2027 consensus, not the 18.9x every screen shows, because trailing earnings are inflated by $99.0B of unrealised marks on private equity stakes. Free cash flow has turned negative. The buyback has been switched off entirely while $30.5B of common equity and $19.0B of 6.25% preferred were sold — at prices below today's. There is $85.2B of not-yet-commenced data-centre lease obligation in no leverage ratio anywhere. And our own knowledge base, the deepest lane we track, is 16% outright bearish with its highest-skill voice on both sides.

Base fair value of $402 is 7.1% above spot, against a 28% bear case. That is not a buy. It is a fine thing to own.

Pre-registered KILL criteria — what would move this to Avoid:

  • Google Search & other revenue growth falling below ~10% year over year for two consecutive quarters. It grew 13.4% in FY2025 and added $9.1B in Q2'26. This line is 55.7% of revenue and funds everything; its deceleration would simultaneously validate the agent-disintermediation bear case and remove the cash flow behind the capital programme.
  • Google Cloud operating margin declining as the 2026 depreciation flows through — the direct test of whether $80.6B of half-year capex was investment or expense.
  • Drawdown on the $40.0B at-the-market equity programme, which would signal that $49.5B of June issuance plus $51.8B of notes was insufficient.
  • Free cash flow remaining negative through FY2027 while capital expenditure continues to rise.
  • An adverse antitrust remedy affecting the distribution agreements underpinning search economics, beyond the $5.2B European fine already paid in July 2026 and the $2.1B Swedish accrual taken in Q2'26.
  • A reversal of the equity-securities marks. The $99.0B gain that made Q2'26 look extraordinary can run the other way; it is not cash and it is not the business, and a large negative mark would be equally meaningless — but it would hit reported book value and the screens would react.

Pre-registered FLIP TO BUY (upsize) criteria:

  • A price below ~$330 (22x FY2027E), which would restore a genuine margin of safety and roughly coincide with the 200-DMA of $326.76.
  • Resumption of share repurchases against the $69.5B authorisation — the clearest available statement that management considers the capital programme funded and the shares undervalued.
  • Google Cloud operating margin expanding for two consecutive quarters through the depreciation ramp.
  • Accelerator-system revenue disclosed as a separate, material line with third-party customers named, converting an internal cost advantage into an external franchise.
  • Free cash flow returning to positive while revenue growth stays above 18%.

Where GOOG fits in the Synthos Framework Portfolio. The mega-cap platform sleeve, as a held position rather than a new one — if already owned, a 2-3% weight is defensible on business quality alone; if not owned, there is no reason to initiate here. This is a name to accumulate on weakness toward the 200-DMA, not at 6% below a 52-week high after a near-double. Note the house standing rule that mega-cap incumbents are guilty until proven innocent on a forward basis: Alphabet largely proves its innocence on growth (+24.2% revenue, cloud +82%) and fails it on price (25.0x FY2027E after +97.6%).

On share classes. GOOG is the Class C capital stock, which carries no voting rights; Class A (GOOGL) carries one vote and Class B, held by founders, carries ten. The two listed classes are the same company and the same economics, and this dive is struck against GOOG's own close of $375.35. Where a Class A dive exists it is written independently; nothing here assumes or depends on it.

Single biggest risk: the return on the capital programme. $80.6B spent in six months against $39.6B in the comparable half, financed by $51.8B of debt and $49.5B of equity and preferred, with $85.2B of lease commitments not yet begun and free cash flow already negative. The depreciation from this arrives in the income statement before the revenue does, and consensus already models zero net margin expansion for four years as a result. If the demand behind it is as contracted as the bulls argue, this is the best capital allocation of the decade. If it is not, Alphabet has converted a fortress into a leveraged infrastructure business at exactly the wrong multiple. Our knowledge base contains both arguments, made recently, by credible people, and we do not claim to know which is right.

Most fragile assumption in the price: that advertising economics are structurally durable through a shift to agent-mediated purchasing. Google Search & other is 55.7% of revenue and pays for everything else. The most specific bear claim we track argues that "ad-based revenue loses value once agents, not humans, make purchase decisions." Nothing in the current numbers tests that claim — Search grew $9.1B in the June quarter — which is precisely what makes it fragile: it is unfalsifiable on a quarterly cadence, and so it is not priced.
