The Overview

Alphabet is the company behind the world's search engine, its largest video platform, the Android phone system, and a fast-growing business renting out computing power to other companies. It makes most of its money selling advertising next to search results and videos.

Three months ago it reported profits that looked impossible: it took in about $120 billion of revenue and reported $112 billion of profit. Almost every dollar of revenue became profit. That does not happen in real businesses, and it did not happen here.

The explanation is an accounting rule. Alphabet owns stakes in private companies it does not control — a rocket company and one other it does not name. When those companies are valued higher by their newest investors, Alphabet must write the increase into its own profit figure, even though it has not sold anything and no money has changed hands. That write-up was $99 billion in one quarter. Take it out and the quarter earned about $2.68 a share, roughly what analysts expected. The reported $9.11 is not earning power. It is a bookkeeping entry that can go into reverse just as fast.

This matters because every stock screener in the world now shows Alphabet as costing 19 times its earnings — which would be cheap. On the profits the business actually generates, it costs at least 34 times. That is not cheap.

The real business, meanwhile, is doing well. Sales grew 24% in the quarter, the fastest in five years. The cloud-computing arm grew 82%. Profit margins went up, not down. This is genuinely unusual for a company this size.

But there is a catch, and it is a big one. Alphabet is spending enormous sums building data centres — $80.6 billion in six months, more than double the $39.6 billion of a year earlier. In the June quarter that spending was larger than all the cash the business generated, so for the first time the company burned cash rather than producing it. It has stopped buying back its own shares entirely. And in June it did something it has never had to do: it sold $49.6 billion of new shares and preferred stock to investors, and set up a programme allowing it to sell $40 billion more. A company that used to hand cash back is now asking for it.

There is also a legal cloud. Courts have already ruled against the company in the United States on search and on advertising technology, and the government has asked a judge to break part of the business up. The filing itself says that outcome "could have a material adverse effect." Nobody, including the company, can put a number on it.

So: a better business than the crowd thinks, at a higher price than the screen says, financed in a way it never used to be, with an unresolved court case attached. Our estimate of fair value is $390 against a price of $377.65 — close enough to call it fully valued.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, plus an independent, public-math cross-check further down the page (“Check our number”) so you can judge it for yourself.

The tax note is the clincher. The 10-Q explains the jump in the effective rate as "primarily attributed to higher pre-tax earnings from unrealized gains on equity securities that are deferred tax liabilities at the statutory tax rate." A deferred tax liability is what you book when income has been recognised for accounting but not for tax — i.e. no cash has arrived and none has been paid out.

Deriving core earnings, stated method first. Core pre-tax income = operating income + (other income and expense − net equity-security gains). Tax at 17%, which is Alphabet's FY2025 effective rate adjusted for the statutory rate applied to the marks. Divide by the diluted share count in the same quarter:

  • Q2'26: $40.770B + ($97.983B − $99.0B) = $39.75B core pre-tax → $2.68 core EPS. Adding back the $2.1B legal charge disclosed in the same filing (a $1.5B damages award in general and administrative expenses plus $581M of interest in other income) gives $2.82 — against a $2.87 consensus. An in-line quarter.
  • Q1'26: $39.696B + ($37.716B − $36.9B) = $40.51B → $2.75 core EPS, against a $2.64 consensus. A modest beat.
  • FY2025: $129.166B + ($29.660B − $24.1B) = $134.73B → $9.14 core EPS, against reported diluted earnings of $10.81.

What this does to the multiple. Trailing reported diluted earnings, summing the four earn_cal actuals, are $19.91 — and $377.65 ÷ $19.91 = 18.97x, exactly the vendor's priceToEarningsDilutedRatioTTM. That figure is rejected. Trailing core earnings are at most $11.12 (Q3'25 $2.87 + Q4'25 $2.82, both upper bounds because the FY2025 equity gains are not split by quarter, plus core Q1'26 $2.75 and core Q2'26 $2.68), giving a trailing core multiple of at least 34.0x. A 34x trailing multiple is a completely different investment case from a 19x one, and every screen in the market shows the latter.

The corroborating evidence is in the cash flow statement. The vendor's incomeQualityTTM — operating cash flow divided by net income — has fallen to 0.76. For FY2025 the same ratio was 1.25 ($164.713B of operating cash flow against $132.170B of net income). Earnings that outrun cash by 32% at a company with Alphabet's collection profile are not earnings.

6b. The return bridge (why the multiple moves)

Expected return over 12-24 months decomposes as: core earnings growth (+31% from our derived FY2026 core of ~$11.48 to consensus FY2027 of $15.01) + multiple drift (COMPRESSION in our base) + shareholder yield (+0.23% dividend, ZERO buyback, and negative once the at-the-market programme is used).

Our base case explicitly assumes multiple compression, and we say so rather than hiding it in a rolldown. Today the market pays 25.2x on the next full year (FY2027). Our $390 base is 22.0x on FY2028 — i.e. one year forward from here the market pays roughly 22x on the then-next full year rather than 25x. That is a genuine ~13% de-rating, not the mechanical rolldown the addendum warns about.

Why compression rather than hold. Four reasons, all traceable. The shareholder yield that supported the multiple has gone to zero and is turning negative. The free-cash-flow yield is 1.17% and structurally so for at least two more years. The balance sheet has moved from a pure equity story to one with $98.2B of debt, $19.0B of preferred ranking ahead of the common, $51.4B of third-party credit backstops and $811.0B of purchase commitments. And an unresolved structural-remedy proceeding sits on the largest profit pool. Against that, revenue is accelerating, which argues for expansion — and that is precisely why the compression assumed is mild rather than severe.

If we are wrong and the multiple simply holds at 25.2x on the next full year, the price in a year is ~$446 (+18%) — that is effectively the path to our bull case. If the market de-rates a re-accelerating platform to 15x, the price is $266 — that is the bear. Almost none of the expected return in the base case comes from re-rating; almost all of it comes from earnings growth, and the multiple takes most of it back. That is the honest arithmetic of a Hold.

11. Verdict, kill-criteria and flip conditions

Hold.

What we are saying is not that the business is bad. It is that the business is good, the price already knows, and the way the growth is being financed has changed in a manner the multiple has not yet acknowledged.

The bull side, in facts from the filings: revenue +24.2% year over year, the fastest in five years and accelerating from 8.7% in FY2023; cloud +82% at a ~$99B run-rate; 54% incremental operating margins in both reporting segments; gross margin up 210 basis points and operating margin up 160 basis points year over year; a new hardware revenue line begun in the June quarter; roughly $140.1B of net cash and $242.5B of liquid resources; and a search franchise that independent voices in the knowledge base put at ~90% share.

The bear side, from the same filings: trailing earnings are 87% non-cash marks and the honest trailing multiple is ≥34x, not the 18.97x every screen shows; free cash flow went negative in the June quarter with capital expenditure at 37.5% of revenue and 5.25x depreciation; buybacks are zero with $69.5B of authorisation unused; $49.6B of equity issued and a $40.0B at-the-market programme live; $51.8B of notes issued in six months taking long-term debt to $98.2B; $51.4B of third-party credit backstops plus $24.1B agreed; $811.0B of purchase commitments; $17.4B of accrued legal fines; and a structural-remedy judgment awaited that the company itself says "could have a material adverse effect" and refuses to size.

Base fair value $390, 3.3% above spot, 8% below the street's $423.44, with a bear at $270 that is larger than the bull at $475. On a stock that has risen 99.7% in twelve months, that is a Hold.

Pre-registered KILL criteria — what would move this to Avoid:

  • A structural remedy in the US advertising-technology case. Judgment is awaited after November 2025 closing arguments. A forced divestiture of the publisher-side stack would hit the highest-margin part of the advertising engine and re-rate the whole company.
  • A material downward revision of the private-stake marks. The $99.0B that produced the June quarter's 94% net margin is unrealised and symmetric. A 30% write-down is roughly $30B pre-tax and could put a quarter into loss with the operating business unchanged.
  • A third consecutive quarter of negative free cash flow accompanied by a further capital-expenditure increase and no stated peak. The filings currently say only "significantly increase, relative to 2025" ($91.4B) with no number.
  • Actual sales under the $40.0B at-the-market programme while repurchases remain at zero — the confirmation that the shareholder yield is not merely absent but negative.
  • Cloud incremental operating margin falling below 35%, which would mean the scaling economics that justify the capital intensity are not there.
  • Credit-derivative backstop notionals continuing to compound at the current rate ($16.9B → $28.4B → $43.8B across three quarters). At that pace the off-balance-sheet exposure to third-party builders passes $70B by year end, and Alphabet becomes a credit provider to the buildout as well as a buyer within it.

Pre-registered FLIP TO BUY (upsize):

  • A price below ~$310 — roughly 20.7x FY2027 consensus, at which the base case offers +26% and the asymmetry inverts. That is a 18% drawdown from here and entirely achievable in a capex-led sell-off.
  • A capital-expenditure figure that flattens year over year, or free cash flow returning decisively positive — either restores the free-cash-flow yield and with it the multiple's support.
  • Resumption of repurchases at scale against the $69.5B authorisation, which would signal management believes the equity is cheap at a moment it has just been selling it.
  • A behavioural rather than structural outcome in the advertising-technology remedy, removing the largest unquantifiable on the name.
  • Two consecutive quarters of cloud revenue growth above 60% with incremental operating margins above 50%, which would make the FY2028-FY2030 consensus ladder look conservative rather than heroic.

Where GOOGL fits in the Synthos Framework Portfolio. The mega-cap platform / AI infrastructure sleeve, as a 2-3% position if already held, with no addition at this price. The justification for holding rather than exiting: this is one of very few $4T-plus businesses whose revenue growth is accelerating rather than decaying, and exiting a compounding franchise on a valuation call has a poor historical record. The justification for not adding: the base case offers 3.3%, the bear case is larger than the bull, the free-cash-flow yield is 1.17%, and the single largest risk cannot be sized by anyone including the company. New money waits for either a lower price (~$310) or a resolved remedy.

Single biggest risk: the US advertising-technology structural remedy. In April 2025 the Eastern District of Virginia found that the publisher-side tools "unfairly excluded rivals." A remedies proceeding ran in September 2025, closing arguments were held in November 2025, and judgment has not arrived. The 10-Q's language is unusually direct for a filing: "The DOJ's remedy proposal includes structural remedies that could have a material adverse effect on our business," and "Given the nature of these matters, we cannot estimate a possible loss." The market is carrying this at zero. A separate state case in Texas follows, and private damages actions across Europe follow determined liability as a matter of course — the $2.1B Stockholm award booked in the June quarter is the first instalment of that pattern, not the last. This is why the risk score is 6 and not 4.

Most fragile assumption in the price: that FY2027 earnings of $15.01 arrive with the depreciation from roughly $200B of 2025-2026 capital expenditure fully loaded, without breaking the ~32.5% operating margin that consensus assumes. First-half depreciation grew 43% while first-half capital expenditure grew 104%; capital expenditure is running at 5.25x depreciation. The entire gap between those growth rates is a future charge against the income statement that has not yet appeared, and every valuation anchor in this dive — ours and the street's — assumes it lands gently.
