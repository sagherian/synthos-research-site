The Overview

When an airline needs a replacement part for a jet engine, it can buy it from the engine manufacturer — General Electric, Pratt and Whitney, Rolls-Royce — or it can buy an approved copy. HEICO makes the approved copies. The regulator certifies them, they work, and they cost roughly 30 to 40 percent less. That is the whole business, and it has made the Mendelson family, who have run it since 1990, one of the great records in American industry: sales up about 15 percent a year, profits up 18 percent, for three decades.

Two things make it unusually safe. First, the quality record — an expert we track noted the company has sold more than 80 million parts with zero safety directives or in-flight shutdowns, which is a record no engine maker can match. Second, and this is the clever bit: HEICO deliberately limits how much of the market it takes. It caps itself around 30 percent of any given part and stays away from the parts that carry the highest safety consequences. Why hold back? Because if it took the whole market, the engine makers would slash prices and destroy the profit pool for everyone. By staying small enough to be tolerated, HEICO keeps its 30-40 percent discount profitable forever. That is a rare kind of discipline.

And business right now is excellent. The most recent quarter in our data — three months to April 2026 — showed revenue up 25 percent and profits up 48 percent, beating what analysts expected by nearly a quarter. Better still, of every extra dollar of sales, about 35 cents dropped through to operating profit, well above the company's usual 23 cents. That is the signature of a business getting genuinely more profitable, not just bigger.

So why are we saying don't buy it? Because of the price. At $360 the stock costs about 52 times what analysts think it will earn in the year to October 2027 — for comparison, the average large US company trades around 20 to 25 times. You are paying a very full price for the quality, and you are paying it at the worst possible moment: the stock is within 1.3 percent of its record high, up 34 percent in three months, and hasn't had so much as a 2 percent dip. Analysts' average target is $376, only 4 percent higher — and their lowest target, $350, is below today's price. Our own estimate of fair value, $351, is also below today's price.

The business deserves a premium. It does not deserve this premium at this moment. Wait for a real pullback.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, plus an independent, public-math cross-check further down the page (“Check our number”) so you can judge it for yourself.

2. The moat — and the most interesting thing in this dive

The knowledge base contains a genuinely non-obvious explanation of why HEICO's economics persist, and it is worth stating in full because it is also the reason the Exponential score is only 4.

Business Breakdowns, 2024-03-31, conviction 75: "HEICO's prisoner's-dilemma strategy caps PMA share at ~30% and avoids life-limited parts, so OEMs stay rational and don't cut price."

And its companion, same source and date, conviction 75: "HEICO undercuts OEMs 30-40%, widening the discount over time by not raising price, which deepens cost savings and customer lock-in."

Put those together and you have the whole model. HEICO's parts are cheaper. It does not raise price, so the discount widens every year, which deepens customer dependence. And it refuses to take more than about 30% of any part's market, because at higher penetration the OEM's rational response flips from "tolerate it" to "cut price and kill it." HEICO is voluntarily leaving revenue on the table to preserve the price umbrella it lives under.

That is an extraordinarily durable structure — and it is also a self-imposed ceiling. Most moats are about taking more share. This one is about taking exactly enough.

The other moat components, from the same source block:

  • "Over 80M parts sold with zero service bulletins, airworthiness directives, or in-flight shutdowns — a quality record no OEM can match." (conviction 70, fact-tagged)
  • "HEICO outperforms in rough industry environments as airlines cut costs and face supply-chain crunches, driving PMA adoption on both price and availability." (conviction 70) — the counter-cyclicality argument.
  • "HEICO's electronics group (ETG) is like analog semis but less cyclical/less growth — diversified mission-critical components at 30%+ defense margins, non-cyclical." (conviction 65)

Moat: wide, durable and deliberately self-limiting.

Read it straight. In a normal year HEICO redeploys roughly three-quarters of its free cash flow into acquisitions — $629.83M against $861.38M in FY2025. Dividends took just $31.97M and buybacks $22.39M. This is a compounding machine that reinvests almost everything, and the capital-allocation record is the reason the knowledge base rates management at conviction 85.

The honest triangulation. FY2025 revenue grew $627M ($3.858B to $4.485B) while the company spent $629.83M on acquisitions. That coincidence does not mean growth was 100% acquired — acquisitions close mid-year and contribute partial revenue, and the multiple paid is unknown. But it does mean a meaningful share of headline growth is bought, and the file cannot tell you how much. The offsetting evidence — the 35.5% incremental operating margin in Q2 FY2026 — is what argues the organic component is substantial. That is as far as the data honestly goes.

The balance-sheet consequence, which is the real cost of the model:

  • Goodwill $3.662B plus intangibles $1.471B = $5.133B, or 60.4% of the $8.500B asset base (km_ttm confirms 61.6% TTM).
  • Tangible book value per share: NEGATIVE $3.73. Tangible asset value: negative $520.97M.
  • Return on tangible assets is 21.5% versus return on assets of 8.2% — the gap is entirely the acquired intangibles.

None of that is a problem while the deals work. It becomes a problem the moment a large acquisition is impaired, and it is why P/B of 10.5x is not a meaningful valuation anchor for this name.

Dual-class caveat, and it affects every multiple above. The reported market capitalisation of $50.216B at $360.44 implies 139.32M shares, against FY2025 weighted diluted shares of 140.77M. If Class A trades at a discount to Common, as dual-class B-share structures typically do, then applying the Common price to the full share count overstates the true blended market value, and every market-cap-derived multiple in this dive (EV/EBITDA, P/E, EV/Sales) is modestly overstated as a result. We cannot quantify the effect from this file and are not going to guess at it. Flag it, size the conclusion accordingly, and check the Class A price before acting on any multiple here.

  • Bull ~$449 — 50x FY2029E consensus EPS ($8.99). Requires the multiple to hold near today's level and the out-years to be beaten, as every quarter in this file has been. Above the Street high of $410. +24.6%.

6a. What today's price assumes (the inversion)

At $360.44 (52.3x FY2027E, 46.2x FY2028E consensus), today's price assumes roughly:

  • Revenue compounds to at least the consensus $6.309B by FY2028 — a 12.0% CAGR from FY2025's $4.485B — and HEICO keeps buying it, since consensus itself has revenue growth halving from 18.1% to 8.1% over the window. (Falsifiable: FY2026 and FY2027 revenue prints; and the acquisition line, which was $629.83M in FY2025.) Consensus-derived, 15 and 12 analysts.
  • EPS reaches ~$7.80 by FY2028E and ~$8.99 by FY2029E — implying net income roughly $1.10B and $1.27B against FY2025's $690.38M, i.e. net income up 84% in four years. (Falsifiable: the FY2027 and FY2028 annual prints.) Consensus-derived, 12 and 5 analysts — note FY2029 rests on only 5.
  • Operating margin holds at or above the ~24-25% the Q2 FY2026 run-rate implies, not the 22.7% FY2025 average — consensus FY2028E EBIT of $1.510B on $6.309B of revenue is a 23.9% EBIT margin, above every full year in the file. (Falsifiable: quarterly operating margin, and specifically the incremental margin. Q2 FY2026 printed 25.5% and 35.5% respectively.)
  • The market keeps paying roughly 45-50x forward earnings for a business decelerating toward 8% revenue growth — i.e. essentially no de-rating for four consecutive years. (Falsifiable in the price itself. This is the assumption doing the most work.)
  • The acquisition pipeline stays available at accretive prices. (Falsifiable: the cash-flow acquisitions line and whether goodwill, already 43.1% of assets, keeps growing without impairment.)

The single most fragile assumption is the last multiple bullet. Everything about the operating business supports the estimates; nothing supports a permanent 45-50x. A move to 35x FY2027E costs 33% and requires no operational disappointment whatsoever.

6b. The return bridge (why the multiple moves)

expected return ≈ EPS growth + multiple drift + shareholder yield

Over a two-year horizon to FY2028E, consensus EPS grows from $6.10 to $7.80 — 13.1% a year. The dividend yield is 0.069% and net buybacks are trivial ($22.39M against a $50B cap), so shareholder yield is effectively zero. That leaves multiple drift as the entire swing factor.

Our base case explicitly assumes multiple COMPRESSION, from today's 52.3x FY2027E to 45x FY2028E, and we want to be precise about why. Two reasons, and neither is a criticism of the company:

1. Growth maturation. Consensus has revenue growth halving from 18.1% (FY2026E) to 8.1% (FY2028E) and 7.9% (FY2029E). Multiples follow growth rates down. At 8% revenue growth and ~13% EPS growth, 52x is a PEG near 4.

2. The rolldown is not the re-rating. 59.1x FY2026E becoming 46.2x FY2028E at an unchanged $360.44 is mechanical — it is the estimate advancing, not the market repaying you. Our base compresses the multiple the market pays at each date, from ~52x to ~45x. That is the actual re-rating assumption and it is deliberate.

So the bridge to our $351 base is: +13.1% a year of EPS growth, minus roughly 14% of cumulative multiple compression, plus ~0% yield — netting to approximately zero over two years. If instead the multiple simply holds near 50x on FY2029E, you get the $449 bull. The thesis for buying here needs multiple expansion or persistence, and that is the fragile leg. Not the earnings.

6c. Variant perception (where we differ, what would surprise)

  • We think consensus is too LOW on earnings and too GENEROUS on the multiple, and the second effect dominates. HEICO beat in all five reported quarters in the file, by an average that includes a 24.8% beat in Q2 FY2026, and the incremental operating margin of 35.5% says the beats are margin-driven, not one-off. So we would not be surprised to see FY2027E and FY2028E revised up. We still do not want to own it here, because a 45-50x multiple has more room to fall than the estimates have to rise. This is the crux of the variant perception: right on the business, unwilling to pay for it.
  • Where we differ from the bulls specifically. Consensus PT of $376 implies 54.6x FY2027E — a multiple above today's. That is the Street underwriting no de-rating at all through the deceleration. We think that is the least defensible part of the consensus view, and notably the Street's own low target of $350 (50.8x FY2027E) agrees enough to sit below spot.
  • Positive surprise that would force a repricing higher. The 2026-08-24 Q3 print beating the $1.50 consensus with incremental operating margin above 30% again, plus an FY2027 revenue guide above the $5.834B consensus. Two consecutive quarters of 30%-plus incremental margin would mean the FY2028E 23.9% EBIT margin is too low and the whole estimate stack moves up — at which point 52x FY2027E is really ~45x on revised numbers and our Watch is wrong.
  • Negative surprise that would force a repricing lower. Either one, at 52x with the stock 1.3% from its high and no drawdown cushion, opens the $241-$300 zone quickly. The watchable number is the incremental operating margin, reported quarterly.

9. Verdict, kill-criteria and the flip conditions

Watch. Hold for existing owners; no new money at 52.3x FY2027E.

We have no argument with HEICO the business. It is a wide-moat, family-controlled, self-funding compounder with 39.8% gross margins, 125% free-cash-flow conversion, capex at 1.6% of revenue, a perfect five-for-five beat record in this dataset, a 35.5% incremental operating margin in the most recent quarter, and a knowledge-base lane that has underwritten it at conviction 65-85 since 2024. If you own it, keep owning it — the We Study Billionaires claim about the headline P/E looking unattractive is the right frame for a holder.

We have a serious argument with the entry. At $360.44 you are paying 52.3x FY2027E and 46.2x FY2028E consensus EPS, 38.4x TTM EV/EBITDA and 54.2x trailing free cash flow, for a company whose own consensus models revenue growth halving from 18.1% to 8.1% over that window. The stock is 1.3% below its all-time high after a 34.3% three-month run, with a maximum drawdown of -1.33% — there is no dip. The consensus PT of $376 offers 4.3%, the Street's low target of $350 is below the current price, and our base fair value of $351 is 2.6% below spot. Every honest anchor available lands at or under where the stock already trades.

Pre-registered kill / do-not-add criteria:

  • Incremental operating margin reverts toward the 22.7% FY2025 average for two consecutive quarters — the Q2 FY2026 acceleration was a one-off and the estimate stack is not going up.
  • A revenue print in line or below consensus ($1.348B for Q3 FY2026 on 2026-08-24) at this multiple.
  • A large acquisition at a full multiple that pushes goodwill materially above the current 43.1% of assets without a clear margin case — the model depends on buying well, and the tangible book is already negative $3.73 per share.
  • Any goodwill impairment. With $5.133B of goodwill and intangibles against $4.305B of equity, an impairment is a balance-sheet event, not a footnote.
  • Loss of the 200-DMA ($318.17) on company-specific news rather than market beta — that would be the market pricing the deceleration, and the $241-$300 zone opens.

Pre-registered flip to Stage-In (start building a position):

  • A 20%-plus drawdown toward the 200-DMA ($318.17) or below on no fundamental deterioration — that takes the FY2027E multiple to roughly 46x and the FY2028E to 41x, which is a price we would engage with for this franchise.
  • OR two consecutive quarters of 30%-plus incremental operating margin plus an FY2027 revenue guide above the $5.834B consensus — that would mean consensus is materially too low, the effective multiple is lower than it looks, and paying up is justified on revised numbers.
