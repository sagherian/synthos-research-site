The Overview

Intercontinental Exchange owns marketplaces. Thirteen of them are regulated exchanges — including the New York Stock Exchange — and six are clearing houses, which stand between buyers and sellers and guarantee that trades settle. It also sells bond pricing data and, since buying a company called Black Knight for $10.2 billion in 2023, runs much of the software that American mortgage lenders use.

These are excellent businesses. Fifty-two cents of every dollar of revenue becomes cash profit before interest, tax and depreciation. The company produces about $4.85 billion of surplus cash a year from fewer than 13,000 employees.

The shares have nonetheless fallen 21% from their high and have trailed the wider market by 43 percentage points over the past year — the worst record of any company in this batch.

And six days ago, the company agreed to buy MarketAxess, a bond-trading platform, paying $167 per share in cash. To be sure it can fund it, Bank of America has committed a $6.25 billion short-term loan facility. That is the biggest financial commitment ICE has made in three years.

Here is the problem for this document: we cannot evaluate it. The regulatory filings we hold announce the deal and describe the financing, but they do not contain the total price, the expected savings, or any of MarketAxess's own financial figures. The company's press release is not in our copies. So the largest recent decision management has made is, from where we sit, an unknown.

What we can say is what it costs. ICE already owes about $20 billion, roughly 2.8 times its annual cash profit. Adding $6.25 billion takes that toward 3.6 times. And ICE has been buying back about $2.4 billion of its own shares a year, which is roughly 3% of the return an investor gets — money that may now go to the acquisition instead.

At $149.20 the shares cost 17 times what analysts expect the company to earn in 2027, which is cheap for this kind of business. Thirty-three of the thirty-six analysts covering it are positive, and the most pessimistic target on the street, $163, is 9% above today's price.

Our estimate of fair value is $165. Bad case $116; good case $222. That is 2.19 dollars of possible gain per dollar of loss, which does pass our usual test. We are still saying Watch rather than Buy, because a company that has just committed $6.25 billion to something we cannot assess is not a company we will recommend on arithmetic alone.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, plus an independent, public-math cross-check further down the page (“Check our number”) so you can judge it for yourself.

1. The MarketAxess acquisition, and everything the file does not say about it

From the 8-K filed 2026-07-30, and this is the whole of what the archive contains:

> "On July 29, 2026, Intercontinental Exchange, Inc.... entered into an Agreement and Plan of Merger... with MarketAxess Holdings Inc.... and Igloo Merger Sub II, Inc.... Merger Sub will be merged with and into MarketAxess, with MarketAxess surviving as a wholly owned subsidiary of ICE."

> "...each share of common stock, par value $0.003 per share, of MarketAxess... will be converted into the right to receive $167.00 in cash per share, without interest."

> "ICE expects to finance the payment of the aggregate Merger Consideration... through a combination of available cash and incremental debt financing. However, there can be no assurance that such incremental debt financing will be completed... On July 29, 2026, ICE, Bank of America, N.A. and BofA Securities, Inc. entered into a financing commitment letter... for a 364-day senior unsecured bridge facility in an aggregate principal amount not to exceed $6.25 billion... The receipt or availability of any financing by ICE, including the Bridge Facility, is not a condition to ICE's obligation to consummate the Merger."

What that establishes. An all-cash acquisition at $167.00 per share, with no financing condition — ICE is obliged to close whether or not the debt markets cooperate — backstopped by a bridge facility of up to $6.25 billion. MarketAxess options and time-based restricted stock units convert into ICE equivalents. The 10-Q's XBRL tagging carries a ScenarioForecast period of 2027-04-01 to 2027-06-30 for MarketAxess Holdings, which implies management expects to close around the second quarter of 2027 — roughly three quarters away.

What it does not establish, and this is the reason for the verdict. The archive contains no aggregate purchase price, no expected synergies, no accretion or dilution estimate, no pro-forma leverage guidance, and no MarketAxess financial statements. The earnings-release exhibits for this name are not in the extraction. The 8-K is checked as "Soliciting material pursuant to Rule 14a-12", which means a proxy process is under way that this archive does not reach.

What can be inferred, labelled as inference. A $6.25 billion bridge sized to backstop the full consideration implies an aggregate price in that neighbourhood — the facility exists "to fund, in part, the acquisition and to pay related fees, commissions and expenses" if permanent financing is unavailable. On ICE's trailing EBITDA of approximately $6.98 billion, adding $6.25 billion of debt to $20,279 million existing takes gross leverage from roughly 2.9x to roughly 3.8x, and net leverage from 2.79x toward roughly 3.6x, before any MarketAxess EBITDA. That is our arithmetic on the vendor's balance sheet and the filing's facility size; it is not the company's guidance, and the company has published none this file can reach.

Two things a reader should hold alongside it. Jeffrey C. Sprecher has built this company by serial acquisition since 2000 — NYSE, Interactive Data, Ellie Mae, Black Knight — and that record argues for more trust in the decision than a stranger's would deserve. And the balance sheet shows what it has cost: $30,646 million of goodwill and $15,353 million of intangibles against $28,915 million of equity, so tangible equity is negative by approximately $17 billion.

The other 8-K in the record is routine: an Item 5.07 annual-meeting report (2026-05-19, meeting held 2026-05-15) recording eleven directors elected against 566,430,761 outstanding voting shares at the March record date.
