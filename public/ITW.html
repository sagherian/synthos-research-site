The Overview

Illinois Tool Works makes industrial products — fasteners for cars, commercial kitchen equipment, welding gear, test instruments, adhesives, construction fixings. Seven separate businesses, none of them more than a fifth of the company.

It is extremely well run. Out of every dollar of sales, about 26.5 cents is operating profit, which is unusually high for this kind of manufacturing. It gets 24.7 cents of profit per year for every dollar of capital invested, which is excellent. It does all this with only 43,000 employees.

There is one problem and it is simple. In 2022 it sold $15.93 billion of products. In 2025 it sold $16.04 billion. That is 0.7% growth in three years. Profits per share have risen anyway, because the company has kept squeezing more margin out of the same revenue and has bought back exactly $1.5 billion of its own shares every year. Both of those work — until they don't.

The shares cost $295.06 and have risen 17% in three months, to within 1.5% of their highest price of the year. At that price you are paying 24 times what analysts expect the company to earn in 2027, for a business those same analysts think will grow sales 3.7% a year.

Here is the unusual part. Wall Street analysts normally publish price targets above the current price. On this stock, the average target is $280 — five percent BELOW where the shares trade. The single most optimistic target on the street is $301, two percent above. And nine of the 28 analysts covering the company have a "sell" rating, against six "buys". That is the most negative professional view of any company in this research batch, and the shares are near a record high anyway.

Our own estimate of fair value is $270, which is about 8.5% below the price. In a bad case the shares fall to around $214; in a good case they reach around $336. That is roughly fifty cents of possible gain per dollar of possible loss — the worst ratio of anything we looked at this week. So: Hold. A good company, at a price that has run ahead of it.

One honesty note. For most companies we check the vendor's numbers against the actual documents filed with regulators. For this one we could not. The copies we hold contain only the written discussion, not the financial tables — and the June-quarter filing is not in the archive at all. So every number in this document that came from the data vendor is labelled unverified. That is a real limit and it is why our confidence ratings are moderate rather than high.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, plus an independent, public-math cross-check further down the page (“Check our number”) so you can judge it for yourself.

9. Verdict, kill-criteria and flip conditions

Hold.

The business deserves the first paragraph. Illinois Tool Works produces $16 billion of revenue with 43,000 employees — $373,000 per employee — across seven segments none of which exceeds 20.5% of revenue, at a 26.5% trailing operating margin and a 24.7% return on invested capital, with 14.45x interest coverage and 1.91x net leverage. That is a superb operating system and we are not disputing any part of it.

The price is the entire objection, and for once we are not alone in it.

First, the arithmetic. At $295.06 the shares are 25.9x the FY2026 consensus of $11.41 and 24.0x FY2027's $12.29, on forecast revenue growth of 4.8% and 3.7% — a PEG of roughly 3.1, the highest in this batch. Revenue was $15,932 million in FY2022 and $16,044 million in FY2025.
