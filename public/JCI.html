The Overview

Johnson Controls makes the systems that keep large buildings working: air conditioning and chillers, fire alarms and sprinklers, security, and the software that runs them. Its customers are offices, hospitals, universities, factories and — increasingly — data centres.

For years it was a sprawling, underperforming company. In 2025 it hired a new chief executive from outside. Since then it has sold its home air-conditioning business to Bosch, agreed to sell its British home-security business, reorganised itself from four divisions into three, and started beating analysts' profit forecasts — five quarters in a row, and by a bigger margin each time: 4%, 5%, 5.8%, 6.3% and, most recently, 9.2%.

The board has also put in place an unusual pay scheme. Executives get share options that are worth nothing at all unless the company hits both a large sales target and a market-value target described as "a substantial increase from the Company's current market capitalization" — over five years, forfeited entirely if they leave for almost any reason. Management is being paid to roughly transform the company, or paid nothing.

The market likes it. The shares rose 4.93% today and closed at $153.62 — which is the highest price they have reached in a year. Over twelve months they are up 44%, roughly double the market. Remarkably, the vendor data shows the shares have not had a single meaningful setback all year.

That is the problem. Analysts on average think the shares are worth $157.33. The median analyst thinks $154.00. They cost $153.62. Our own estimate is $160. Nobody — including us — thinks there is much left.

Three things to understand before deciding.

The huge buyback was not paid for by the business. Last year Johnson Controls bought back $5.99 billion of its own stock, about 6% of the company. But it only generated $965 million of spare cash. The difference came from selling the home air-conditioning business to Bosch. That money is now spent, and net debt is $10.8 billion.

There is no asset backing. Of the company's $12.9 billion of book value, $20.2 billion is goodwill and intangibles — accounting entries from past acquisitions. Strip them out and the tangible book value is negative $7.3 billion.

And the company keeps changing shape, which makes its own history hard to read. Four divisions became three in April. One business was sold, another is being sold. The supplied financial data still shows the old structure.

A real improvement, honestly earned, at a price that has already paid for it. That is a Hold. Our number is $125.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, plus an independent, public-math cross-check further down the page (“Check our number”) so you can judge it for yourself.

1. The company, and it has been reshaped twice

Johnson Controls International plc is an Irish public limited company headquartered in Cork, NYSE-listed, with approximately 87,000 employees and Joakim Weidemanis, appointed from outside the company in 2025, as chief executive. The 10-Q's own description of what it is:

> "Johnson Controls International plc, headquartered in Cork, Ireland, is a global leader in thermal management, mission-critical building systems, energy efficiency, and decarbonization. The Company helps customers create, maintain, and optimize indoor operating environments that use energy more productively, reduce carbon emissions, and support the precise, reliable and resilient operating conditions in rapidly expanding industries such as data centers, healthcare, pharmaceuticals, advanced manufacturing, and higher education."

Note the market it names first. That single sentence is the entire disclosed basis for the structural growth case, and it is qualitative — no data-centre revenue figure appears anywhere in this archive.

The structure has changed twice in two years, and the vendor payload has not kept up:

> "On April 1, 2025, the Company realigned into three reportable segments (Americas, EMEA and APAC) from four reportable segments (Global Products, Building Solutions North America, Building Solutions EMEA/LA and Building Solutions APAC). Historical information has been recast to present the comparative periods on a consistent basis."

The payload's seg_prod uses NEITHER structure correctly. For fiscal 2024 it reports the old four segments — Building Solutions North America $11,348M, Global Products $5,071M, Building Solutions EMEA/LA $4,296M, Building Solutions APAC $2,237M, summing to $22,952M, exactly reported revenue. For fiscal 2025 it reports three lines — Building Solutions North America $15,831M, Building Solutions EMEA/LA $4,968M, Building Solutions Asia Pacific $2,797M — summing to $23,596M, exactly reported revenue. Both years reconcile to the dollar, and neither uses the Americas/EMEA/APAC names the company now reports. The fiscal-2025 line labelled "Building Solutions North America" at $15,831M is 39% larger than the fiscal-2024 line of the same name, because Global Products was absorbed into it — so the two years cannot be compared. No segment growth figure in this dive comes from the payload.

The divestitures

Completed: residential and light commercial HVAC, sold to the Bosch Group, July 2025.

> "In 2024, the Company determined that the R&LC HVAC business, which was previously reported in the Global Products segment, met the criteria to be classified as discontinued operations as it represented a strategic shift in the Company's operations and resulted in the exit of substantially all of its residential and light commercial HVAC businesses. In July 2025, the Company completed the sale of its R&LC HVAC business."

That transaction is the explanation for four otherwise puzzling figures in the payload: fiscal-2025 inc_a net income of $3,291M against cf_a net income of $1,721M (the gain on sale); noncontrolling interests collapsing from $1,263M to $27M (the joint-venture interest went with it); total assets falling from $42,695M to $37,939M; and — most importantly — the $5,991M share repurchase in a year of $965M free cash flow.

Pending: ADT United Kingdom Residential Security.

> "During the third quarter of fiscal 2026, the Company signed a definitive agreement to sell its ADT United Kingdom Residential Security business and determined that it met the criteria to be classified as held for sale. The transaction is expected to close in fiscal 2027. The business did not meet the criteria to be classified as a discontinued operation... As of June 30, 2026, $229 million of assets and $39 million of liabilities associated with the business were separately presented as held for sale."

Read the two together and the direction is clear: out of residential, out of consumer-adjacent security, into commercial and institutional building systems. That is a portfolio being simplified toward the higher-margin, specification-driven end of the industry, and it is the substance of the turnaround.

The compensation scheme, which is the strongest governance signal in this batch

From the 8-K filed 2026-05-13:

> "On May 11, 2026, the Compensation and Talent Development Committee... approved the adoption of a long-term Value Growth Incentive Program... The Options vest only to the extent the Company achieves ambitious performance objectives at levels beyond what is currently embedded in the Company's incentive plans over a five-year performance period consisting of fiscal year 2026 through fiscal year 2030..."

> "Fifty percent (50%) of the Options will be contingent on achieving a specified level of net sales... representing significant growth relative to the Company's fiscal year 2025 net sales... Achievement of the Sales Goal is contingent on the Company also achieving a specified level of market capitalization... Fifty percent (50%) of the Options will be contingent on achieving a specified level of market capitalization... that is tied to achievement of aggressive growth and increased valuation goals."

> "...will be granted... with an exercise price equal to the price of the Company's ordinary shares on the date of grant and will therefore have value only to the extent the Company's share price increases after the grant date... The Options will be forfeited upon any other termination, including retirement or an involuntary termination for poor performance, prior to the end of the Performance Period."

This is unusually direct. Both halves of the award are conditioned on market capitalisation "representing a substantial increase from the Company's current market capitalization"; the options are struck at the money so they are worthless without appreciation; and they are forfeited on retirement. Management is paid to roughly transform this company or to receive nothing.

Two honest qualifications. The specific thresholds are not disclosed in the extracted text, so "significant" and "substantial" cannot be quantified. And a grant struck at the money on 2026-05-11, when the shares were near $145, is now materially in the money after a 44% year — an option package whose value has already begun accruing, and a dilution item that will show up in the share count.
