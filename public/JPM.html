The Overview

JPMorgan is the largest bank in America and one of the best-run large companies anywhere. It takes deposits from 80 million-odd customers and businesses, lends the money out, runs the world's biggest trading and investment-banking operation, and manages money for wealthy families and institutions. It made $57 billion last year.

Three weeks ago it reported a quarter that looked spectacular: it earned $7.59 a share when analysts expected $5.59. That is a 36% beat, which almost never happens at a company this size and this well covered. The stock has since run to a twelve-month high.

The beat was not what it looked like. JPMorgan has owned a block of restricted Visa shares for years, carried on its books at essentially zero. In April, Visa offered to swap some of those restricted shares for freely tradable ones. JPMorgan said, in a filing on 1 May, that it had accepted, and that when the swap completed it would have to record the new shares at their real value — producing a large one-off profit, probably in the second quarter. That is exactly what happened. It had done the same thing in 2024, for $7.9 billion.

You can see it in the numbers without needing the Firm to spell it out. Interest income — the actual banking business — barely moved between the first and second quarter. The money set aside for bad loans barely moved. Taxes were normal. The only thing that jumped was fee-and-other income, by about $7.4 billion in three months. Take that out and the quarter earned about $5.50 to $5.60 — right at the estimate. So the underlying business was fine. It was not 36% better than expected.

Now the price. For a bank, the right measure is not enterprise value or cash flow — those are industrial-company concepts that break completely on a balance sheet where customer deposits are the raw material. The right measure is what you pay for a dollar of tangible net worth, compared with how much profit the bank earns on that net worth. JPMorgan's tangible net worth was $108.87 a share at the end of March, and it earns about 20 cents of profit on every dollar of it — a very high rate.

Here is the problem. There is a standard piece of arithmetic that says how much a bank earning 20 cents on the dollar should be worth: about three times its tangible net worth. JPMorgan trades at about 3.2 times. The market has already given the company full credit for being excellent. That is not a mistake by the market, and it is not an opportunity. It just means there is nothing here to buy cheaply.

Our estimate of fair value is $372 against a price of $357.52. The analysts covering it average $367.83. When our number and the crowd's number agree that closely, honesty requires saying we have no edge on this one.

  • Downside Risk 5/10. The bank is a fortress; the price is not. A return to a 17% profit rate takes fair value to $276.
  • Growth Quality 5/10. Excellent quality, ordinary rate. The reported acceleration is largely the Visa item.
  • Exponential Potential 2/10. A $958 billion bank capped by capital rules. The interesting optionality here is arguably negative.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, plus an independent, public-math cross-check further down the page (“Check our number”) so you can judge it for yourself.

2. The 36% beat — what it actually was

This is the analytical core of the dive, and it resolves cleanly.

Q2'26 was reported 2026-07-14 at EPS of $7.59 against a $5.59 estimate — a 36.0% beat. A firm of this size, covered by 61 analysts, does not out-earn consensus by 36% on operating momentum. So the question is what the item was.

The Firm disclosed it ten weeks in advance. From the Q1'26 10-Q, Recent events (and again in Note 2):

> "On April 13, 2026, Visa commenced an exchange offer expiring on May 8, 2026 for any and all outstanding shares of Visa Class B-1 common stock and Visa Class B-2 common stock... The Firm has tendered its 18.6 million Visa B-2 shares, and that tender is pending Visa's acceptance. Upon acceptance by Visa of the Firm's tender, the Visa C shares received by the Firm would be recognized at fair value, which is expected to result in a gain that may be recorded as early as the second quarter of 2026."

Note 2 adds the mechanism that makes the gain nearly all profit: the Visa B-2 shares "are held at their nominal carryover basis." A near-zero-cost asset marked to fair value is a gain almost equal to its value.

And the Firm has done this before, at a size that anchors the estimate. The 2025 Form 10-K explains FY2025's noninterest revenue as being held back by "the absence of the $7.9 billion net gain related to Visa shares recorded in the second quarter of 2024."

Now the payload arithmetic, which independently reproduces exactly that shape. The vendor's revenue for a bank is gross interest income plus noninterest revenue, so noninterest revenue is recoverable as revenue − interestIncome, and net revenue as revenue − interestExpense. Both reconcile to the filing for Q1'26 (derived NIR $24.470B against the 10-Q's "$24.5 billion"; derived NII $25.366B against "$25.4 billion"), which validates the method before applying it to Q2.

6b. The return bridge (why the multiple moves)

Expected return over 12-24 months decomposes as tangible-book-value-per-share growth (+8-10%, filed rate) + multiple drift (modest COMPRESSION assumed, 3.19x → ~3.05x, roughly −4%) + dividend yield (1.68%) — call it 5-7% a year total.

We assume the multiple compresses slightly, and the reasoning is explicit. 3.19x prices a 21% ROTCE as permanent. The 21% is being earned at a point when capital-markets revenue is exceptional (CIB net income +30% in Q1'26, advisory fees +82%), when credit costs are benign, and before the Basel re-proposals bite. We are not forecasting a collapse — we are declining to capitalise a cycle peak into perpetuity. A 3.05x exit on 12-month-forward tangible book of about $122 gives $372.

Note what this bridge does NOT contain: any re-rating. Almost the whole expected return is tangible-book compounding. If the multiple simply held at 3.19x against $122 of forward tangible book, the price would be $389 — 8.8% above spot — so the base case is actually conservative relative to a flat multiple. We take the conservative leg deliberately, because the buyback runs against us here. At 3.2x tangible book, $34.6B a year of repurchases retires capital at more than three times its tangible carrying value; that supports EPS but suppresses tangible-book growth, which is why the observed 8-10.5% compounding is slower than a 20% ROTCE alone would produce.

The bull case at $422 requires the multiple to expand to 3.40x — i.e. the market capitalising a 22% ROTCE. That leg is fragile and is not needed for the base. The bear case at $276 requires only that ROTCE normalise to 17%, with no crisis and no credit event. That asymmetry — a bull that needs a re-rating and a bear that needs only mean reversion — is the honest shape of the risk here.

11. Verdict, kill-criteria and flip conditions

Hold.

This is a genuinely excellent institution trading at a genuinely fair price, and the discipline of the dive is to say so rather than to manufacture a view.

What the business is: the largest bank in the United States, earning a 20% return on tangible common equity (filed, FY2025) and 23% in Q1'26, with CET1 at 14.3% against an 11.5% requirement, $1.5 trillion of liquidity sources, $2.6 trillion of average deposits growing 7%, the number-one global investment-banking franchise by fees, an asset-management arm compounding net income at 20%, and tangible book value per share compounding 8-10.5% while $34.6B of stock is repurchased annually. There is no quality objection to make.

What the price is: 3.19x derived tangible book value per share of ~$112 (3.28x the last filed figure of $108.87), which on a residual-income frame — justified P/TBV = (ROTCE − g) ÷ (COE − g) — already requires a sustained ROTCE of 20.95% at a 10% cost of equity and 5% growth. The Firm earns 20-21%. The multiple and the return meet exactly. Our $372 base is 4.0% above spot; the street is at $367.83. Two methods and the crowd converge on the same number.

What the quarter was: the 36% beat that drove the last three months of outperformance was, on the arithmetic in Section 2, almost entirely a Visa Class B-2 exchange gain the Firm disclosed in its Q1 10-Q on 2026-05-01 — net interest income flat, provision flat, tax rate up, noninterest revenue up $7.366B in one quarter. Cleaned, the quarter earned about $5.50-5.60 against a $5.59 estimate. The street already knows: its Q3 estimate is $5.83, a step down from $7.59. The price may not.

Why Hold and not Buy: no discount, no variant perception, and a negative skew — 17.9% to the bull against 22.8% to the bear, because the bull needs a re-rating and the bear needs only mean reversion in ROTCE from a cycle peak. Why Hold and not Avoid or Watch: nothing here is broken. A 20% ROTCE compounder with a fortress capital position, bought at fair value, still returns tangible-book compounding plus a 1.68% dividend — 5-7% a year — and does so with a 0.982 beta. If you own it, own it. If you do not, this is not the price at which to start.

Pre-registered KILL criteria — what would take this to Avoid:

  • ROTCE printing below 17% on a trailing basis, which takes the justified multiple to 2.40x and fair value to $276, 23% below spot. This is the single most important line.
  • A provision quarter above $4.0B, or allowance coverage rising above ~1.95% from the current 1.82%, or the Card Services net charge-off rate exceeding the guided 3.4% — the credit cycle turning while the price embeds it staying benign.
  • Net interest income excluding Markets missing the ~$95B guide, which would mean the deposit franchise is not carrying the guided growth and Markets NII is masking a flat core.
  • A Basel III / GSIB outcome at or above the Firm's own ~6% CET1 estimate combined with a suspension or material slowdown of the $50B repurchase programme.
  • A second one-off gain being treated as recurring — the Q1'26 10-Q notes Visa may extend further exchange offers on timing outside the Firm's control. If FY2027 consensus rises on another such item, the forward multiple is being computed on a fiction.
  • Commercial & Investment Bank net income falling more than 15% year-on-year — at 48.8% of firm earnings, a capital-markets downturn is now the dominant single-segment risk.

Pre-registered FLIP TO BUY (initiate or upsize):

  • A price below ~$330 (the 50-DMA), at which the derived multiple is ~2.95x and the required ROTCE falls to about 19.8% against a filed 20% — the first level where the arithmetic pays you rather than merely being fair. Below ~$300 (~2.68x, required ROTCE ~18.4%) the case becomes emphatic.
  • A Basel III / GSIB final rule materially softer than the ~4-6% CET1 increase the Firm estimates, which would free capital, lift both ROTCE and buyback capacity, and justify 3.4x.
  • Two consecutive quarters of ROTCE above 22% with the provision stable and NO one-off items — evidence that the current return is structural rather than cyclical.
  • NII excluding Markets beating the ~$95B guide, showing the deposit franchise re-pricing.
  • A clean Q3'26 print near or above $5.83 with no gain, which would validate the underlying run-rate and remove the principal information risk in this dive.

Where JPM fits in the Synthos Framework Portfolio. The low-beta financials / quality-compounder sleeve, as a 0-1% position at this price — a hold for existing holders and not an initiation. Beta 0.982 means it offers no defensive discount to the index either.
