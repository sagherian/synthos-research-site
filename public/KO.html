The Overview

Coca-Cola does not, for the most part, make or deliver Coca-Cola. It makes the concentrated syrup, sells it to independent bottling companies around the world, and lets them buy the factories, the trucks and the vending machines. Those bottlers keep a share of the revenue; Coca-Cola keeps the brand, the marketing and about 62 cents of gross profit on every dollar of sales, without owning much of anything.

That is an extremely good arrangement. It means the company spends only about $2 billion a year on equipment against nearly $48 billion of sales, and it earns roughly 43 cents of profit each year for every dollar of shareholders' money invested.

The problem is that it barely grows. Sales rose 1.9% last year and 2.9% the year before, and analysts expect them to rise 0.3% next year. In the three months to July, sales in Latin America rose 16% and in North America 7.5%, but in Europe, the Middle East and Africa they rose 2%, and across Asia and the Pacific they rose 0.6%. So a bit less than half the company is growing and a bit more than half is standing still. Profits grow faster than sales — about 7% a year — because the margins improve and the company buys back a small number of shares.

There is one number worth correcting, because most stock screening tools have it badly wrong. Last year Coca-Cola appeared to generate only $5.3 billion of spare cash, which is very poor for a company this size. The reason is that in March 2025 it made a one-off payment of $6,173 million to the former owners of a milk-drinks business called fairlife, which it had bought in 2020 with a deal that paid more if the business did well. It did very well, so the payment was enormous. Once that is out of the way, the underlying figure is about $14.3 billion a year — nearly three times what the annual accounts suggested. The company's own quarterly filing shows the swing directly: in the first half of 2025 it consumed $1.4 billion of cash; in the first half of 2026 it generated $7.5 billion.

Two things to watch. The American tax authority is trying to collect roughly $3.3 billion plus interest relating to the years 2007 to 2009, and the tax court has so far mostly agreed with it. And the company has just changed chief executive — Henrique Braun signed the latest quarterly filing, with the previous chief executive staying on as chairman.

The shares are $86.56. We think they are worth about $89. Analysts on average say $95.82. When our number is essentially the price, on a company whose sales grow 2% a year, the answer is to hold what you own and collect the 2.45% dividend, which is comfortably covered.

  • Downside Risk 4/10. Very safe by the standards of this batch — the least volatile share here — with a live tax dispute as the one real threat.
  • Growth Quality 4/10. High-quality profits from very little growth. Half the world is expanding, half is not.
  • Exponential Potential 2/10. None, and none is claimed.

Putting a number on it: our fair-value estimate is $89 against a current price of $86.56 — real upside if our numbers are right.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, plus an independent, public-math cross-check further down the page (“Check our number”) so you can judge it for yourself.

1. What the business is, and how the disclosure actually works

Coca-Cola manufactures and sells beverage concentrates and syrups, and finished beverages, under Coca-Cola, Coca-Cola Zero Sugar, Diet Coke, Sprite, Fanta, Simply, Minute Maid, fairlife, Costa, BodyArmor, Powerade and dozens of others. Chief executive Henrique Braun, who signs the certifications on the 10-Q filed 2026-07-29; James Quincey remains Chairman and appears in the insider filings as "director, officer: Chairman". Approximately 65,900 employees. CIK 0000021344; NYSE-listed since 1962.

Five operating segments, per the 10-Q — and this is the point at which the vendor file breaks. The filing states the segments are "Europe, Middle East and Africa ('EMEA'), Latin America, North America, Asia Pacific, and Bottling Investments", plus Corporate and Eliminations. Global Ventures, which was a reportable segment in FY2024, no longer exists, and the 10-Q adds that "Effective March 31, 2026, our Company's chief operating decision maker ('CODM') is our Chief Executive Officer" — a governance change concurrent with the chief-executive transition.

The BodyArmor writedowns deserve separate mention. Coca-Cola acquired the remaining interest in BodyArmor in 2021 for approximately $5.6 billion and has now impaired the trademark by $760 million in 2024 and a further $960 million in 2025 — $1,720 million in two consecutive years. Two impairments of the same asset in two years is a statement about the original price, and it sits alongside a $6,173 million fairlife earnout as evidence that the acquisition record has been expensive.

Gross margin is the stable line and it is genuinely excellent: 61.9% trailing, against 61.6% in FY2025 ($29,544M on $47,941M) and 61.1% in FY2024. Coca-Cola's gross margin has not moved materially in six years, through a global inflation cycle — that is pricing power, and it is the single best number in the file.

11. Verdict, kill-criteria and flip conditions

Hold.

What is genuinely excellent: a 61.9% gross margin that has barely moved in six years through a global inflation cycle; a franchise structure that produces a 59.1% operating margin in Latin America and a 13.9% return on invested capital and 43.0% return on equity on only $2.1B of annual capital expenditure; roughly $14.3B of trailing free cash flow covering a $9.13B dividend 1.57 times; net debt of $30.0B at 1.51x EBITDA with total debt down $1.95B in six months; a 0.349 beta, the lowest in this batch; and a forward dividend of $2.12, a 2.45% yield, on a distribution record the knowledge base describes as running more than sixty years.

What we are declining to pay up for: revenue growing 1.9% in FY2025 and 2.9% in FY2024, with consensus at +0.3% for FY2027; two of four geographic segments — 36% of operating-segment revenue — growing 2.0% and 0.6% in the June quarter; $1,720 million of BodyArmor impairments in two consecutive years on top of a $6,173 million fairlife earnout; an Internal Revenue Service claim for approximately $3.3 billion plus interest on which the Tax Court has "predominantly sided with the IRS" twice; a brand-new chief executive with no strategy disclosure in the file; and a price at 26.0x trailing and 24.5x FY2027 consensus, at the 89th percentile of the annual range.

The distinction that matters. This is not a business problem — Coca-Cola's operating execution in the June quarter was better than its reported margins suggest and its cash generation is roughly triple what the annual statements imply. It is a price problem of an unusually pure kind: 26x forward earnings for 2% revenue growth means the return is the dividend plus mid-single-digit earnings growth plus whatever the multiple does, and the multiple has already expanded. The knowledge base supplies the precedent in a single sentence — bought at 12-14x, ran to 60x, "then stayed expensive for 25 years and underperformed since." A holder is being paid properly to wait. A buyer is not being paid at all.

Pre-registered KILL criteria — what would take this to Avoid:

  • An adverse final ruling on the tax matter at or near the full $3.3 billion plus interest, particularly if extended to years after 2009 as the 10-K warns is possible.
  • A third consecutive BodyArmor impairment, or a new impairment against Costa or fairlife.
  • North American operating margin falling below 28% — it was 31.3% in the June quarter and is the lowest of the four geographic segments on the largest revenue base.
  • Consolidated revenue declining year on year in any quarter, which has not happened in the nine quarters of data here.
  • A dividend increase skipped, breaking a streak the knowledge base puts at more than sixty years. This would be a signal of unusual severity.
  • A large restructuring programme from the new chief executive that resets the earnings base — the most common event following a chief-executive transition, and this company has taken large charges in each of the last two years already.

Pre-registered FLIP conditions — what would take this to Buy — Tactical:

  • Price below approximately $77 — roughly 21.8x FY2027E and near the 200-day average of $76.28 — at which the dividend yield is 2.75% and the base case is +16%. This is the single most likely path to a Buy and it is 11% below today's close.
  • EMEA or Asia Pacific revenue growth inflecting above 5%. Together they are 36% of operating-segment revenue and are currently contributing nothing; either turning would materially change the consensus revenue path.
  • A meaningful step-up in the buyback. At $746M — 0.20% of market capitalisation — against roughly $14.3B of free cash flow and a $9.13B dividend, there is $4B a year of unallocated cash. Deploying it would add roughly 100 basis points to the shareholder yield.
  • Favourable resolution of the tax dispute, removing a quantified multi-billion contingency.
  • Genuine multi-voice, recent knowledge-base conviction appearing. At present 41% of the lane is one podcast episode from February 2024 whose headline forecast has been falsified, and only one claim carries a named speaker. That thinness is a direct input to the verdict.

Single biggest risk: the Internal Revenue Service dispute. The 10-K discloses a claim to increase 2007-2009 U.S. taxable income creating potential additional federal tax of approximately $3.3 billion plus interest, states that the Tax Court "issued an opinion... predominantly siding with the IRS" in November 2020 and a supplemental opinion also siding with the IRS in November 2023, and warns that the adjustments "could have a material adverse impact on the Company's financial position, results of operations and cash flows" and could extend to subsequent years by broadening the scope to additional foreign licensees. The company points to a favourable Eighth Circuit decision in a comparable case as "highly supportive" of its position, and that is a real mitigant — but it is a mitigant, not a resolution. At $3.3 billion before interest this is 0.9% of market capitalisation; extended across the subsequent years the 10-K contemplates, it is a materially larger number, and no provision for the disputed amount is reflected in the figures used in this dive.

Most fragile assumption in the price: that the market keeps paying 24-26x forward earnings for 2% revenue growth. Every operational judgement in this dive can be right and the shares can still return nothing, because the return is 6.9% consensus earnings growth plus a 2.65% shareholder yield against a multiple sitting at the top of its range. If the multiple compresses to 22x FY2027E the price is $78 — a 10% decline with the business performing exactly to plan. That is the specific mechanism the knowledge base's own bear claim describes, and it is why a name with nothing wrong with it is a Hold rather than a Buy.
