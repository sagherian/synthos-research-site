The Overview

When a data centre runs artificial intelligence, thousands of chips have to talk to each other constantly. Sending those signals as electricity over copper works over short distances. Over longer ones it becomes hot, slow and enormously power-hungry.

The solution is light. Convert the signal to a laser pulse, send it down a glass fibre, convert it back. Lumentum makes the lasers, the modules that do the converting, and — new this year — the switches that route the light between racks of chips.

That is why this share price has done something unusual. Over the past twelve months it has risen 696%. In the three months to late March, the company's revenue was 90% higher than a year earlier, and it went from losing $65 million on its operations to making $176 million.

Two details in the company's own report are worth more than the headline.

Prices are going up, not down. Normally a components maker sells more units at lower prices. Lumentum says its laser chip prices rose, because customers are moving to a faster standard called 200G lanes. A supplier able to raise prices while doubling volumes is a supplier its customers cannot easily replace.

And there is a genuinely new product. Optical circuit switches — which route light between racks — produced more than $25 million in a single quarter having produced essentially nothing before, and the company says it is expanding manufacturing.

Three independent commentators in our research database, writing in March and April, all identified the same thing: optical connections are the bottleneck in AI data centres, and only a handful of companies make the parts. Unusually, we were able to check their claim against the company's own filings, and it holds up.

So why not simply buy it?

Three reasons, all numbers.

It is expensive. You pay about 46 times what analysts expect the company to earn in the year to June 2027, and about 103 times this year's figure.

Two customers are 38% of the business. The filing says so directly: one accounted for 26% of revenue last quarter and another for 12%. If either designs a competitor's part into its next system, a large chunk of this revenue disappears in one cycle.

And the share count is rising. Lumentum borrowed money using bonds that turn into shares. Those bonds convert at around $188 a share and the stock is $849. On 7 April alone the company handed over 5.7 million new shares — 7% more shares than existed — to retire some of that debt. More is queued: $500.8 million of bondholders had asked to convert early as of 30 April.

And the next results come out on 11 August, seven days from now.

Our estimate of fair value is $1,000, about 18% above the price. Analysts on average say $1,082.67 — and their lowest target, $900, is already above today's price.

Our conclusion is to wait one week. Not because the business looks bad — it is the most impressive in this batch — but because paying 46 times forward earnings for a stock that has risen sevenfold, seven days before it reports, is a decision about courage rather than analysis.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, plus an independent, public-math cross-check further down the page (“Check our number”) so you can judge it for yourself.

Four things in that disclosure deserve separating out, because they are what a components story usually cannot supply.

First, and most important: laser chip average selling prices ROSE. "A slight increase in average selling prices of laser chip products... driven primarily by a shift to 200G lane speeds." A components supplier raising price while nearly doubling volume is describing a supply constraint it controls. That is the single most valuable sentence in this filing and it is the direct empirical test of the knowledge base's "choke point" claim.

Second, and pulling the other way: cloud transceiver prices FELL. "Higher shipment volume, partially offset by lower average selling prices." The transceiver business — the larger of the two Systems drivers at $137.0 million of incremental quarterly revenue — behaves like a normal component business, winning on volume and losing on price. Both dynamics are present in the same company, and the mix between them determines whether the margin holds.

Third, optical circuit switches are a new category ramping from zero. "More than $25.0 million" in a quarter and "more than $38.0 million" across nine months, with manufacturing expansion under way. On $808.4 million of quarterly revenue this is 3.1% — small, growing from nothing, and the specific product the knowledge base's most technical claim names.

Fourth, the growth is broad rather than single-application. "Intra-data center, data center interconnect, and long-haul applications", plus "line subsystems solutions for long-haul terrestrial networks and charge pump products used in sub-sea network installations." Roughly 12% of Components growth came from data transport products unrelated to the AI build.

2. The convertible-note machine — the most consequential thing in this file after the growth

Lumentum funds itself with convertible notes, four series are outstanding, and the conversion mechanics are actively producing shares right now.

The 2032 Notes, from the 10-Q:

> "On September 8, 2025, we issued $1,265.0 million in aggregate principal amounts of 0.375% Convertible Senior Notes due in 2032 ('2032 Notes') in a private placement... The net proceeds from the sale of the 2032 Notes was approximately $1,254.7 million, after deducting $10.3 million of debt issuance costs. Concurrent with the issuance of the 2032 Notes, we used $843.1 million of the net proceeds to repurchase $581.1 million aggregate principal amounts of the 0.50% Convertible Senior Notes due in 2026 and $102.0 million of the net proceeds to pay the cost of the 2032 Capped Call Options...

>

> The initial conversion rate is 5.3257 shares of common stock per $1,000 principal amounts of the 2032 Notes (which is equivalent to an initial conversion price of approximately $187.77 per share)."

The share price is $849.47. The conversion price is $187.77. The stock is 4.52 times the strike.

The other series, also disclosed: the notes "rank equally with all of the Company's existing senior unsecured indebtedness, including the Company's outstanding 0.50% Convertible Senior Notes due 2026, 0.50% Convertible Senior Notes due 2028, and 1.50% Convertible Senior Notes due 2029, and do not contain any financial covenants, restrictions on dividends, incurrence of senior debt or other indebtedness, or the issuance or repurchase of securities by us."

How the dilution actually arrives:

> "Under each series of Notes, we are required to satisfy our conversion obligation with respect to converted Notes by paying cash equal to the principal amount of such converted Notes and paying or delivering... cash, shares of common stock or a combination, at our election, with respect to any conversion value in excess thereof."

So the principal is repaid in cash and the entire gain above par is settled in stock at the company's election. At 4.52 times the conversion price, that excess is roughly 3.5 times the principal.

And it is happening, in two documented forms:

> "As of April 30, 2026, we have received early conversion requests totaling $500.8 million aggregate principal amount of the Notes, which will be settled in cash and the conversion value in excess thereof will be settled in shares of common stock."

> "On April 7, 2026, we entered into privately negotiated exchange arrangements with certain holders of our 2026 Notes and 2029 Notes. Pursuant to these agreements, we issued an aggregate of approximately 5.7 million shares of our common stock in exchange for approximately $264.8 million and $209.8 million aggregate principal amount of the 2026 Notes and 2029 Notes, respectively, and the related conversion value in excess of the principal amounts thereof."

5.7 million shares against 77.8 million outstanding is 7.3% dilution in a single transaction, on 2026-04-07. And $500.8 million of principal has already requested early conversion. The capped call options bought for $102.0 million protect only the 2032 Notes — "specifically designed to mitigate the dilutive impact of the 2032 Notes, such that no dilution will occur until the capped call price is exceeded" — and the cap price is not disclosed in the extractable text of this archive.

The visible evidence in the payload confirms the direction. weightedAverageShsOutDil for fiscal 2025 reads 69.6 million; the 10-Q cover states 77.8 million outstanding at 2026-04-30. An 11.8% increase in ten months. And the balance sheet shows the mirror image: net debt of $2,087.5 million at 2025-06-28 against an implied trailing net debt of approximately $696 million — a $1.4 billion reduction achieved substantially by turning debt into equity.

The honest framing. This is not a scandal and it is not hidden — it is the ordinary and expected consequence of financing a company with converts and then having the stock rise sevenfold. The company got extremely cheap money (0.375%, no covenants) and the holders got the upside. What it means for a buyer today is that the denominator of every per-share figure is rising, and that consensus EPS estimates struck on a share count that keeps growing carry a source of downward pressure the revenue line does not.

4b. The return bridge (why the multiple moves)

Expected return over the next twelve months decomposes as: EPS growth (+125.9%, FY2026E $8.225 to FY2027E $18.579) + multiple compression (assumed LARGE, 103.3x on the forward year to about 53.8x on the then-forward year, −48%) + no dividend ≈ +18%.

That decomposition should be read carefully because it is unusual. The base case assumes earnings more than double AND the multiple almost halves, and the two nearly cancel. That is the correct way to value a company at this stage: the multiple must compress, because 103x forward earnings on 82% revenue growth is a statement about the growth rate rather than about the business, and growth rates decelerate by arithmetic if nothing else — consensus itself has revenue growth going 82.4%, 89.0%, 54.7%.

The practical consequence, stated plainly: almost none of the expected return is re-rating and all of it is earnings. That is the return shape this programme prefers. What it does not do is make the entry safe — because if the earnings do not double, the multiple compresses anyway and there is nothing to catch the price. The bear case at $500 is exactly that scenario and it is 41.1% down.

If the multiple compressed to 35x FY2027E the price would be $650 (−23.5%). At 60x, $1,115 (+31.3%).
