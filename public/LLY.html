The Overview

Eli Lilly makes medicines. For most of its 150-year history it made insulin, antidepressants and cancer drugs. Since about 2022 it has made something else: a class of injections, sold as Mounjaro for diabetes and Zepbound for obesity, that cause people to lose roughly a fifth of their body weight and that appear to improve a long list of other conditions at the same time.

The commercial result has no real precedent at this scale. Revenue went from $28.5 billion in 2022 to $65.2 billion in 2025. In the first three months of 2026 alone the company sold $19.8 billion, up 55% on the same quarter a year before. Roughly 84 cents of every sales dollar is gross profit.

Here is the thing you have to sit with. The company's own annual report says those two drugs were 56% of everything it sold in 2025. Its quarterly report, three months later, says they were 65% of everything it sold in the first quarter of 2026. The business is not diversifying as it grows — it is concentrating.

That is not automatically bad. But it means three ordinary risks become large ones. Patents expire, and the chief executive has said so himself in unusually blunt terms: every drug goes to zero eventually, and this one reverts sometime in the 2030s. Governments set prices, and the annual report confirms that the U.S. health department has already selected three Lilly medicines for administered pricing and expects to select more. And competitors arrive — several are already in late-stage testing.

There is also a price problem that is happening right now rather than later. Read either filing and every single sentence about growth says the same thing: more volume, lower realised prices. Lilly is selling far more units at falling prices per unit, in America, in Europe and especially in China. Management has told investors to expect prices in the category to fall 5-10% a year.

Against that, the news is genuinely good. In April 2026 the FDA approved the company's pill version, which does not need refrigeration and can be manufactured at a scale injections never could — that opens India, Brazil and China. Also in the first quarter, the company finalised a pricing deal with the U.S. government that gives Medicare patients access. And a stronger triple-acting drug is behind it.

The money is real but the cash is lagging. Lilly reported $20.6 billion of profit last year but only $9.0 billion of actual free cash, because it is pouring money into factories and stockpiling inventory to meet demand. It paid out more in dividends and buybacks than it generated, and borrowed the difference. Debt has gone from $33.6 billion to roughly $52 billion in eighteen months.

The shares cost about 25 times what analysts think the company will earn in 2027. For this growth rate that is not expensive. Our estimate of fair value is $1,220 against a price of $1,116 — about 9% higher, and notably lower than the analyst average of $1,315.

The catch is the calendar. Lilly reports its next quarter tomorrow. It has beaten expectations five times running, which means the market now assumes it will. And the number analysts expect — $6.06 a share — is actually lower than the same quarter last year even though sales should be a third bigger, because the company is booking large charges for the companies it is buying. Whatever prints tomorrow will be hard to interpret in the first hour. Buy a piece now if you want the position; keep the rest until you have seen the release.

  • Downside Risk 6/10. Extraordinary returns on capital, against 65% single-franchise concentration, falling realised prices, ~$52B of debt against $26.5B of equity, and two product-liability MDLs.
  • Growth Quality 9/10. +44.7% revenue in FY2025, +55.5% in Q1'26, volume-led, 83.8% gross margin, broadening geographically.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, plus an independent, public-math cross-check further down the page (“Check our number”) so you can judge it for yourself.

6b. The return bridge (why the multiple moves)

Expected return over 12-24 months decomposes as EPS growth (+44.7% in 2026, +28.9% in 2027 on consensus) + multiple drift (we assume COMPRESSION, from 24.7x to ~27x on the following year — which is compression on a rolling basis) + shareholder yield (~1.0%: 0.62% indicated dividend plus roughly 0.4% net buyback).

This is the opposite structure to most names in this batch, and it is worth being precise about. Our base case of $1,220 is 27.0x FY2027E, higher than today's 24.7x FY2027E — but the same $1,220 is only 23.4x FY2028E, against 21.4x today. The multiple is not expanding; the earnings are. Essentially the entire base-case return is earnings growth, and we are assuming the market pays a slightly lower forward multiple next year than it does today, because the growth rate decelerates from +30.8% to +16.5% to +12.1% and because concentration risk rises.

Why compression rather than expansion. Three reasons, all evidenced above. The revenue growth rate roughly halves between 2026 and 2027 on consensus. Free cash flow conversion is 43.5% of net income and shareholder returns are debt-funded. 11. Verdict, kill-criteria and flip conditions

Buy — Tactical.

The business deserves ownership. The entry deserves patience of a very specific and short-dated kind.

What the filings establish. Revenue of $65.179B in 2025, up 44.7%, and $19.799B in the first quarter of 2026, up 55.5% year-over-year, at an 83.8% gross margin, driven by volume: Mounjaro U.S. +53% in 2025 and +59% in the first quarter of 2026, Mounjaro ex-U.S. from $2.6B to $9.3B, Zepbound U.S. +174% then +79%. The FDA approved Foundayo (orforglipron) for obesity in April 2026, validating the $1.5 billion of pre-launch inventory the 10-K had capitalised and removing the injectable-capacity constraint. U.S. government pricing agreements were finalised in the first quarter, converting a policy overhang into Medicare access. Return on invested capital is 32.1%, interest coverage 37.5x, research spending $13.338B across approximately 12,000 research employees.

What you are accepting, stated without softening. Mounjaro and Zepbound were 65% of revenue in the first quarter of 2026, against 56% for full-year 2025 — concentration rising nine points in a year. Every growth sentence in both filings ends with "lower realized prices", and management guides to 5-10% annual category price deflation. Free cash flow was $8.972B against $20.638B of net income (43.5% conversion), with $8.093B absorbed by working capital and 445 days of inventory on the balance sheet. HHS has selected three Lilly products for administered pricing and the 10-K says "we expect other significant products will be selected in future years." Two federal product-liability MDLs cover the incretins. And the chief executive himself has said that "every drug goes to zero at patent expiry; Mounjaro reverts to baseline ~2030s."

On the earnings print — the decision this dive was written to make. Lilly reports tomorrow, 2026-08-05, on consensus EPS of $6.06 and revenue of $20.685B, after five consecutive beats. We are striking a Buy recommendation into that, and here is the justification, stated as a specific decision rather than a preference.

Why we are not waiting entirely. The valuation does not depend on the quarter: at 24.7x FY2027 consensus for a business growing revenue 30% this year, a single quarter moves the fair value by low single digits. The technical set-up is favourable rather than stretched — RSI 42, below the 50-DMA, 9.7% off the high — which is the opposite of the conditions that argue for waiting. The entire published analyst target range, floor included, sits above the current price. And the structural catalysts (orforglipron launch, Medicare access, ex-U.S. ramp) are already in the filings and do not require tomorrow to confirm them.

Why we are not buying the full position. Three reasons, in order of weight. First, the expectation is now in the price: beats of 22.7%, 9.1%, 23.4% and 12.7% mean the market prices a beat, so the payoff around a sixth is asymmetric against the buyer. Second, tomorrow's number is close to uninterpretable in the first hour: consensus implies an EPS decline of 4.0% on a revenue increase of 33.0%, which reconciles only through large charges — most plausibly the acquired in-process research from the up-to-$12 billion of pending deals the 10-Q discloses, up from under $3 billion three months earlier. Third, the last beat's composition was mixed: the 10-Q attributes part of it to "a favorable one-time adjustment to estimates for rebates and discounts," the tax rate fell 380 basis points, and the prior-year comparison carried a $1.72-per-share charge. We would be underwriting a run-rate that the filing itself tells us was not a clean run-rate.

The operational instruction, therefore: establish roughly one third of the target weight today at $1,116.11; hold the remainder for the session after the release, sized according to what the release actually says about realised price and incretin concentration rather than about the headline. A staged entry is Buy — Tactical by definition.

Pre-registered KILL criteria — what would break the thesis:

  • A cut to the full-year 2026 guidance range of $82.0-$85.0B revenue or $35.50-$37.00 non-GAAP EPS. The implied back-half of $20.89-$22.39 (59-61% of the year) is steep enough that a cut is a live possibility, and it would falsify the growth path directly.
  • Gross margin below approximately 80%. It has gone 82.5% → 84.3% → 82.9% → 85.1% → 81.9%, and the 10-Q names lower realised prices as the cause of the last decline. Sub-80% would mean price erosion has outrun manufacturing scale.
  • The incretin share of revenue above 70% without offsetting pipeline revenue. 56% → 65% in one year is already the fastest-moving risk on this page.
  • An incretin selected for U.S. government-administered pricing. The 10-K explicitly anticipates further selections.
  • Free cash flow conversion staying below 50% of net income for a second full year while capital expenditure continues to rise. One year is a capacity ramp; two is a business model.
  • A material adverse development in either incretin product-liability MDL, given the 10-K's disclosure that Lilly is "predominantly self-insured for litigation liability losses."
  • Failure of the Centessa acquisition, which would trigger the special mandatory redemption at 101% on five of the eight tranches issued 2026-05-20.

Pre-registered FLIP TO BUY — CORE (upsize) criteria:

  • A clean second-quarter release in which revenue meets or exceeds $20.685B, gross margin holds at or above 81.9%, and no new one-time rebate adjustment appears. This is the single most likely path to full size and it resolves within 24 hours.
  • A retracement toward the 200-DMA at $1,027.79 (7.9% below) with the fundamentals intact — the second entry point, and a materially better one.
  • Evidence that the oral franchise is additive rather than substitutive: total incretin revenue accelerating while the injectable lines hold.
  • Working capital reversing — inventory days falling from 445 and free cash flow conversion rising above 60% of net income.
  • A first sequential gross-margin increase alongside revenue growth, which would mean scale has finally outrun price erosion.
