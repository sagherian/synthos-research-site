The Overview

MercadoLibre is Latin America's Amazon, and also its PayPal, and increasingly its bank. It runs the biggest online marketplace in Brazil, Mexico and Argentina; it processes the payments; it lends money to buyers and sellers; and it has been building its own network of warehouses and delivery vehicles.

The business is growing very fast. Revenue rose 39% last year and 49% in the first three months of this year. Very few companies of this size grow like that.

But profits are not growing at all. In that same quarter, operating profit fell 20% and net profit fell 16%. Out of every dollar of sales, the company kept about 7 cents in operating profit, against 13 cents a year earlier.

There are two explanations and they lead to opposite conclusions.

The optimistic one — which several commentators in our research library make — is that this is deliberate. MercadoLibre is spending heavily on warehouses and delivery, exactly as Amazon did for years, and on building a lending business. Those costs are largely fixed once built, so as sales keep growing the profits eventually arrive in a rush. Analysts believe this: they expect profits to be flat this year and then jump 43% next year.

The pessimistic one is that the spending is competitive — Amazon and Sea Limited are attacking the same markets — and it does not stop.

The market has been voting for the pessimistic reading. The shares are down 20% over the past year while the American market rose 24%, and they sit 25% below their high. The company has also come in below analysts' profit forecasts in each of the last four quarters.

At $1,888.50 the shares cost about 50 times last year's profits and 34 times what analysts expect for 2027. Our estimate of fair value is $2,140, about 13% above the price. Analysts on average say $2,167.

Two things to know before deciding. First, the company reports results tomorrow, and analysts themselves expect profits to fall 16% while sales rise 44%. Second, MercadoLibre returns nothing to shareholders — no dividend, and last year it bought back one million dollars of stock, which on a $96 billion company is nothing. If the shares do not rise, you earn zero.

We are saying Watch rather than Buy: the business is excellent, the price is not obviously wrong, and the single fact that would settle it — whether profit margins have turned — arrives tomorrow. We would buy this at $1,600.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, We don’t publish a reverse-DCF cross-check for pre-profit companies — negative or missing earnings break that math — so take this number on our modeling alone.

Expected return over the next twelve months decomposes as: adjusted EPS growth (+43.1%, from FY2026E $39.343 to FY2027E $56.318) + multiple COMPRESSION (48.0x on the forward year moving to roughly 38x on the then-forward year, −21%) + shareholder yield (ZERO) ≈ +13%.

A caution the addendum requires: the rolldown from 48.0x FY2026 to 33.5x FY2027 at a constant price is mechanical, not compression, and is not counted as such. What our base DOES assume is that the market pays 38x the FY2027 number a year from now, against the 48.0x it currently pays on FY2026 — genuine de-rating, on the ground that a company whose earnings compound at 40% deserves a lower multiple than one whose earnings are flat only if you believe the 40% is durable, and four consecutive misses argue for discounting it.

Note what this makes the return: all of it is earnings growth, roughly half of which is handed back in de-rating, and there is no yield whatsoever. MercadoLibre pays no dividend and repurchased $1 million of stock in fiscal 2025 — on a $95.7 billion market capitalisation, that is 0.001%. A shareholder here owns growth and nothing else, and if the growth is deferred the wait is uncompensated.

If the multiple fell to 28x FY2027E the price is $1,577 (−16.5%). If it held at 48x, $2,703 (+43.1%).

> "Adjusted free cash flow represents cash from operating activities less the increase (decrease) in cash and cash equivalents and investments related to customer funds due to regulatory requirements and other restrictions... investments in property and equipment and intangible assets, changes in loans receivable, net and net proceeds from/payments on loans payable and other financial liabilities related to our Fintech solutions, since we consider those liabilities as the working capital of the Fintech activities."

A payments business holding customer balances shows those balances as operating cash inflows; they are not the company's money. freeCashFlowYieldTTM, priceToFreeCashFlowRatioTTM, evToFreeCashFlowTTM (9.75x), priceToOperatingCashFlowRatioTTM (8.01x), freeCashFlowToEquityTTM ($14,557M — 15.2% of market capitalisation), freeCashFlowToFirmTTM and incomeQualityTTM are all rejected.
