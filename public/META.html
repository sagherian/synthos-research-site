The Overview

Meta owns Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp and Messenger, and makes almost all its money selling advertising on them. About 3.6 billion people use one of its apps every day.

The advertising business is doing extremely well. In the three months to June, revenue grew 28% from a year earlier. Meta showed 14% more ads than a year ago and charged 12% more for each one — both of those growth rates are faster than they were the year before. That is a rare combination and it is not the behaviour of a business in trouble.

And yet the shares have fallen 26% from their high and are down 22% over a year in which the wider market rose 24%.

The explanation is in the costs. Meta is spending an enormous amount of money building data centres and buying computing power for artificial intelligence. In the June quarter its profit from the apps business actually fell 6% even though sales rose 28% — the costs grew faster than the revenue. Research spending rose 67%.

The numbers behind the spending are hard to absorb. Six months ago Meta disclosed that it had signed data-centre leases worth $104 billion that had not yet started. Its latest report says that figure is now $279 billion, and that it signed another $68 billion of leases in July. Its total contracted commitments went from $131 billion to $349 billion in half a year. It expects to spend $130-145 billion on equipment and buildings this year alone — up from a $115-135 billion estimate it gave in January.

The cash consequences are already visible. In 2025 Meta kept $46 billion of cash after all its investment; in the first half of 2026 it kept about $13 billion, and full-year spending is heavily weighted to the second half. And it stopped buying back its own shares completely — none at all in the first six months of the year, after $26 billion last year, with $25 billion of approval sitting unused. That is the clearest signal management can send about where the money is going.

Two things you should not be misled by. The January-to-March quarter looked spectacular — profit of $10.44 a share against an expected $6.70 — but roughly $3.13 of that was a one-off tax refund of $8 billion, not business performance. And the April-to-June quarter looked bad — $6.18 against an expected $7.19 — partly because it included $1.18 billion of redundancy payments for about 8,000 job cuts. Strip both out and the underlying earnings are around $29.61 for the last year, which puts the shares on about 20 times earnings — and about 17 times what analysts expect for 2027.

One more correction, and it matters. The data file we work from says Meta has $48 billion of net debt. It does not. The file ignores $46 billion of marketable securities Meta actually owns. Real net debt is about $2.3 billion. This company is not financially stretched; it is spending its own money.

Our estimate of fair value is $690 against a price of $587.94. Every analyst target — even the lowest, at $595 — is above today's price. The catch is that nothing new will be learned until 28 October. Buy some now, keep some back.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, plus an independent, public-math cross-check further down the page (“Check our number”) so you can judge it for yourself.

2. The two distorted quarters — and what Meta actually earns

Neither of the last two headline earnings numbers means what it appears to mean, and the vendor file gives no indication of that.

Q1 2026: EPS $10.44 against an estimate of $6.70 — an apparent 56% beat. The 10-Q filed 2026-07-30 explains it:

> "Our effective tax rate for the six months ended June 30, 2026 was (5)%, compared to the U.S. federal statutory rate of 21%. The rate was lower than the statutory rate primarily due to a discrete income tax benefit of $8.03 billion recognized in the first quarter of 2026 in connection with U.S. Corporate Alternative Minimum Tax (CAMT) transitional relief provided under Treasury Notice 2026-7 ... The income tax benefit of $8.03 billion partially offsets the $15.93 billion discrete charge recognized in the third quarter of 2025 upon enactment of the One Big Beautiful Bill Act."

$8.03 billion on approximately 2.564 billion diluted shares is $3.13 a share. Strip it and Q1 EPS was roughly $7.31 against a $6.70 estimate — a real beat, of 9%, not 56%. The tell was on the face of the income statement and the vendor file carries it without comment: Q1 net income of $26.773B EXCEEDED operating income of $22.872B by $3.9 billion, which for a company with $562M of interest expense is only possible through a negative tax provision.

The mirror image sits in Q3 2025. The vendor's inc_q reports GAAP diluted EPS of $1.05 for the September 2025 quarter on operating income of $20.535B; the vendor's earn_cal reports $7.25 for the same quarter. The difference is exactly the $15.93 billion discrete charge: $15.93B ÷ 2.572B shares = $6.19, and $1.05 + $6.19 = $7.24. 11. Verdict, kill-criteria and flip conditions

Buy — Tactical.

The business is performing. The stock is not. The reason is documented, dateable, and does not resolve for 85 days.

What the filings established, five and six days ago. Advertising revenue up 27% in the June quarter and 30% for six months, with ad impressions delivered up 14% (against 11% a year earlier) and average price per ad up 12% (against 9%) — both accelerating simultaneously. Family of Apps revenue up 28%; Family of Apps other revenue — paid messaging and subscriptions — up 73%. 3.60 billion daily active people. Gross margin 81.4%. Return on equity 29.7%. Net debt of $2.305 billion, not the $48.024 billion the vendor reports. Sixty-five covering analysts with a consensus of $723.69 and a low target of $595 — the entire band above the price. RSI 26.2.

What you are accepting, stated without softening. Family of Apps income from operations FELL 6% while its revenue rose 28%. Total operating margin fell from 43.0% to 30.9% in a year. Free cash flow was $46.109B in 2025 and roughly $13.2B in the first half of 2026, against full-year capital-expenditure guidance of $130-145 billion — raised from $115-135 billion six months earlier. The buyback stopped completely: "we did not repurchase any shares of Class A common stock during the six months ended June 30, 2026", with $25.03 billion authorised and unused. Uncommenced lease obligations went from $103.77B to $278.99B in six months, with ~$68B more signed in July; contractual commitments went from $131.05B to $349.31B; and Meta discloses no contracted revenue backlog against any of it. Senior notes went from $18.4B to $84.0 billion of principal in thirty months, now maturing out to 2066. And Meta's own risk factors state that these investments "have the effect of reducing our cash flows, operating margin and profitability." Our knowledge base called this in March 2026 — "flipped from cash cow to capital-intensive cash burner" — and was right.

Why Tactical rather than Core, and why not Watch. Base fair value of $690 is 17.4% above spot with roughly 2.3:1 asymmetry — the best risk-reward in this batch — and sits 4.7% below a street consensus of $723.69, so we are the conservative party. The reason this is not Watch is that an RSI of 26.2 on a business growing advertising revenue 27%, with a corrected balance sheet showing essentially no net debt, is a price at which accumulation is the correct action rather than observation. The reason it is not Core is that there is no catalyst for 85 days, the operating-margin decline has no demonstrated floor, and the single variable that matters — 2027 capital expenditure — has not been guided. Buy it in tranches into the weakness.

Pre-registered KILL criteria — what would break the thesis:

  • A third consecutive increase in capital-expenditure guidance, or a 2027 guide above the 2026 range of $130-145 billion. Two raises is a build; three is a business-model change.
  • Family of Apps income from operations declining for a second and third consecutive quarter. One quarter is a severance charge; three is inverted operating leverage.
  • Full-year 2026 free cash flow below zero, or any indication that 2027 free cash flow will also be negative.
  • Ad impression growth or price-per-ad growth decelerating. Both are currently accelerating (14% and 12% against 11% and 9%) and they are the load-bearing facts in the bull case.
  • The uncommenced-lease disclosure rising materially above ~$350 billion, or contractual commitments above ~$400 billion, without any disclosure of contracted revenue against them.
  • A material adverse development in the state-court product-addiction litigation, or a European accrual large enough to be quantified separately.
  • A capital raise in equity, or notes issuance at a scale that takes principal materially above $100 billion.

Pre-registered FLIP TO BUY — CORE (upsize) criteria:

  • The share repurchase programme restarting. $25.03 billion is authorised and unused; resuming it would be the single clearest statement that management sees a ceiling on the capital cycle, and it is the highest-signal event available.
  • 2027 capital-expenditure guidance at or below the 2026 range.
  • Family of Apps income from operations returning to year-over-year growth, which the ~8,000-person reduction should assist from the September quarter onward.
  • Free cash flow positive and rising for two consecutive quarters.
  • A test of the 52-week closing low of $525.72 with the advertising metrics intact — this is the second most likely path to a full-size position and the explicit reason a tranche is held back.
  • Any disclosure of contracted revenue against the infrastructure investment — the absence of one is the most articulate bear argument in the tracked lane, and its arrival would remove it.
