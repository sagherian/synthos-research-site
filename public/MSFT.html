The Overview

Microsoft sells the software that businesses run on — the office suite, the email, the databases, the professional network, the video-game business, and above all the rented computing power that companies use instead of owning their own machines.

For most of the past year, investors did not like it. The shares fell 34% in the first three months of 2026 and are still down about 6% over twelve months in which the wider market rose 24%. The reason was simple and rational: Microsoft has been spending staggering sums building data centres. It spent $116 billion last year, which is 35 cents of every dollar of revenue. Historically Microsoft was a business that took your money and barely had to spend any to earn more of it. Now it has to build factories.

The worry is that all that concrete and equipment has to be written off over time as an expense, and that the write-offs will arrive before the revenue does — turning a wonderfully profitable software company into an ordinary infrastructure company.

Then the annual report arrived on 29 July and gave the strongest available answer to that worry. Microsoft disclosed that it has $678 billion of business already signed and contracted but not yet delivered — up 84% in a single year, against annual sales of $332 billion. In other words, more than two years of business is already booked. About 30% of it turns into revenue within the next twelve months.

That does not prove the spending will pay off. But it is the closest thing to proof that anyone has offered, because it is a contractual number, not a forecast.

Two things you should hold against it. First, the cash Microsoft actually keeps has been falling for three years — $74 billion, then $72 billion, then $67 billion — even as accounting profits rose from $88 billion to $134 billion. All the difference went into buildings and equipment. Second, the report reveals $329 billion of data-centre leases that have been signed but have not started yet. That is a real obligation, running out to 2033, and it appears in none of the debt figures anyone quotes.

The shares cost 25 times what analysts expect the company to earn in the year to June 2027. For a business earning a 33% return on its equity, that is reasonable. Our estimate of fair value is $550 against a price of $492.81.

The catch is timing. The shares have jumped 19% in three months, and the standard measure of "overbought" reads 80.6 out of 100 — the most stretched in this batch. Good business, fair price, poor moment. Buy it over several weeks rather than in one go.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, plus an independent, public-math cross-check further down the page (“Check our number”) so you can judge it for yourself.

2. Two earnings numbers, and which one to use

Microsoft reports both GAAP and adjusted results, and the difference is entirely one investment. The 10-K states:

> "Adjusted net income and adjusted diluted earnings per share ('EPS') are non-GAAP financial measures. These non-GAAP financial measures exclude net gains and losses from investments in [a strategic artificial-intelligence partner]."

and quantifies it:

> "Current year net income and diluted EPS were positively impacted by net gains from investments ... which resulted in an increase in net income and diluted EPS of $5.0 billion and $0.67, respectively. Prior year net income and diluted EPS were negatively impacted by net losses ... which resulted in a decrease in net income and diluted EPS of $3.6 billion and $0.49, respectively."

The other-income footnote adds the gross figures: "Other income (expense), net included $6.5 billion of net gains and $4.8 billion of net losses for fiscal years 2026 and 2025, respectively ... primarily net recognized gains (losses) on our equity method investment ... The net gains recorded for fiscal year 2026 primarily relate to the dilution gain from the ... Recapitalization."

11. Verdict, kill-criteria and flip conditions

Buy — Tactical.

The business case is strong enough to own. The entry point is poor enough to stage.

What the FY2026 annual report established, six days ago. Commercial remaining performance obligation of $678 billion, up 84% — more than two years of contracted revenue against $331.839B of annual sales, with a 2.3-year weighted duration and 30% recognisable within twelve months. Cloud revenue up 27% to $214.4B; the infrastructure line up 41%. Segment operating income up $26.7B or 21%, concentrated in the two cloud-facing segments. Adjusted EPS up 22.3% to $17.28. Return on equity 33.2%, return on invested capital 20.6%, gross margin 67.9%, operating margin at a record 46.8%. The vendor's own quality model rates it A−, the highest in this batch, and 82 covering analysts produce zero sell ratings.

What you are accepting, stated without softening. Free cash flow has fallen three years running — $74.071B, $71.611B, $66.987B — while net income rose from $88.136B to $133.749B. Capital expenditure is 34.9% of revenue, up from 13.3% three years ago. Gross margin has fallen in each of the last four quarters, from 69.0% to 67.2%. There is $329.1 billion of data-centre lease commitment that has not yet commenced, running to fiscal 2033. And Microsoft's own risk factors state that these investments "are in advance of fully developed revenue streams" and that "the associated revenue may not be realized in the expected timeframes or at expected levels." Our knowledge base spent the first half of 2026 arguing exactly this, and it was right about the stock for nine months.

Why Tactical rather than Core. Base fair value of $550 is 11.6% above spot and sits within 1.5% of a street consensus of $541.88 — we do not have a variant perception on the destination, only on the earnings basis, the balance-sheet composition, and the entry. RSI is 80.6 after a 19.1% three-month move, and the price is 22.5% above its 50-day average. Those two facts together mean the right action is to establish a position and hold capital back for a retracement, not to complete the purchase today. A staged entry is Buy — Tactical by definition.

Pre-registered KILL criteria — what would break the thesis:

  • A fourth consecutive annual decline in free cash flow. Three is a capital programme; four is a business model change.
  • Gross margin falling below ~66%. The trajectory is 69.0% → 68.0% → 67.6% → 67.2% over four quarters, and the FY2026 asset additions have barely begun depreciating.
  • Commercial remaining performance obligation declining in absolute terms, or its weighted duration shortening materially below 2.3 years — either would indicate the backlog is being consumed rather than replenished.
  • Intelligent Cloud segment operating income growth falling below revenue growth, which would mean the cloud business is buying revenue with margin.
  • A material increase in the not-yet-commenced lease disclosure beyond $329.1B without a corresponding increase in backlog — commitment outrunning demand is the precise failure mode.
  • A resumption of bond issuance at scale. Microsoft has been repaying debt ($3.0B and $8.962B net in the last two years); reversing that to fund capex would change the risk profile.

Pre-registered FLIP TO BUY — CORE (upsize) criteria:

  • A retracement to the $430-450 area (the 200-DMA is $433.35) with the backlog intact — this is the single most likely path to a full-size position and the reason capital is being held back.
  • Intelligent Cloud operating margin expanding for two consecutive quarters through the depreciation ramp, which would decisively falsify the malinvestment argument.
  • Free cash flow re-expanding above $74.071B, the FY2024 peak.
  • Capital expenditure plateauing as a share of revenue below the current 34.9%.
  • Commercial backlog above $700B with duration maintained.

The mega-cap platform sleeve, at a 2-3% target weight built in three tranches over 4-8 weeks — roughly one third now, one third on any move back toward the 200-DMA at $433.35, one third reserved. Microsoft and Alphabet (also covered 2026-08-04) are both mega-cap platforms funding very large infrastructure programmes, but they are not redundant and they resolve differently. Alphabet has risen 97.6% over twelve months, has stopped repurchasing stock, has issued $30.5B of equity and $19.0B of preferred, has negative free cash flow, and carries $85.2B of uncommenced leases — we rate it Hold at roughly 7% base upside. Microsoft is down 5.97% over twelve months, is still repaying bond debt, retains positive if declining free cash flow, and discloses $678B of contracted backlog against $329.1B of uncommenced leases — we rate it Buy — Tactical at 11.6% base upside. If only one mega-cap platform is held, on today's prices it should be this one. Logged as a tracked Synthos call (Buy — Tactical) as of 2026-08-04 at $492.81.

Single biggest risk: that the depreciation from the capital programme arrives faster than the revenue it was built for. Capital expenditure of $115.948B in one year, $329.1B of leases not yet commenced, gross margin already down 180 basis points in four quarters, and the company's own risk factors conceding that the spend is "in advance of fully developed revenue streams." The $678B backlog is the best available counter-evidence, but backlog is a promise of revenue, not of margin — a contract can be honoured at a price that does not cover the cost of the capacity built to serve it.

Most fragile assumption in the price: that operating margin holds near 45-47%. It was 48.9% two quarters into fiscal 2026 and 45.1% by the fourth quarter. Every element of the valuation — the 25.1x forward multiple, the consensus EPS path, the base case of $550 — assumes that slide arrests. If margin instead continues at the four-quarter rate, FY2028 earnings would fall materially short of the $23.254 consensus and the multiple would compress at the same time.
