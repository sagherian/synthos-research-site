The Overview

Cloudflare runs a huge global network that sits between websites and their visitors. It blocks attacks, speeds pages up, keeps sites online, and now increasingly runs small bits of computing right at the edge of the network, close to users. The exciting new idea is that as AI agents start doing things on people's behalf — buying, logging in, calling other services — all that traffic has to cross a network, and Cloudflare could charge for handling it. It is also well placed to let publishers charge AI companies for crawling their content.

The business is doing well. Revenue grew 33.5% in the most recent quarter, faster than a year ago. It now generates real spare cash — about $324 million last year — and its accounting losses are shrinking fast.

Two things should give you pause. First, the price: the stock costs about 44 times its annual sales — not profits, sales — which makes it the most expensive large company in this batch. Even the analysts covering it, 29 of whom rate it a Buy, have an average target of $257, about 9% below today's price. Second, the profitability of each sale is slipping: five quarters ago the company kept about 76 cents of gross profit on every revenue dollar; now it keeps about 71. Building out computing capacity to serve AI costs money, and it is showing up in the margin.

And on the AI story itself, the most recent expert we track who has looked at this specifically was measured, not excited: he said Cloudflare has a genuine path to carrying AI traffic, but today it carries well under 1% — possibly less than a tenth of a percent — of it. That is a real opportunity. It is not yet a real revenue line.

Earnings are in two days. That is not the moment to start buying something at 44 times sales.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, We don’t publish a reverse-DCF cross-check for pre-profit companies — negative or missing earnings break that math — so take this number on our modeling alone.

1. What they actually sell — and the segment disclosure is essentially nil

Cloudflare provides an integrated cloud platform spanning security (cloud firewalls, bot mitigation, DDoS protection, IoT security, SSL/TLS, rate limiting), performance (content delivery, intelligent routing, content/mobile/image optimisation), reliability (load balancing, its Anycast network, a virtual backbone, DNS and resolvers, waiting rooms), infrastructure on-ramps and filters (connecting users, devices and locations to the network, with data protection, inspection and access management), and developer services (serverless compute, programmable networks, site creation, domain registration). Founded and led by CEO Matthew Prince. 5,156 full-time employees — worth pausing on, because it means the company generates roughly $420,000 of revenue per employee, a striking figure for an infrastructure business.

The segment disclosure is the weakest of any name in this batch, and it must be stated plainly. The seg_prod block contains exactly one line:

SegmentFY25FY24
"Reportable Segment"$2,167.937M$1,669.626M

That is the entire product-level disclosure: one segment equal to total revenue. There is no breakout of security versus performance versus developer platform versus edge compute, and critically no separate disclosure of any AI, inference or agent-related revenue line. This is the single largest analytical hole in the name, and it matters more here than almost anywhere, because the entire premium multiple rests on an AI narrative that cannot be verified or sized from the company's own segment reporting. Any figure you see quoted for "Cloudflare's AI revenue" is not sourced from these filings.

3. The balance sheet — the leverage step-up nobody talks about

Balance sheet (2025-12-31):

  • Cash and short-term investments $4.101B; total debt $3.700B — of which $1.362B is classified as SHORT-TERM and $2.156B long-term.
  • A reconciliation note that matters: on a cash-and-short-term-investments basis the company is in a net cash position of roughly $0.401B. The data file's netDebt field reads $2.757B because it nets only pure cash equivalents against total debt. The enterprise value used throughout this dive ($102.997B) reflects the latter, more conservative convention. Both figures are disclosed; neither is smoothed away.
  • Total equity $1.459B; debt-to-equity 2.31x; financial leverage 4.04x; accumulated deficit -$1.205B.
  • Deferred revenue $684.2M; current ratio 1.96x.
  • Property, plant and equipment net $856.3M, up from $635.8M — the physical buildout is visible on the balance sheet.

The step-up is recent and large. Total debt went $1.435B (FY23) → $1.463B (FY24) → $3.700B (FY25) on $1.933B of net debt issuance in FY25 alone. For a company with $1.459B of equity and a $102.997B enterprise value, the absolute leverage is not dangerous — but the direction is, and it coincides exactly with the capex acceleration and the gross-margin compression. Those three facts are one fact.

Dilution, by contrast, is well controlled. Diluted shares: 333.7M (FY23) → 341.4M (FY24) → 348.4M (FY25) → 352.6M (Q1'26), roughly 2% a year, with no buyback programme at all (commonStockRepurchased is zero in FY25 and FY24). That is materially better discipline than Snowflake's, which needed $2.8B of repurchases to still increase its share count.

6b. The return bridge (why the multiple moves)

Expected return ≈ EPS growth + multiple drift + shareholder yield.

Shareholder yield is slightly negative — no dividend, no buyback, and ~2% annual dilution from stock compensation (352.6M diluted shares at Q1'26 versus 333.7M at FY23).

Revenue and earnings growth is the entire return, and it is strong: consensus revenue compounds ~28% and non-GAAP EPS goes from $1.198 to $1.587 (+32%) between FY26E and FY27E. Our base case assumes multiple COMPRESSION — from 36.7x FY26E EV/sales today to roughly 25.4x FY27E at our $250 base. We assume compression rather than a hold for three specific reasons, each of which we can name: (i) the gross-margin trend is going the wrong way, which lowers the quality of each revenue dollar being multiplied; (ii) the AI premium in the multiple is unsupported by any disclosed revenue line and is contradicted by the panel's most current quantification; (iii) the entire covering analyst group already has a target below the price, which historically bounds how much further a multiple runs without an estimate revision.

Note carefully what is NOT re-rating. The fall from 44.2x trailing to 28.7x FY27E at a constant price is mechanical rolldown — it happens automatically as revenue grows and is not evidence the stock gets cheaper. Only movement relative to the multiple the market is paying at each date counts, and on that basis our base case is a genuine de-rating of roughly 30%.

If your thesis requires the multiple to HOLD near 36x forward sales, that is your fragile leg — and it means you are underwriting the token-path revenue arriving inside the next 12-18 months.

  • We are more positive than the multiple implies on capital discipline. ~2% annual dilution with no buyback and only a $1.205B accumulated deficit is materially better stewardship than most of this cohort. It does not justify 44x sales, but it is a reason the bear case is $150 rather than $100.

7. Moat and competitive position

A real architectural moat, of an unusual kind. Cloudflare's advantage is not a product; it is placement. A globally distributed Anycast network sitting in front of a large share of internet traffic is extraordinarily expensive to replicate, and its value compounds with every additional point of presence and every additional customer whose traffic improves the threat-intelligence dataset. The security business is genuinely sticky (nobody rips out DDoS protection casually), and the developer platform creates switching costs at the code level.

The margin evidence is strong: 73.3% TTM gross margin on a business that operates physical infrastructure in hundreds of locations, and $420,000 of revenue per employee with just 5,156 people. Those are not the numbers of a commoditised CDN.

Where the moat is contested:

  • The edge-inference economics are unproven. Serving models at the edge requires expensive accelerators in many locations, which is precisely what the five-quarter gross-margin decline and the 15.8% capex ratio look like. A moat that costs you 4.7 points of gross margin to defend is a moat with a toll.
  • The hyperscalers can price against it. Every major cloud provider offers CDN, security and edge compute, and can bundle them below cost to defend a larger relationship.
  • The token path is aspirational. Per Gavin Baker (2026-06-15), current delivery is "under 1% — maybe under 10bps." A moat around a market you do not yet meaningfully serve is a moat around a plan.

8. Management, capital allocation and insider activity

Capital allocation is growth-first and debt-funded: no dividend, no buyback in any year of the available cash-flow data, $342.6M of capex (15.8% of revenue, up from 11.1%), $451.5M of stock-based compensation (20.8% of revenue), and $1.933B of net debt raised in FY25. R&D is $512.5M (23.5% of revenue) and SG&A $1.310B (59.1% of revenue) — a heavy go-to-market load, which is where the GAAP loss comes from. Founder-CEO Matthew Prince.

Cloudflare has reached $2.17B of revenue with a cumulative accumulated deficit of only $1.205B — it has burned remarkably little to get here.

9. Verdict, kill-criteria and flip conditions

Watch (do not initiate). Hold existing.

Cloudflare is an excellent company with a genuinely unusual structural position, and this dive should not be read as bearish on the business. Revenue growth accelerated across five quarters to +33.5%; free-cash-flow margin has improved three years running to 15.0%; the GAAP loss has narrowed to $22.9M in Q1'26; capital discipline is better than the cohort; Asia Pacific is growing 47.7%; and the structural case — that every platform shift still needs a network — is compelling.
