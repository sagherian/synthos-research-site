The Overview

Everpure sells the storage systems that big companies use to hold their data — the fast flash-memory kind, not spinning hard drives. The reason this matters right now is that AI systems are enormously data-hungry: the expensive processors sit idle unless data can be delivered to them fast enough. Everpure sells the part that keeps them fed.

The number that makes this interesting: last fiscal year the company grew revenue about 16%. In the quarter just reported, it grew 35% — more than double the rate, in one step. It also has $1.2 billion of revenue already booked and paid for that it has not yet recognised, which is up 23%. About seven of every ten revenue dollars are gross profit, and it holds $1.3 billion more cash than debt. That is a healthy company accelerating.

Two things argue for caution. First, on official accounting rules the company barely makes a profit — its operating margin is about 3% — because it pays employees heavily in stock, roughly 13 cents of every revenue dollar. When you see "27 times earnings" quoted for this company, that figure quietly excludes that cost. Second, and more concretely: in two days in July, the chief executive and the co-founder sold about $13 million of their own shares at prices essentially identical to today's. Nobody at the company bought any.

So: a real acceleration, a strong balance sheet, analysts overwhelmingly positive, and a fair value about 12% above today's price — against thin accounting profits and insiders selling into it. That combination argues for starting a position rather than completing one, and for letting the 26 August earnings report tell you whether one great quarter was the start of something.

  • Downside Risk 5/10. No solvency question; the risks are accounting quality and the insider tape.
  • Growth Quality 8/10. A documented acceleration to 35%, with 70% gross margins and growing deferred revenue behind it.
  • Exponential Potential 6/10. A genuine multi-year substitution theme; a $3.7B revenue base that compounds rather than doubles.

Putting a number on it: our fair-value estimate is $90 against a current price of $80.14 — real upside if our numbers are right.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, We don’t publish a reverse-DCF cross-check for pre-profit companies — negative or missing earnings break that math — so take this number on our modeling alone.

  • Every forward multiple in this dive — 32.9x FY27E, 26.7x FY28E — is a NON-GAAP multiple, computed on consensus EPS of $2.433 and $3.003 that exclude the stock-compensation charge.   • The mitigant is that the share count is not exploding. Diluted shares went 285.9M (FY22) → 339.2M (FY23) → 332.6M (FY24) → 342.7M (FY25) → 346.1M (FY26). That is +21% over four years against +68% revenue growth, and FY23-FY26 dilution is only +2.0% total. The buyback record is mixed (a $374.0M repurchase in FY25, but a positive $56.0M net stock issuance in FY26), so the dilution restraint is coming from grant discipline rather than from buying stock back.

Our position: the free cash flow is real and it is the number that matters, but the 27x FY28E headline is not comparable to a 27x multiple on a company that expenses its compensation in cash. We use the non-GAAP series because it is what the market prices, and we label it every time.

Two fields duplicating the year-ago quarter to the dollar is the vendor-corruption signature this house screens for. Conclusion: the FY27 Q1 share-count and interest-expense fields appear to be carried forward from the prior-year quarter and are not reliable. The reported GAAP EPS of $0.07 is arithmetically consistent with that share count ($24.078M ÷ 326.539M = $0.0737), which means the EPS figure inherits the same doubt.

What we did instead: revenue ($1,052.9M) and net income ($24.1M) from that quarter are used, because they reconcile independently — revenue matches the earnings-calendar actual to the dollar, and the non-GAAP EPS actual of $0.47 against a $0.398 consensus is a separate, corroborating series. No per-share arithmetic in this dive is built on the FY27 Q1 share count. Share-count-dependent work uses either the FY26 diluted count of 346.1M or the market-implied 332.4M (market cap ÷ price).

7a. What today's price assumes (the inversion)

At $80.14 — 32.9x FY27E and 26.7x FY28E non-GAAP earnings, 5.6x FY27E sales — the price embeds the following falsifiable claims:

  • Revenue reaches ~$4.49B in FY27 (+22.5%) and ~$5.16B in FY28 (+15.0%) (consensus, 15 analysts each). Note this is below the +35.2% just delivered — the price does not require the Q1 growth rate to persist; it requires roughly two-thirds of it. That is the most encouraging thing in this section.
  • Non-GAAP EPS reaches ~$3.00 by FY28 (consensus), i.e. 23% compound growth from the $1.99 FY26 base, which requires operating leverage on a cost base where R&D alone is 26.3% of revenue.
  • Gross margin holds above 70% (FY26: 70.4%; four-year range 67.5%-71.4%) — no price war with the larger storage vendors in the peer set (HPE $66.5B, NetApp $35.9B, Sandisk).
  • Stock compensation grows more slowly than revenue, so that the gap between $842M of consensus "net income" and GAAP profitability narrows rather than widens. If stock compensation stays at 13.2% of revenue, FY28 would carry a $680M charge — and the non-GAAP multiple would remain a fiction rather than a forecast.
  • The market keeps paying ~27-33x forward non-GAAP earnings — no de-rating, despite a trailing GAAP P/E of 118x and a 2.3% free-cash-flow yield.

The single most fragile assumption: that one quarter is a trend. The entire re-rating case rests on FY27 Q1's +35.2%, a single observation following a full year at +15.6% and, two years before that, a year at +2.8%. Consensus for the 2026-08-26 quarter implies +28.0% ($1.102B against $861.0M) — i.e. the street itself is modelling deceleration from Q1. If the actual comes in near +20%, the inflection narrative loses its evidence base and 33x forward has nothing holding it up.

7b. The return bridge (why the multiple moves)

Expected return ≈ EPS growth + multiple drift + shareholder yield.

  • EPS growth: consensus +22.3% (FY27E, $1.99 → $2.433) and +23.4% (FY28E, → $3.003). This is the dominant and most reliable term.
  • Multiple drift: our base case assumes modest COMPRESSION — we apply 30x to FY28E against the 32.9x the market pays on FY27E today. The rationale: the growth curve itself decelerates (from +22.5% to +15.0% to +12.5% on consensus), and multiples follow growth rates down. We explicitly do not count the mechanical roll from 32.9x FY27E to 26.7x FY28E at a constant price as compression — that is arithmetic, not re-rating. What we are assuming is that the market pays 30x at the horizon versus 33x today, a ~9% de-rating, offset by ~23% earnings growth.
  • Shareholder yield: approximately zero, and possibly negative. No dividend. FY26 showed a net stock issuance of +$56.0M rather than a buyback, after a $374.0M repurchase in FY25. There is no cash-return cushion here.

The bridge reads: ~23% earnings growth, less ~9% multiple compression, plus ~0% yield ≈ +12-14% over the horizon. That matches our base fair value of $90, and it is honest about where the return comes from — all of it is earnings growth. If earnings growth disappoints, there is nothing else in the bridge.

Scenario bands (stated arithmetic, no DCF):

  • Bear ~$54 — 22x FY27E non-GAAP EPS of $2.433. The Q1 acceleration proves to be a one-quarter comparison effect, growth reverts to the mid-teens, and the market applies a mature-hardware multiple. Roughly the 52-week low. -33% from spot.
  • Base ~$90 — 30x FY28E non-GAAP EPS of $3.003. Growth lands between the Q1 rate and the consensus curve, gross margin holds at 70%, the multiple compresses modestly with the growth rate. +12% from spot.
  • Bull ~$127 — 35x FY29E non-GAAP EPS of $3.619. The AI-infrastructure storage pull proves durable, revenue growth holds above 20% through FY29, and the market re-rates the name toward its AI-adjacent peers. +58% from spot. Base sits 12% above spot and 5% below the $94.38 street consensus. We are marginally more cautious than a sell-side that is 33-to-1 positive.

9. Moat and competitive position

The competitive position is a product-architecture and software moat rather than a scale moat. This company is materially smaller than several of its assigned peers — HPE at a $66.5B market cap, NetApp at $35.9B, Sandisk, Super Micro — and cannot win on purchasing power. What the financials say it wins on instead:

  • 70.4% gross margin in a hardware-adjacent business, versus what commodity storage economics would produce. That is a software-and-architecture margin.
  • 26.3% of revenue in R&D — a scaled, self-funded innovation base, and among the highest R&D intensities in this batch.
  • A Service line at 46.2% of revenue and growing 129% over four years, converting a hardware sale into a recurring relationship. This is the moat being built, visibly, in the disclosure.
  • $1.214B of deferred revenue growing 23.2% — customers committing cash ahead of delivery.

Where the moat is thin: there is no customer-concentration disclosure, so we cannot assess dependence on a handful of hyperscale buyers; the AI demand pull that is driving the acceleration is available to every vendor in the peer set, not just this one; and the FY24 result (+2.8% revenue growth, operating income down) demonstrates that when enterprise budgets pause, this company's moat does not prevent a flat year.

Assessment: a real but narrow moat — architecture and recurring service, inside a market where much larger competitors are chasing the same AI-driven demand. It supports a premium multiple; it does not support an unlimited one.

10. Verdict, kill-criteria and flip conditions

Stage-In. The operating case is documented and dated: revenue growth accelerated through four consecutive quarters to +35.2%, gross margin is 70.4%, R&D reinvestment is 26.3% of revenue, deferred revenue is up 23.2%, free cash flow is $615.7M, the balance sheet holds $1.331B of net cash, international revenue is compounding at more than twice the domestic rate, and the panel's highest-skill-weighted source has placed the business inside two named structural themes. Three specific reasons the position is staged rather than completed: (1) the inflection rests on one quarter, and consensus itself models deceleration to +28.0% for the quarter reporting on 26 August; (2) GAAP operating margin is 3.1% and the trailing GAAP P/E is 118x — the attractive multiples are all non-GAAP and exclude a charge equal to 13.2% of revenue; (3) the CEO and the co-founder sold ~$13.1M in two days last month at today's price, with no purchases anywhere in the record.

Staged entry — build a 2-3% position in three tranches:
