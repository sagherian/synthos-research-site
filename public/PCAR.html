The Overview

PACCAR builds heavy trucks — Kenworth and Peterbilt in North America, DAF in Europe. It also sells the spare parts for them, which is the more profitable half, and it lends truck buyers the money to buy them through its own finance arm.

Truck manufacturing is one of the most cyclical businesses there is, and PACCAR has just been through the down leg. Revenue fell 15.5% last year and profit fell 42.9%. Quarterly earnings per share went from $2.13 in mid-2024 to $0.96 in early 2025 — a 55% fall in three quarters.

It has now turned. Revenue bottomed at $6.23 billion in the first quarter of this year and jumped 21% to $7.55 billion in the second. Profit per share is back to $1.43. Encouragingly, the truck-and-parts side made 16.2% more pre-tax profit in the first half than a year earlier even though revenue was lower — which means margins are improving, not just volumes.

So why not buy it?

Because the share price has already risen 40.9% over the past year and now sits 1.6% below its highest point ever. At $135.96 you are paying about 19 times what analysts expect the company to earn in 2027. And here is the telling part: that is more than the analysts themselves think it is worth. Their average price target is $132.40 — below today's price. The median is $131. And 28 of the 45 analysts covering the stock rate it "hold", the most of any company in this batch.

There is one more number worth sitting with. Even in 2028 — three years out — analysts expect PACCAR to earn $7.83 a share. It earned $8.76 in 2023. So on consensus, this company will not get back to its previous peak earnings within the forecast horizon, and you are being asked to pay 19 times for the journey.

What is genuinely good: the balance sheet. PACCAR holds $9.5 billion of cash and securities, and — unusually — carries no goodwill and no intangible assets at all. Its book value is real machinery, inventory and loans, not the residue of acquisitions. It pays about 2% in dividends and has a long history of large one-off year-end payments on top.

Our estimate of fair value is $128, about 6% below today's price.

We considered calling this Avoid. We did not, because nothing about the business is going wrong — the cycle is turning up, margins are improving, and the balance sheet is excellent. The problem is entirely the price, and prices change.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, plus an independent, public-math cross-check further down the page (“Check our number”) so you can judge it for yourself.

Financial Services is stable and small. One structural note the payload does not make. PACCAR consolidates a manufacturer and a captive lender in one income statement, which is why interestExpense in the payload swings from $346.5 million in FY2025 to $31.1 million in FY2024 to zero in FY2022 and FY2020 — the field is not capturing a consistent concept. All interest and financing figures in this dive come from the filings, and the consolidated debt of $15,636.3 million should not be read as manufacturing leverage: most of it funds the finance receivables and lease portfolio.

First, consensus does not get back to the peak. FY2028's $7.826 is 10.7% below the $8.76 PACCAR earned in FY2023, and FY2029's $7.680 is lower still. So the sell side's own model says this cycle's next peak is below the last one, and the stock is 1.6% from its all-time high.

Second, the multiple is at the wrong end of the cycle. 19.2x FY2027 is being paid on recovering, not depressed, earnings. The classic cyclical discipline is to pay a high multiple on trough earnings and a low one on peak earnings; at $135.96 the market is paying 28.6x trailing and 19.2x a recovering forward number, which is neither.

3a. What today's price assumes (the inversion)

At $135.96 — 23.2x FY2026 consensus and 19.2x FY2027 — the price embeds:

  • Diluted EPS of $5.870 in FY2026, up 30.2%. (Consensus, 9 analysts.) The first half delivered $2.58 ($1.15 plus $1.43), leaving $3.29 for the second half — a 27.5% sequential-half increase. The September consensus of $1.56 implies a December quarter of $1.73, which would be the best quarter since June 2024. That is a demanding second half.
  • Diluted EPS of $7.088 in FY2027, up a further 20.8%, on revenue growth of 9.2%. (Consensus, 11 analysts.)
  • A Class 8 truck cycle that recovers on schedule. (Our derivation; the filings supply the trough.) This is the most fragile assumption in the price. PACCAR does not disclose order books or build rates in the extracted filings, so the only evidence for the recovery in this archive is one quarter of sequential revenue growth.
  • The market keeps paying 19-23x forward earnings for a heavy-truck manufacturer. (Our number.) At 15x FY2027E the stock is $106; at 22x it is $156.
  • That consensus is not too optimistic again. (Our derivation.) The FY2025 consensus overshot the actual by 11.6%. A repeat of that error on FY2027 would put the number at $6.27 and the stock at $120 on an unchanged multiple.

3b. The return bridge (why the multiple moves)

Expected return over the next twelve months decomposes as: EPS growth (+20.8%, from FY2026E $5.870 to FY2027E $7.088) + multiple drift (COMPRESSION, from 19.2x toward roughly 18.1x) + shareholder yield (+2.1%, dividend-led).

Our base assumes modest multiple compression and the reason is cyclical discipline rather than any deterioration. A cyclical whose earnings are recovering should see its multiple fall as the E rises — that is how the cycle works — and 19.2x on FY2027 is already above where this business has typically been valued on recovering earnings. The street agrees more strongly than we do: its consensus target implies 18.7x FY2027E and sits below the market.

If the multiple simply holds at 19.2x and consensus is met, the stock is $136 in a year plus a 2.1% dividend. The earnings growth is real and it is already in the price.

The arithmetic. At $135.96 PACCAR trades at 23.2x the FY2026 consensus of $5.870 and 19.2x FY2027's $7.088. Our base fair value of $128 is 5.9% below spot with 0.83:1 asymmetry and a 2.1% dividend. What is genuinely good. The cycle has turned — revenue bottomed at $6,234.3M in March and rebounded 21.1% to $7,546.7M in June, with earnings per share back to $1.43 from a $0.96 trough. Truck, Parts and Other pre-tax income rose 16.2% in the first half on lower revenue, which is margin expansion in a downturn and the highest-quality thing a cyclical management can deliver. The balance sheet is the strongest in this batch: $9,515.6 million of cash and marketable securities, and zero goodwill and zero intangible assets — so book value is real. And the dividend is $2,267.1 million a year, 3.2% of market capitalisation, with a long record of year-end specials on top.

Why not Avoid. Because nothing is deteriorating. Revenue is rising sequentially, margins are expanding, free cash flow is stable through the cycle, the balance sheet is unlevered on a manufacturing basis, and the dividend is covered nearly one and a half times by free cash flow. Avoid is for businesses that are getting worse; PACCAR is getting better and is merely expensive. A base 5.9% below spot is a full price, not a broken one.

Why not Watch. Because there is no pending disclosure that resolves anything: the 2026-10-27 print is an ordinary quarter in a cycle that will take years to play out, and the condition we want is a lower price rather than new information.
