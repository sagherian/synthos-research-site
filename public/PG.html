The Overview

Procter & Gamble makes the things in your bathroom and laundry cupboard: Pantene and Head & Shoulders shampoo, Olay skin cream, Gillette razors, Crest toothpaste and Oral-B brushes, Tide detergent, Pampers nappies and Bounty kitchen roll. It has been paying a dividend every year since 1890 and raising it every year since 1956 — 70 years in a row, which is one of the longest such records anywhere.

Its annual report was published this morning, and it was not a good year.

Sales rose 3%, to $87 billion. But the company itself explains that 2 percentage points of that came from favourable exchange rates and 1 point from raising prices. The number of items it actually sold did not grow at all. Strip out currency and acquisitions and the underlying growth was 1%.

Worse, profit went backwards. Operating profit fell 3%. And the company reports profit for each of its five divisions — beauty, razors, health care, laundry, and baby and family products. All five earned less money than the year before. Profitability got progressively worse as the year went on: the operating profit margin was 26.2% in the first quarter and 18.6% in the fourth.

It is also losing ground to competitors. The report discloses market share by category, and share fell in shampoo, skin cream, razors, laundry, nappies and — most sharply — American kitchen roll and tissue, where it lost 0.7 percentage points.

The company is responding by cutting costs: up to 7,000 office jobs are going by mid-2027, and headcount has already fallen 4% to about 104,000.

None of this threatens the dividend. P&G generated $15.8 billion of spare cash last year and paid out $10.2 billion in dividends — comfortable cover. The yield is 2.90%. The balance sheet is sound. This is not a company in trouble.

It is a company that is not growing. And at 21 times what analysts expect it to earn next year, you are paying a price that assumes it will start again. Our estimate of fair value is $155 against a price of $148.02 — about 5% of upside, plus the dividend.

One more thing worth saying plainly: we track more than fifty thousand recorded views from investors and analysts we follow, and only two of them mention Procter & Gamble at all. Both are more than three years old. We have no informational edge on this company. That, by itself, argues against a strong opinion.

  • Downside Risk 3/10. Beta 0.38, 70 years of dividend increases, 22.5x interest cover, 100% cash conversion.
  • Growth Quality 3/10. 1% organic growth, 0% volume growth, all five segments' earnings down.
  • Exponential Potential 1/10. Detergent, nappies and razors. By design, and by admission.

Putting a number on it: our fair-value estimate is $155 against a current price of $143.19 — real upside if our numbers are right.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, plus an independent, public-math cross-check further down the page (“Check our number”) so you can judge it for yourself.

Geography. The vendor supplies only a US versus non-US split, rounded to the nearest $100 million, and has no fiscal-2026 row: FY2025 was United States $41.6B and non-US $42.7B, a roughly even split. The filing's segment commentary works at the regional level (North America, Europe, Latin America, Greater China, Asia Pacific, India/Middle East/Africa) and the recurring pattern is instructive: growth in Latin America, Europe, Greater China and IMEA, attributed to innovation and market growth; declines in North America, attributed to competitive activity and market contraction. For a company with roughly half its sales in the United States, that is the wrong way round.

Headcount. The 10-K states "approximately 104,000 employees, a decrease of 4% versus the prior year due to the ongoing restructuring program." The vendor profile says 109,000. The filing wins; we use 104,000.

Leadership. The 10-K is signed by Shailesh Jejurikar as Chairman, President and Chief Executive Officer, and Andre Schulten as Chief Financial Officer. The filing notes Mr Jejurikar "previously served as Chief Operating Officer (2021-2025) and Chief Executive Officer — Fabric and Home Care (2019-2021)." This is the first annual report under a new chief executive, promoted from within — a fact worth registering when assessing whether the cost programme will be joined by portfolio change.

2. The growth decomposition — the single most important paragraph in the filing

> "Net sales increased 3% to $87.0 billion in fiscal 2026 driven by favorable foreign exchange of 2% and pricing of 1%. Unit volume and mix were unchanged versus the prior year." — FY2026 10-K, MD&A

Procter & Gamble sold no more product in fiscal 2026 than in fiscal 2025. The entire revenue increase came from currency translation and price.

> "Organic sales, which exclude the impact of acquisitions and divestitures and foreign exchange, increased 1%. Organic sales increased mid-single digits in Beauty and low single digits in Health Care, Grooming and Fabric & Home Care. Organic sales declined low single digits in Baby, Feminine & Family Care."

Against management's own published long-term objectives, disclosed in the same document:

This is stated not as a criticism of management but as the honest reading of a document management published this morning.

The productivity programme is working — 180 basis points of manufacturing savings is a large number — and it was still outrun. Unfavourable product mix alone cost 120 basis points, which is the arithmetic signature of consumers trading down.

On the cost line, the filing states: "Total SG&A increased 6% to $23.9 billion and increased 60 basis points to 27.5% as a percentage of net sales due primarily to an increase in marketing spending." Marketing spending rose 80 basis points as a share of sales; overhead costs were unchanged as "wage inflation headwinds and restructuring spending were offset by productivity savings"; and productivity delivered a further 160 basis points of SG&A benefit. P&G is spending more on marketing while losing share in nine categories — that combination is the definition of an expensive defence.

The conclusion in the filing's own words: "Operating income decreased $703 million, or 3%, to $19.7 billion... The operating margin decreased 160 basis points to 22.7% due primarily to the decrease in gross margin and increase in marketing spending."

Why net earnings still rose slightly. Net earnings increased $79 million to $16.1 billion, and the filing explains that this was "as the decrease in operating income was offset by higher non-operating restructuring charges in the prior year, primarily driven by the non-cash charge for accumulated foreign currency translation losses due to the substantial liquidation of operations in Argentina." Reported earnings grew only because the prior year contained an $801 million after-tax charge that did not repeat. The operating business earned less.

4. Two earnings numbers, and the balance sheet

Core versus GAAP. The 10-K states: "Diluted EPS increased 2% to $6.62 due to an increase in net earnings and a reduction in shares outstanding. Core EPS, which excludes the gain from the dissolution of the Glad joint venture business and incremental restructuring charges, increased 1% to $6.89."

The vendor payload carries both bases without labelling either — the same trap seen elsewhere in this batch. inc_a/inc_q are GAAP ($1.95 + $1.78 + $1.63 + $1.26 = $6.62); earn_cal is core ($1.99 + $1.88 + $1.59 + $1.43 = $6.89). The reconciliation is exact and the filing settles it. Consensus estimates are on the core basis: FY2026 epsAvg was $6.875 against a core actual of $6.89 — a fractional beat, and evidence that the estimate block is core, not GAAP. All forward multiples in this dive therefore run on the core basis.

6b. The return bridge (why the multiple moves)

Expected return over 12-24 months decomposes as: core EPS growth (+1.6% in FY2027, +5.8% in FY2028 on consensus) + multiple drift (we assume approximately FLAT, 21.2x to ~22x) + shareholder yield (~4.2%: a 2.90% dividend plus ~1.3% of net buyback).

We assume the multiple essentially holds, and we should be explicit that this is the least interesting return bridge in the batch — which is itself the point. There is no re-rating case to make: a business growing organic sales 1%, growing volume 0%, losing share in most categories and reporting lower earnings in all five segments does not obviously deserve to be paid more than 21x. Nor is there a strong compression case while the dividend record is intact and the balance sheet is sound, because the yield puts a floor under the price — at $126 the yield would be 3.4%, which is where income buyers historically appear.

Almost none of the expected return is re-rating, and unusually, almost none is earnings growth either. Most of it is the dividend. From $148.02, at an unchanged 21.2x and consensus FY2027 core EPS of $6.998, the price is $148 — flat. The base case of $155 implies 22.1x, roughly one turn of expansion, and delivers +4.7%. The bull case at $182 requires 26.0x FY2027E, roughly the multiple P&G commanded in its better years, and it needs organic growth to re-accelerate and margins to recover simultaneously. On today's evidence that is the fragile leg and it is not the central expectation.

11. Verdict, kill-criteria and flip conditions

Hold.

Procter & Gamble is a superb defensive asset having a genuinely poor operating year, priced as though the poor year is temporary. It might be. There is not enough margin of safety in the price to bet meaningfully either way.

What is genuinely excellent, and none of it is in doubt. 136 consecutive years of dividend payments and 70 consecutive years of increases — a record that exists nowhere else in this batch and almost nowhere in the market. A 2.90% yield costing $10.232 billion against $15.8 billion of adjusted free cash flow, giving 1.54x cover at a 62.3% core payout. 100% adjusted free-cash-flow productivity against a 90% target. Operating cash flow at a record $20.322 billion, up 14%. Return on equity of 29.8%, return on invested capital of 15.8%, interest coverage of 22.5x, capital expenditure of only 5.1% of revenue, a −43.1-day cash conversion cycle, and a beta of 0.38 — the lowest here.

What you are accepting, in the words of a filing published this morning. Net earnings declined in all five operating segments. Net sales rose 3% but "unit volume and mix were unchanged" and organic sales grew 1%. Operating income fell $703 million or 3%; operating margin fell 160 basis points to 22.7%, with a quarterly path of 26.2% → 24.2% → 21.5% → 18.6%. Fourth-quarter GAAP EPS of $1.26 was 14.9% below the prior year and revenue missed consensus. Market share fell in nine of fifteen disclosed categories, with the filing attributing the losses to "competitive activity" in North America and Greater China. Core EPS grew 1% against a stated objective of mid-to-high single digits — management missed two of its own three long-term goals. Up to 7,000 overhead roles are being removed. Pre-registered KILL criteria — what would move this to Avoid:

  • Operating margin below 18% for two consecutive quarters. The fourth quarter of fiscal 2026 printed 18.6%.
  • Continued share loss in Fabric & Home Care, which is 34.8% of sales and where segment share was unchanged but fabric care fell 0.4 points — this is the segment the company cannot afford to lose.

Pre-registered FLIP TO BUY (upsize) criteria:

  • A price near $138 (the 52-week low), giving a 3.11% yield — the level at which the income case becomes compelling on its own and at which we would add regardless of the growth picture.
  • Positive unit volume growth with organic sales above 3%. Volume was zero in fiscal 2026; its return would be the single cleanest signal that the franchise is intact.

  • Two or more segments returning to net earnings growth.
  • Evidence that the restructuring has delivered a structurally lower cost base — specifically, overhead costs falling as a percentage of net sales, which were merely "unchanged" in fiscal 2026 despite the programme.

Where PG fits in the Synthos Framework Portfolio. The low-beta defensive income sleeve, as a 1-2% holding for an income mandate and nothing at all for a growth mandate. The justification for the small size: the total expected return of roughly 7.6% is adequate but unexceptional; there is no conviction lane; and the house selection philosophy is explicitly to pick forward next-exponentials rather than trailing compounders — P&G is neither, it is a trailing annuity, and it should be sized as one. If the objective is defensive ballast with an inflation-linked income stream and a 0.38 beta, this is among the best instruments available. If the objective is return, it is not.

Single biggest risk: that 1% organic growth, zero volume growth and falling category share are the new normal rather than a bad year. The filing gives evidence for both readings. For "bad year": Beauty grew organic sales 5%, the productivity programme delivered 180 basis points of manufacturing savings, and the restructuring costs that hurt fiscal 2026 margins are explicitly temporary. For "new normal": share fell in nine of fifteen categories, the losses are concentrated in North America where roughly half the revenue sits, unfavourable product mix cost 120 basis points of gross margin (the signature of consumers trading down), and marketing spending rose 80 basis points while share fell anyway. We do not claim to know which it is, and the price does not pay us enough to guess.

Most fragile assumption in the price: that operating margin stabilises near 22-23%. It ended fiscal 2026 at 18.6% after four consecutive quarterly declines totalling 760 basis points. Every element of the valuation — the 21.2x forward multiple, the consensus core-EPS path to $7.404 in fiscal 2028, the base case of $155, and the dividend's 1.54x cover — assumes that slide arrests in the first half of fiscal 2027. The next print, on 2026-10-23, is the first test.
