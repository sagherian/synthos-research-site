The Overview

Prologis is the world's largest owner of warehouses. Not the retail kind — the big distribution buildings near motorways and ports where goods sit between the factory and your door. It owns about $99 billion of them directly and manages another $58 billion for other investors, taking a fee.

The business is simple and it is working. Last quarter it collected 11% more revenue than a year earlier, and the measure real-estate investors use — funds from operations, essentially cash profit before property depreciation — rose 11.6% per share. Its buildings are 95% full and it expects to collect about 7% more cash rent from the same buildings this year than last, because leases signed years ago at lower rents are being renewed at today's higher ones.

Its debt is modest by the standards of property companies: about 32% of asset value, against a limit of 60% it agreed with lenders, and its profits cover its interest bill seven times over.

Then, on the morning this was written, Prologis announced it is buying SEGRO, a large British warehouse company, for about £14 billion — roughly $18.7 billion. It will pay mostly in its own shares, which means existing shareholders will own about 9% to 11.5% less of the company than they did, plus up to £3.5 billion in cash borrowed under a loan that has to be repaid or refinanced within a year.

Shareholders reacted by selling: the price fell 3.5% on about three times the usual volume. That is a normal reaction when a company issues shares to buy something, and it is not by itself a verdict. But there is one detail worth noticing. The filing describes the offer as Prologis' "best and final proposal", which is language used when a bidder has been pushed to raise its price.

The shares cost $139.05. Management expects the company to produce between $6.22 and $6.30 of funds from operations per share this year, so you are paying about 22 times. The dividend is $4.28 a year, a 3.1% yield, and it costs about 68% of that cash flow.

Our estimate of fair value is $150, about 8% above the price. The average analyst says $153.

Eight percent of upside, on the day a company announces the largest acquisition in this research batch, with no way yet to judge what it bought or what it paid, is not enough to act on.

One warning about the data. The automated feed on this company is unusually poor. It reports the company as having no goodwill and no intangible assets at all, which the filings contradict; its geographic table for 2025 contains segment names instead of countries; and its earnings forecasts come from as few as one analyst and are on an accounting basis nobody uses to value property companies. Everything operational in this analysis comes from the company's own filings.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, plus an independent, public-math cross-check further down the page (“Check our number”) so you can judge it for yourself.

1. The SEGRO transaction — announced today, absent from the payload

From the 8-K filed 2026-08-04, verbatim:

> "On August 4, 2026, Prologis, Inc. issued an announcement (the 'Rule 2.7 Announcement'), pursuant to Rule 2.7 of the United Kingdom City Code on Takeovers and Mergers disclosing the terms of a recommended offer by the Company to acquire the entire issued and to be issued share capital of SEGRO plc."

The consideration:

> "SEGRO shareholders will receive 0.0920 shares of common stock of the Company, par value $0.01 per share, for each SEGRO ordinary share. SEGRO shareholders may elect to receive cash in lieu of some or all of their New Prologis Shares consideration, subject to the terms of the partial cash alternative. The maximum aggregate amount of cash available under the partial cash alternative is approximately £3.5 billion. Each SEGRO shareholder's basic entitlement under the partial cash alternative is equal to 25% of the fixed price of 1,031.7 pence per SEGRO ordinary share. Accordingly, a SEGRO shareholder electing to receive only its basic entitlement would receive 258 pence in cash and 0.0690 New Prologis Shares for each SEGRO ordinary share."

The valuation and the dilution:

> "...based on Prologis' closing share price of $149.94 and a GBP:USD exchange rate of 1.3371 on July 21, 2026 (the last practicable date prior to the announcement of Prologis' best and final proposal on July 22, 2026) and assuming that the partial cash alternative is fully taken up, the Combination values each SEGRO ordinary share at 1,031.7 pence and the entire issued and to be issued ordinary share capital of SEGRO at approximately £14.0 billion. Assuming the partial cash alternative is fully taken up... SEGRO shareholders would hold approximately 8.9% of the combined group following the closing of the Combination. If no elections are made for the partial cash alternative, SEGRO shareholders would hold approximately 11.5%."

The financing:

> "On August 4, 2026, and in connection with the Combination, Prologis, L.P. entered into a Term Loan Credit Agreement with various lenders and JPMorgan Chase Bank, N.A., as Administrative Agent... the Borrowers may obtain loans in connection with the consummation of the Combination in pounds sterling or Euro not exceeding £3,575,000,000. The Term Loan Facility is scheduled to mature one year after the initial borrowing... but the Operating Partnership may, at its option and subject to payment of an extension fee, extend the Initial Maturity Date for one year on two occasions... As of the effective date of the Term Loan Facility, the spread was 70 basis points."

Five observations, and the fifth is the one to hold onto.

First, the size. At the disclosed exchange rate of 1.3371, £14.0 billion is approximately $18.7 billion — the largest transaction in this entire batch, larger than Danaher's $9.8 billion Masimo purchase, Capital One's $4.5 billion Brex acquisition and Lockheed's $3.45 billion Ultra Maritime deal combined.

Second, the currency of payment is equity, and the dilution is large. 8.9% to 11.5% of the combined company goes to SEGRO holders. The spread between the two figures is the cash election: full take-up of the £3.5 billion alternative reduces share issuance, so a high cash election means less dilution and more debt.

Third, the debt is short. A £3,575,000,000 term loan maturing in one year, extendable twice at the company's option for a fee, at 70 basis points over benchmark. That is a bridge, not permanent capital, and it will have to be refinanced into bonds or equity within one to three years. At a 32.2% leverage ratio Prologis has room to absorb it, but the refinancing terms are a live variable.

Fourth, the acquirer's reaction was ordinary. The stock fell 3.54% on 15.90 million shares. A modest decline on an equity-funded acquisition is the standard market response and carries no verdict.

Fifth — and this is the detail that deserves weight — the filing describes the terms as following "Prologis' best and final proposal on July 22, 2026." "Best and final" is City Code language used when a bidder commits not to raise its offer, and it is characteristically deployed after a process in which the price has already moved. We cannot assess from this archive whether Prologis paid a full price, and we say so: there is no SEGRO financial information anywhere in the filings we hold, no disclosed synergy figure, no pro forma balance sheet and no accretion arithmetic. That is the principal reason this dive ends at Watch rather than at a higher tier.
