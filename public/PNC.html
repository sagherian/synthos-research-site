The Overview

PNC is a big American bank — the sixth largest — headquartered in Pittsburgh. It takes deposits, makes loans, manages money for wealthy clients and provides treasury services to companies. There is nothing exotic about it.

Last quarter was unusually good. Revenue rose 21% compared with a year earlier, which for a bank of this size is a lot, and profit per share rose 26%. It has beaten analysts' forecasts five quarters in a row, by an average of 9%.

Here is the honest problem with this report. We cannot tell you why. Banks file a detailed quarterly report with the regulator, and PNC's for the June quarter has not been filed yet. The press release that would have explained the numbers exists, but it was attached to a filing as a separate document that our archive did not capture. So we can see that revenue grew 21% and we cannot see the loan losses, the capital cushion, the interest margin or the deposit balances that would tell us whether that growth was healthy. For a bank, those are the numbers that matter most. We say so rather than guessing.

On price: the shares cost $254.14, which is within half a percent of their highest price in the past year, after rising 37% over twelve months. That is about 12 times what analysts expect PNC to earn in 2027 and about 2.2 times the tangible book value of the company — a fair, ordinary price for a good bank. Forty-six analysts follow it and they are almost exactly split: 23 say Buy, 22 say Hold, one says Sell. Their average target of $279 is 10% above the price.

The dividend is $7.10 a share, a 2.8% yield, and it takes up less than a third of profits, so it is well covered.

Our estimate of fair value is $270, about 6% above the price. That is a Hold: a good bank, fairly priced, bought at its high, with the most important risk disclosures temporarily unavailable.

  • Downside Risk 5/10. Well capitalised and diversified — and we cannot currently see the loan book.
  • Growth Quality 6/10. Real acceleration, five straight beats, and no explanation in the file.
  • Exponential Potential 2/10. A commercial bank. The score would be the same for any of its peers.

Putting a number on it: our fair-value estimate is $270 against a current price of $254.14 — real upside if our numbers are right.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, plus an independent, public-math cross-check further down the page (“Check our number”) so you can judge it for yourself.

This dive has a materially less complete evidence base than any other in this batch, and the reason should be on the first page rather than buried in a data-integrity note.

  • 10-Q filed 2026-05-05 (March 2026 quarter — the most recent complete financial statements available)
  • 8-K filed 2026-07-15 (Item 2.02, second-quarter results — Exhibits 99.1, the press release, and 99.2, the Financial Supplement, are FURNISHED and were not extracted)

What that means in practice. For the June 2026 quarter, the only figures available anywhere are those in the vendor's inc_q and earn_cal blocks: revenue, net income, earnings per share and share count. There is no common equity Tier 1 ratio, no net interest margin, no net charge-off rate, no allowance for credit losses, no allowance coverage ratio, no deposit balance, no loan balance and no segment result for the period. For a bank, those are precisely the numbers that determine risk.

This dive therefore does two things. It values PNC on the earnings and book-value data that ARE available and states the multiple with normal confidence. And it declines to make any statement whatever about credit quality, capital adequacy or funding, because it has no basis for one — the short-horizon and medium-horizon confidence ratings in the front matter are Low for this reason as much as any other, and the largest single item in the watch lists is the September 10-Q itself.
