The Overview

America is trying to build a lot of electrical infrastructure very quickly — new power lines, new power plants, and enormous buildings full of computers. Someone has to physically do that work: run the cable, weld the steel, put up the towers. Quanta Services is the largest company in America that does it, with about 69,500 employees.

Business is extraordinary. Revenue last quarter was up 41%. Profit per share, on the company's preferred measure, was up 71%. The company has $53.4 billion of work already signed — nearly two years of revenue at the new, much higher run rate. And five days ago it told investors that this year's revenue will be $39.3 to $39.7 billion, up about 39% from last year.

Here is something unusual and worth understanding. The analysts who publish forecasts have not caught up. Their average estimate for this year's profit is $15.55 per share, and even the most optimistic of them says $15.96 — while the company itself says it will earn between $16.45 and $16.95. Every single analyst estimate is below the bottom of the company's own guidance. That gap will close, and it will close upward.

So why not buy it?

Two reasons.

The price. The shares cost $693. Against the company's own profit guidance, that is about 41 times earnings. Against last year's actual accounting profit it is 79 times. Most companies in this research batch trade between 15 and 23 times.

How the growth is being made. Quanta has bought eleven companies in the last eighteen months, four of them in the last three months alone. Buying companies means paying more than the assets are worth, and the excess sits on the balance sheet as "goodwill". Quanta now has so much of it that if you subtract goodwill from shareholders' equity, the answer is negative $8.94 per share. It also means constant borrowing: the day before this report, the company sold $2 billion of new bonds.

None of that is wrong. It is how a company grows this fast. But it means that if the extraordinary growth slows even to merely good, there is a long way to fall — and the shares are already 11.7% below their high and down 8.5% over the last three months.

Our estimate of fair value is $740, about 7% above the price. The analysts average $731. That is not enough for the risk being taken.

  • Exponential Potential 6/10. Highest in this batch. Quanta may be buying a scarce resource — skilled electrical labour — rather than just winning contracts.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, plus an independent, public-math cross-check further down the page (“Check our number”) so you can judge it for yourself.

6b. The return bridge (why the multiple moves)

Expected return over the next twelve months decomposes as: adjusted EPS growth (roughly +17%, from a guided FY2026 $16.70 to a guidance-consistent FY2027 near $19.50) + multiple drift (COMPRESSION of roughly 9%, from today's 41.5x on the current year toward about 38x on the then-current year) + shareholder yield (~0.1%) ≈ +7%.

Our base assumes the multiple compresses modestly, and the reason is arithmetic rather than a view about the business. A 41.5x multiple on a company growing adjusted EPS 57% this year is defensible; the same multiple on a company growing 17% next year is not. As the acquisition-driven step-change annualises, the growth rate normalises toward the high teens, and high-teens growers do not sustain low-forties multiples indefinitely. The base case therefore lets the earnings do the work and gives back a small amount of the multiple.

Note what this makes the return: essentially all earnings growth, with the multiple a modest drag and the dividend — $0.43 a share, a 0.06% yield — a rounding error. There is no shareholder yield to cushion a de-rating here, and the $1.0 billion repurchase authorisation, if used, would be 1.0% of the market capitalisation.

The sensitivity is the whole story and it is brutal in both directions. If the multiple held at 41.5x a guidance-consistent FY2027 of $19.50, the price would be $809 (+16.7%). If it compressed to 30x, $585 (−15.6%). If it compressed to 27x — the FY2027 consensus multiple this stock would carry at the low end of a normal high-growth industrial band — $527 (−24.0%).
