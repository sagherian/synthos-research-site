The Overview

Roblox runs a platform where users — mostly young ones — build and play three-dimensional games and experiences made by other users. It makes money selling a virtual currency that players spend inside those experiences, sharing the proceeds with the creators.

The business, as reported, is doing well. Revenue grew about 36% in the most recent quarter. It loses money on paper, but the loss is shrinking fast — down from $322 million to $229 million in a year, while revenue grew a third. More importantly, it generates real cash: about $1.35 billion of free cash flow last year, more than double the year before. It has more cash than debt, and it holds $4.2 billion of money customers have already paid but that has not yet been counted as revenue.

And yet the stock has fallen 74% from its high, and sits within 3% of its lowest point in a year.

That gap is the whole issue. When the reported numbers get better and the share price falls by three-quarters, one of two things is happening: either the market has made a mistake, or the market is reacting to something that does not appear in the financial statements. We cannot tell which from the data we have. There is no news, no regulatory information, no competitive analysis in this dataset — only the numbers, and the numbers look good.

What we can say precisely: at today's price you are paying roughly 18 times last year's free cash flow for a business growing over 35%, with more cash than debt. If nothing is fundamentally broken, that is cheap. If something is broken and we cannot see it, it is not.

Our answer is to buy a small position rather than a full one — enough to participate if this is a mistake, small enough that being wrong about an invisible risk does not matter much.

  • Downside Risk 7/10. The balance sheet is fine. The unexplained 74% decline is not.
  • Growth Quality 8/10. 36% growth with margins improving 14 points in a year and prepayments growing faster than revenue.
  • Exponential Potential 7/10. A genuinely compounding data asset — 13 billion hours a month of 3D interaction data.

Putting a number on it: our fair-value estimate is $43 against a current price of $38.44 — real upside if our numbers are right.

1. Data integrity — three problems in this file, and they must come first

This is one of the more compromised files in the batch and the reader needs the caveats before the analysis, not after.

(a) REJECTED — the entire consensus EBITDA series. The estimate block reports negative ebitdaAvg in every forecast year: -$1.771B (FY26E), -$2.032B (FY27E), -$2.396B (FY28E), -$3.028B (FY29E), -$3.389B (FY30E) — a figure that grows more negative every year while the same rows show netIncomeAvg turning positive at +$179.3M in FY30. A company cannot have EBITDA of -$3.39B and net income of +$179M in the same year. The series is internally impossible. No EBITDA-based multiple appears anywhere in this dive. This same failure mode appears in several other files in this batch and looks systematic rather than company-specific.

(b) FLAGGED — the estimate revenue series is on a DIFFERENT BASIS from reported GAAP revenue, and the earnings calendar switches between them mid-series. This is subtle and it invalidates the most obvious comparison an investor would make.

  • Consensus FY25 revenue was $6.616B. Actual FY25 GAAP revenue was $4.891B. The estimate exceeds the actual by 35%.
  • Consensus FY26 revenue is $7.156B. Actual first-half 2026 GAAP revenue is $2.911B ($1.442B + $1.469B), which annualises to roughly $6.0B. The estimate again sits far above the GAAP run-rate.
  • The earnings-calendar revenueActual field switches basis mid-series. For 2025-07-31 it reports $1,080,677,000 and for 2025-10-30 $1,359,646,000 — matching GAAP quarterly revenue to the dollar. But for 2026-02-05 it reports $2,222,275,000 against GAAP revenue of $1,415,021,000; for 2026-04-30 $1,731,000,000 against $1,442,000,000; and for 2026-07-30 $1,557,000,000 against $1,469,000,000. The last three do not match GAAP at all.

The most likely explanation is that the estimate and recent-calendar series are on a bookings basis — the gross cash value of virtual-currency purchases, which for this business model is recognised as revenue over an extended period and therefore substantially exceeds GAAP revenue. That is a standard metric for this company type. But whatever the label, the practical consequence is unambiguous: do not compare consensus revenue to reported revenue, and do not compute EV/sales using the estimate series. Every revenue multiple in this dive uses reported GAAP revenue and says so.

(c) FLAGGED — segment and geographic disclosure are entirely absent. seg_prod and seg_geo are both empty. For a platform business whose economics depend on regional mix, age-cohort mix and the split between platform revenue and advertising, this is a substantial gap. We have no geographic revenue split, no user metrics, no daily-active-user figure, no bookings-per-user, no age-demographic disclosure. For a company where the bear case most plausibly involves regulation or user safety in specific jurisdictions, the absence of any geographic data is exactly the wrong gap to have.

(d) DO NOT MISREAD — the leverage and book-value ratios. The vendor reports debtToEquityRatioTTM of 12.08, priceToBookRatioTTM of 172.9 and currentRatioTTM of 0.82. Read naively these describe a distressed, over-levered company. They describe nothing of the kind. Shareholders' equity is only $394.5M because the balance sheet carries a -$5.061B accumulated deficit and $4.169B of deferred revenue sitting in current liabilities. Recomputing by hand from the balance sheet: cash $1.205B + short-term investments $1.850B − total debt $1.803B = $1.252B of NET CASH. The current ratio of 0.82 is caused by deferred revenue — an obligation to deliver a service, not to pay cash — being classified as a current liability. This company has no leverage problem. We flag this specifically because these are exactly the ratios a screen would flag as distress.

CHECKED AND CLEAN — the most recent quarter. inc_q[0] (Q2 2026, 2026-06-30) passes the standard screen: net income -$183M against 716.767M shares gives -$0.255 versus the reported -$0.26; share counts do not duplicate the prior quarter (716.767M against 711.697M) and continue a steady upward trend; interest expense of $10M is consistent with every prior quarter; the reported EPS matches the earnings-calendar actual of -$0.26 exactly. The quarter is usable.

4. The drawdown — what we can and cannot say

The facts: $36.67 against a 52-week high of $141.56 — -74.1%. Twelve-month return -73.4% against SPY's +19.9% and QQQ's +23.9%, a 93-point relative deficit. Three-month return -18.7%. The stock is 3.0% above its 52-week low of $35.60. RSI 23.4. MACD -2.42. Both moving averages are falling and price is 47% below the 200-DMA.

A minor data inconsistency: the profile block reports a 52-week range of $33.88-$142.00 while the technical block reports $35.60-$141.56. The conclusion is unaffected.

What this file does NOT contain, and what we therefore cannot assess: any news, regulatory filing, safety or child-protection development, litigation, competitive entry, management guidance, user metric, or analyst commentary. There is no field in this dataset that explains a 74% decline.

What we can rule out from the data available:

  • Not a revenue break. Revenue grew 39.3% and 35.9% in the two most recent quarters.
  • Not a margin break. Operating margin improved 14.3 points year over year in the most recent quarter.
  • Not a cash break. Free cash flow more than doubled in FY25 and the balance sheet holds net cash.
  • Not an earnings miss. The last four prints all beat consensus, the most recent by 24%.
  • Not a demand break at the customer level. Deferred revenue grew 38.7%, faster than revenue.
  • Not, principally, a sell-side capitulation. The consensus target remains $49.47, 34.9% above spot, with 16 buy ratings — though the rating has moved to Hold (18 holds, 3 sells), so there has been partial capitulation.

What remains as candidate explanations, none verifiable here: a regulatory or child-safety development (the most commonly cited structural risk for this business model, and one that would not appear in any financial field); a guidance cut for periods beyond the estimate horizon in this file; a change in the bookings-to-revenue relationship; a broad de-rating of high-multiple growth names; or forced selling.

The intellectually honest position: we are looking at a large, quantifiable dislocation between price and reported operating performance, and we do not know its cause. That is a reason to take a position, because the arithmetic is compelling. It is equally a reason to take a small one, because an unexplained 74% decline usually has an explanation. Every element of the verdict — Stage-In rather than Buy, 1.5-2.5% rather than 3-5%, three tranches rather than one — follows from that single sentence.

6a. What today's price assumes (the inversion)

At $36.67 — approximately 18.4x trailing free cash flow and 4.39x trailing GAAP revenue — the price embeds the following falsifiable claims:

  • Free cash flow does not grow materially from the FY25 $1.353B base. An 18.4x multiple on a business growing revenue 36% is what the market pays for a cash flow it expects to stall. If free cash flow simply grows in line with revenue, the forward multiple is roughly 13x — which is a value multiple for a growth asset.
  • GAAP losses persist through FY29 (consensus: -$1.45, -$1.15, -$0.93, -$0.42 per share), i.e. the 14.3-point annual margin improvement just delivered does not continue at anything like that rate. This is the assumption most directly contradicted by the last reported quarter.
  • Revenue growth decelerates from 36% to roughly 15% by FY28 (consensus, on the mixed basis: +14.7% FY27, +18.0% FY28) — a halving of the current rate within two years.
  • Dilution continues at roughly 5% annually with no buyback, despite $1.25B of net cash and a share price 74% off its high.
  • Whatever caused the 74% decline is permanent and material. This is the assumption we cannot test, and it is doing the most work in the price.

The single most fragile assumption: that the operating-margin improvement stops. Q2 2026 delivered a 14.3-point year-over-year margin gain and a $93.5M absolute reduction in operating loss on 36% revenue growth. Consensus models losses persisting for four more years. One of those two things is wrong, and the reported quarter is the one with evidence behind it.

6b. The return bridge (why the multiple moves)

Expected return ≈ free-cash-flow growth + multiple drift + shareholder yield. We use free cash flow rather than EPS because there are no earnings.

  • Free-cash-flow growth: FY25 delivered $1.353B against $641.3M in FY24 — a 111% increase. Even assuming that pace halves and then halves again, free cash flow above $1.6B within the horizon is a modest assumption given 36% revenue growth and a narrowing operating loss.
  • Multiple drift: our base case assumes modest EXPANSION, from ~18.4x to ~22x free cash flow, and we flag this as the fragile leg of the bridge. The justification: 18.4x for a 36% grower with net cash and improving unit economics is a distressed multiple, and even partial normalisation of sentiment moves it. But a thesis that requires multiple expansion is inherently weaker than one that does not, and we say so explicitly rather than burying it. If the multiple simply holds at 18.4x and free cash flow grows 20%, fair value is approximately $38 — barely above spot. The base case needs both legs.
  • Shareholder yield: NEGATIVE, approximately -5%. No dividend, no buyback, and roughly 5% annual dilution. This is a real and under-discussed drag — the only name in this batch where the shareholder yield subtracts from returns rather than adding to them.

The bridge reads: solid free-cash-flow growth, plus assumed multiple normalisation, less ~5% annual dilution. Net approximately +18% to our base case. The dilution term is why the base case is $43 rather than $46.

7. Moat and competitive position

The moat has three layers, and the knowledge base describes all three.

The creator economy. Roblox does not make the content; users do, and they are paid for it. The knowledge base records that top creators earn tens of millions of dollars annually, and — the more important detail — that long-tail creator revenue is growing faster than the top creator's. A creator ecosystem where the tail grows faster than the head is a healthy one: it means opportunity is widening rather than concentrating, which is what keeps new creators arriving. This is a two-sided network that a competitor cannot buy.

The data asset. The strongest claim in the lane: 13 billion hours per month of three-dimensional vector interaction data — scene geometry rather than rendered video, reproducible from any camera angle. For training world models and non-player-character behaviour that is a materially more useful corpus than video, because it contains the underlying structure rather than a projection of it. No competitor has an equivalent, and the asset compounds with usage.

Switching costs. Users' social graphs, purchased items, creations and reputations live on the platform. For the core cohort these are not transferable.

Where the moat is weakest. The business is concentrated in a demographic that ages out, so cohort replacement is a permanent requirement. The 78.1% gross margin is struck after creator payouts, so the economics depend on a revenue-share that competitors could bid against. And — the honest gap — with no user metrics, no geographic split and no age-cohort disclosure in this file, we cannot verify that the moat is holding. We are inferring platform health from revenue growth and deferred revenue, which are good proxies but not direct evidence.

The vendor's assigned peer set (CoreWeave, Electronic Arts, Fortinet, Corning, Infosys, Motorola Solutions, Strategy, NetEase, TE Connectivity, Take-Two) is a market-capitalisation cohort rather than a competitive set. The only genuinely comparable names in it are Electronic Arts ($52.98B) and Take-Two ($45.84B) — both traditional publishers with fundamentally different economics, and both carrying market capitalisations roughly twice Roblox's on materially slower growth.
