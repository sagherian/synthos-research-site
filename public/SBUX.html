The Overview

Starbucks sells coffee. About two years ago it was in trouble: customers were waiting too long, the menu had grown unwieldy, prices had risen faster than the experience improved, and people stopped coming. A new chief executive, Brian Niccol, arrived and started a programme called "Back to Starbucks" — simplify, speed up, restore service, invest in staff hours before marketing.

In the three months to June, it worked. Sales at stores open more than a year rose 7.9% — and more importantly, the number of customer visits rose 4.2%. That distinction matters enormously. A company can raise its comparable sales just by charging more; a company whose customer count is rising is genuinely being chosen more often. For contrast, Lowe's — also in this batch — had comparable sales up 0.6% with customer transactions falling 0.9%.

The second big thing happened on 30 March: Starbucks sold 60% of its Chinese business to a local investor, Boyu Capital, valuing it at about $4 billion. Starbucks received $3.1 billion in cash, kept a 40% stake worth about $1.2 billion, and no longer runs the shops — it licenses the brand and collects a fee. This is why the company's reported revenue actually fell 1% in the quarter even though sales at existing stores rose nearly 8%: about $776 million of Chinese store revenue simply moved off the books. Starbucks used the money to pay down debt, from $14.6 billion to $11.8 billion.

None of that appears in the standard financial data we buy — the most recent balance sheet in it is from September 2025, six months before the sale.

Now the problems, and they are about money rather than operations.

Starbucks pays a dividend of $2.47 a share. Last year it earned $1.63 and this year is tracking about $2.43 on an adjusted basis. The dividend costs more than the company earns, and in the year to September 2025 it cost $2.77 billion against $2.44 billion of spare cash — so it was partly funded by borrowing. The share buyback, which was $4 billion three years ago, is now exactly zero. And because Starbucks has bought back so much stock over the years, its accounting net worth is negative $7.7 billion.

The shares cost $104.97, which is 3% below their highest price of the last year and about 33 times what analysts expect the company to earn in the year to September 2027. Analysts are perfectly split — 28 say buy, 28 say hold. Our estimate of fair value is $112, about 7% above the price, and slightly below the average analyst's $115.

  • Growth Quality 6/10. Outstanding comparable sales; declining reported revenue; growth in profit comes from repairing margins, not selling more.
  • Exponential Potential 3/10. A very large, very mature coffee retailer.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, plus an independent, public-math cross-check further down the page (“Check our number”) so you can judge it for yourself.

> "During the first and second quarters of fiscal 2026, we classified the assets and liabilities of the disposal group as held for sale ... which required us to cease property, plant, and equipment depreciation and operating lease right-of-use asset amortization of the related long-lived assets, resulting in reduced depreciation and amortization and store operating expenses. That last sentence explains the December 2025 quarter's bizarre tax charge of $471.6 million on pre-tax income of $764.8 million — a 61.7% effective rate — and the September 2025 quarter's $700.6 million on $833.7 million, an 84.0% rate. A reader looking at Starbucks' reported net income across fiscal 2026 without the filing would see a company whose earnings collapsed and then recovered for no visible reason.

Why this matters to every growth rate in the file. The 10-Q states: "consolidated net revenues decreased 1% to $9.3 billion compared to $9.5 billion in the third quarter of fiscal 2025, primarily due to the conversion of Starbucks retail operations in China to our licensed joint venture model." This was partially offset by a 7.9% increase in comparable store sales ($524 million)."

So the June quarter decomposes as: minus $776 million of China conversion, minus $52 million of currency, plus $524 million of comparable sales, plus licensed-store and other revenue. Reported revenue fell 1% while the underlying business grew nearly 8%. No vendor field reflects any of this.

2. The comparable-store number, which is the whole operating case

From the 10-Q filed 2026-07-29:

> "Starbucks results for the third quarter of fiscal 2026 showed continued progress and momentum on key 'Back to Starbucks' initiatives ... global comparable store sales growth, consolidated operating margin expansion, and improved customer engagement. These initiatives included the Green Apron Service standard to improve operational consistency ..., engaging consumer marketing, disciplined menu innovation, a redesigned Starbucks Rewards program, and coffeehouse uplifts ... The decline in consolidated revenues was offset by a 7.9% increase in global comparable store sales, driven by a 7.9% increase in the U.S. market ... the increase in comparable store sales was driven by a 4.2% increase in comparable transactions and a 3.6% increase in average ticket, primarily driven by higher delivery sales and strength in customer food attach and beverage modifications. Consolidated operating margin expanded 60 basis points from the prior year to 10.5%, primarily driven by sales leverage and lower inflation paired with tariff refunds, offset by higher restructuring costs and labor investments largely in support of 'Back to Starbucks.'"

Three things in that passage deserve isolating.

First, transactions are up 4.2%. In restaurant retail this is the number that cannot be faked. A comparable-sales figure driven by price tells you the company raised prices; a comparable-sales figure driven by transactions tells you more people chose to come. Starbucks got 4.2 points of its 7.9 from customer counts. In this batch, Lowe's comped +0.6% with transactions DOWN 0.9%. The contrast is the single sharpest operating comparison available across these twelve names.

Second, the margin expanded despite the investment, not because it stopped. The 10-Q says expansion came from sales leverage "offset by higher restructuring costs and labor investments." That is the favourable order of events: leverage is already outrunning the cost of the turnaround while the turnaround is still being paid for.

Third, "tariff refunds" is in that sentence and is a one-off. It is not quantified, and a reader should not extrapolate the full 60 basis points.

10. Verdict, kill-criteria and flip conditions

Watch.

What is genuinely good, and this is the strongest operating evidence in the batch: global comparable store sales up 7.9% with United States comparable TRANSACTIONS up 4.2% — customer counts rising, which is the only way a restaurant turnaround can be proved and which no other name here can claim; consolidated operating margin expanding 60 basis points to 10.5% while still absorbing the labour and restructuring investment; two consecutive large earnings beats (+17.6% and +28.8%) after three consecutive misses; the China structural risk removed via a $4 billion-valuation transaction that delivered $3.1 billion of cash, retained a 40% economic interest, and converted a besieged company-operated business into an asset-light licence; long-term debt cut from $14,575.9M to $11,780.2M; a shareholders' deficit that is improving; and a brand that independent voices describe as an innovation and franchise machine.

What holds us back: 60.6x trailing GAAP earnings and 33.4x the FY2027 consensus for a company consensus expects to grow revenue 1.6% and 5.3%; a base fair value of $112, just 6.7% above spot, below the street's $115.15 and identical to the analyst median of $112; a street split exactly 28 buy against 28 hold; a stock at the 88th percentile of its annual range, 3.1% from its high; a dividend of $2.47 that is 213% of trailing GAAP earnings and exceeded fiscal 2025 free cash flow by $329.4 million, with freeCashFlowToEquityTTM at MINUS $53 million; a buyback suspended at exactly zero; shareholders' equity of minus $7,674.3 million and net leverage of 3.23x; and an operating margin that must travel roughly 450 basis points to justify the consensus earnings path, of which the first 60 have been delivered.

The distinction that matters. This is not a case where we doubt the business — the June quarter is genuinely impressive and the transaction that removed the China overhang was well executed and correctly anticipated by an independent voice in our own knowledge base eight months before it happened. It is a case where the market has already paid for the recovery. The stock is 3% from its high, our number equals the analyst median, and the yield that makes it look defensive is currently funded by the recovery rather than by earnings. With 450 basis points of margin repair still required and 60 delivered, waiting for the next two prints costs 6.7% of base-case return and buys a great deal of confirmation.

  • Comparable TRANSACTIONS turning negative in any quarter. This is the number that distinguishes a real turnaround from a price-and-marketing effect, and it is currently +4.2%.

  • Any reduction to the dividend, which at 213% of GAAP earnings and above free cash flow is the most likely place a balance-sheet problem would surface.
  • A write-down of the retained 40% China interest (~$1.2 billion), which would say the transaction was a rescue rather than a strategic choice.
  • Fiscal 2027 guidance below approximately $2.90 of adjusted EPS, against the $3.14 consensus.
  • A break below the 200-day average of $95.11 on company-specific news.
