The Overview

Shopify makes the software that online shops run on. About 4 million businesses use it, from one-person operations to large brands. It charges a monthly subscription — but that is now only a quarter of its income. Three quarters comes from taking a small percentage of everything those shops sell, mostly by processing the card payments.

That second business is why the company grows so fast. In the three months to March, merchants sold $100.7 billion of goods through Shopify — up 35% on a year earlier, and faster than the 23% growth the year before. Of that, $67.1 billion was paid for using Shopify's own payment system, up 41%, because Shopify keeps persuading more of its merchants to use it: 67% of sales now go through Shopify Payments, up from 64%. Every percentage point there is worth roughly $4 billion of extra payment volume a year on which Shopify earns a full fee.

Revenue grew 34% and operating profit grew 88%.

Three cautions.

The company reports again tomorrow. That alone would argue for waiting.

The official profit figure is meaningless. Shopify owns stakes in other public companies, and those stakes are revalued every quarter and run through the profit line. In the March quarter they fell about a billion dollars, so the company reported a $581 million loss despite making $382 million from actually operating. A year earlier the same thing happened; in late 2024 it went the other way and produced a $1.3 billion profit. The published "price to earnings ratio" of 120 times is arithmetic on noise.

Growing costs it margin. The payments business is much less profitable per dollar than the subscription business — roughly 39 cents of gross profit per dollar against about 80 cents. Because the payments business is growing nearly twice as fast, the blended margin falls every year: 50.4% in 2024, 48.1% in 2025.

The shares are $123.30, up 5.4% today. On the profits analysts expect, that is about 67 times this year's earnings and 52 times next year's. Our estimate of fair value is $133, about 8% higher. The average analyst says $154 — and unusually, none of the 64 analysts covering it has a target below today's price.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, plus an independent, public-math cross-check further down the page (“Check our number”) so you can judge it for yourself.

What is genuinely excellent, and all of it is filing-verified: gross merchandise volume of $100.743 billion in a single quarter, up 35% and ACCELERATING from 23% a year earlier (30% constant currency, from 25%); revenue up 34%; merchant solutions up 39% with Shopify Payments revenue alone up $572 million; Payments penetration from 64% to 67%, processing $67.1 billion of GMV, up 41%; operating income up 88% to $382 million on 34% revenue growth; zero debt and $6,451 million of liquid assets plus $4,114 million of strategic stakes; free cash flow of $2,007 million on $26 million of capital expenditure; stock compensation of only 3.9% of revenue; and a new US$5.0 billion repurchase authorisation on a company that has never bought back a share.

What stops us: the company reports tomorrow; the shares just rose 5.38%; beta is 2.59, the highest in this batch; the price is 66.8x the 2026 consensus and 52.4x 2027; gross margin falls every time the company grows, from 50.4% in 2024 to 48.1% in 2025, because the 39%-growing line carries half the margin of the 21%-growing one; GAAP earnings are unusable and every screen therefore misprices the security in one direction or another; our base fair value of $133 is 13.6% BELOW the street's $154 on a name where all 64 analysts sit above the price; and our own knowledge base is net negative, with its highest-skill voice arguing that stablecoins collapse the payment take rate that is 76% of this company's revenue.

The distinction that matters. This is not a judgement that Shopify is expensive relative to its quality — it may well not be. It is a judgement that a 7.9% base-case return does not compensate for taking 2.59-beta exposure at 52x forward earnings, the day before a print, having already paid a 5.4% move, on a name where our research library contributes one bullish claim at conviction 30 and three describing structural threats. Every one of those is a reason to wait rather than a reason to decline. The business would justify a position at a price and after an event; the price and the event are both wrong today.

Pre-registered KILL criteria — what would take this to Avoid:

  • GMV growth below 25% in tomorrow's print or the next. It grew 35% and the entire multiple rests on it.
  • Gross margin below 48%, which would mean the mix shift is outrunning any operating-expense leverage and consensus EPS growth of 27.5% and 34.9% is unreachable.
  • Shopify Payments penetration flat or falling, which removes the mechanism by which revenue outgrows volume.
  • Monthly recurring revenue growth below 15%, which would say the subscription base is genuinely stalling rather than lapping the 2024 Shopify Plus price increase.
  • Any disclosure of credit deterioration in the $2,097 million of loans and merchant cash advances, which grew 17.5% in one quarter and which no metric in this file measures.
  • Evidence that AI-native platforms are taking new-merchant share at the long-tail end, which the no_priors claim names specifically.

Pre-registered FLIP conditions — what would take this to Buy — Tactical:

  • Tomorrow's print delivering GMV growth at or above 32% with gross margin at 48.5% or better and Payments penetration above 68%. That combination validates volume, margin and take rate at once and is the single most likely path to a Buy — and it resolves in twenty-four hours.
  • A price below approximately $105 (roughly 44.6x the 2027 consensus, near the 50-day moving average) with the fundamental picture intact, which would put the base case at +27%.
  • Actual execution of the US$5.0 billion repurchase authorisation. The first buyback in Shopify's history, worth 3.1% of the company, would change the shareholder-return arithmetic from zero to something.
  • A named-speaker, high-conviction bullish knowledge-base claim. The lane's only bullish entry carries conviction 30 and its bearish entries carry 60, 60 and 80.
  • Any credible refutation of the payment-disintermediation claim — for example, disclosure of stablecoin settlement inside Shopify Payments at a preserved take rate, which would turn our highest-skill bear point into a non-event.

Single biggest risk: the take rate. Seventy-six percent of Shopify's revenue is merchant solutions, and the dominant component is payment processing and currency conversion fees on $67.1 billion of quarterly Shopify Payments volume at a 67% penetration rate. The company's entire operating leverage story runs through raising that penetration toward 100%. Our own highest-skill knowledge-base voice — skill weight 2.0, conviction 80, dated November 2025 — argues that stablecoins "collapse the payment vig/float" and that deregulation is triggering a race in which everyone issues one. Nothing in Shopify's filings addresses it; nothing in the data contract lets us quantify it. At 52x the 2027 consensus, a security whose largest profit pool faces a named disintermediation risk that management has not addressed is not a security to buy the day before it reports.
