The Overview

Sherwin-Williams sells paint. Most people know the brand from the retail shelf, but that is the small part. The large part is 4,853 company-owned stores that exist to serve professional painting contractors — the people who paint houses and offices for a living. A contractor picks a store because it is close, because it has the colour in stock, and because it extends credit. Once that relationship exists it rarely moves. That is the whole business, and it is a very good one.

The most recent quarter, reported a week ago, was solid. Sales up 7.5%. Profit per share up 14%. The stock rose 2% the day this dive was struck.

Two things are worth understanding about how the growth arrived. First, the biggest single segment — the store network — grew only 5.1%. Second, the segment that grew 21.5% did so mostly because the company bought a Brazilian paint business from BASF for about $1.15 billion in October last year, and that business is now counted inside it. So the headline acceleration is partly a purchase.

Now the price. The shares cost $361.57. Analysts expect the company to earn about $13.52 per share in 2027. That is 26.8 times. For a company whose main division is growing 5% a year, that is a full price — you are paying about two and a half dollars of price for every dollar of growth. Our own estimate of what the shares are worth is $358, which is slightly below where they trade. If we are wrong to the downside the shares could fall to about $272. If we are wrong to the upside they could reach about $424. That is more room to fall than to rise, and that is why the verdict is Hold rather than Buy.

Two technical notes about the data, because honesty about the data is the point of this document. We check every company's reported capital spending against the actual filing, because the data vendor gets it wrong about four times in ten and the error always makes the company look better than it is. Here it was right — $797.6 million, matching the annual report exactly. We record that as a genuine clean result. But the vendor's figure for the company's debt is $12.9 billion when the annual report says $10.871 billion, because the vendor has counted store leases as borrowings. Where the filing and the vendor disagree, the filing wins, and this dive uses $10.871 billion.

Finally: our knowledge base of 52,021 expert claims contains exactly one that mentions Sherwin-Williams. It is not a buy or sell opinion. It says the company is wrongly grouped with chemical manufacturers when it behaves like a home-improvement retailer. We report that as the near-empty lane it is rather than inflating it into a view.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, plus an independent, public-math cross-check further down the page (“Check our number”) so you can judge it for yourself.

11. Verdict, kill-criteria and flip conditions

Hold.

The business is not the problem and we want to be clear about that before the objections. Sherwin-Williams operates 4,853 company-operated stores serving American professional painting contractors, earns a 24.6% pre-tax margin on that segment, converts $3.887 billion of trailing operating cash on $673.5 million of trailing capital expenditure, runs negative working capital so that its customers finance it, earns 14.3% on invested capital, and returns roughly 5.1% a year to shareholders. The June quarter beat, revenue accelerated to +7.5%, and two of three segments expanded margin. Nothing in this file is deteriorating.

The price is the problem, and the arithmetic is not close. At $361.57 the shares are 30.0x the FY2026 consensus of $12.05 and 26.8x FY2027's $13.52 — a PEG of 2.2 — against adjusted EPS that grew 0.9% in FY2025, revenue that grew 2.3% across FY2024 and FY2025 combined, and a largest-and-most-profitable segment that grew 5.1% last quarter with its margin down 20 basis points. The gross-margin expansion from 42.1% to 48.9% that produced most of the last three years' earnings growth is finished. Our base fair value of $358 is 1.0% below spot with 0.70:1 asymmetry — 24.8% of downside against 17.3% of upside.

Three specific reasons the verdict is Hold rather than Watch.

First, the downside case is not a disaster scenario — it is a normal multiple. The bear at $272 is 21.0x the FY2027 consensus low. That is not a crisis multiple; it is what a mid-single-digit revenue compounder trades at in an ordinary market. The distance between "priced for quality" and "priced for growth it is not delivering" is the entire 25% of downside.

Second, there is no independent support to lean on. The knowledge base holds one claim on this company in 52,021, it is neutral, and it is about peer-group classification. The lane is a genuine void and we report it as one. A name at 30x forward with no expert conviction behind it is relying on the multiple alone.

Third, the entry point is the worst available in twelve months. Up 16.45% in three months, 3.64% below the 52-week high, 11.2% above the 50-day average, RSI 62.6. The entire twelve-month return arrived in the last quarter.

And one honest counterweight, because the case is not one-sided. If the multiple simply holds at 30x forward, the twelve-month return is roughly 12% plus a dividend — a good outcome from a business with this quality of cash generation, and the street's $392.5 target is exactly that view. The disagreement between our $358 and their $392.5 is 2.5 multiple points on one estimate row. We are not claiming a superior forecast, and a reader who believes premium multiples on premium businesses are durable should weight the street's number over ours. What we will not do is manufacture a Buy out of a base case below spot.

Pre-registered KILL criteria — what would take this to Avoid:

  • Paint Stores Group organic sales growth below 3% in any quarter, indicating the US repaint and new-residential cycle has rolled over. It was 5.1% in June.

  • Consolidated gross margin below 48% — a 120-basis-point retreat from the June quarter's 49.2%, which a propylene-driven raw-material cycle could produce.
  • A material reduction in the repurchase pace below roughly $2.5 billion annualised while short-term borrowings continue rising. The buyback is about two of the twelve points of consensus EPS growth.
