The Overview

SLB — most people still know it as Schlumberger — sells the equipment, chemicals and services that oil companies use to find, drill and produce oil. It is the biggest company of its kind in the world. If oil companies spend more, SLB earns more; if they spend less, it earns less. That is the whole business, with two exceptions we will come to.

Last quarter looked fine on the top line and was not fine underneath. Revenue rose 5%. But SLB bought a company called ChampionX in July 2025, and ChampionX contributed $870 million of that revenue. Take it out and revenue fell 5%. Profit fell harder: earnings per share dropped 26%.

The reason is geography. Roughly a third of SLB's revenue comes from the Middle East and Asia, and there is a conflict in the Middle East. Wells have been shut in. Some operations are constrained by security problems. Revenue from that region fell 14%. Management does not know when it recovers — they say so, in those words.

Now the two exceptions, and they are more interesting than the oil business.

The first is Digital: software and data services sold to the same oil companies. It is small — about $700 million a quarter — but it is growing 18% a year, it now has just over $1 billion of recurring revenue, and it earns a much better margin than anything else SLB does.

The second is stranger. SLB has started building data centres. It turns out that the skill of manufacturing complex modular equipment off-site and assembling it in remote places transfers directly from oil fields to AI infrastructure. That business grew 63% in the first half of this year, SLB was chosen as a delivery partner for a one-gigawatt Meta facility in Canada, and management says it will pass $1 billion of annualised revenue this year and $2 billion by the end of next year. One commentator in our knowledge base called SLB "the best AI+energy trade" and "a dark-horse AI company". That is the most interesting thing about this company and it is not yet 5% of it.

The shares cost $50.81. Analysts expect $3.24 of earnings in 2027, so you are paying about 15.7 times. That sounds cheap. But to get there, SLB has to earn $1.43 per share in the back half of this year having earned $1.07 in the front half — and that requires the Middle East to come back on a schedule nobody has.

Our estimate of fair value is $54, about 6% above the price. The average analyst says $63.83. We are meaningfully below the street and we explain why in Section 6.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, We don’t publish a reverse-DCF cross-check for pre-profit companies — negative or missing earnings break that math — so take this number on our modeling alone.

3. The two businesses that are not oilfield services

Digital. Revenue of $697M in the June quarter, +9% sequentially and +18% year on year (+12% excluding $34M of ChampionX digital revenue). Pretax operating margin 27.8%, up 683bps sequentially and 187bps year on year — the only division whose margin expanded on both bases. Adjusted EBITDA margin 34.7%, the highest in the company. Annualised recurring revenue for the Digital Division was $1.04bn at 2026-06-30, up 15% from $904M a year earlier. That is a filing-sourced number and it is the single most useful disclosure SLB makes about the quality of this business.

Data Center Solutions, which sits inside the All Other category and has no segment line of its own. What the company discloses in prose:

> "Our Data Center Solutions business maintained its strong growth trajectory during the second quarter... Data Center Solutions revenue in the first six months of 2026 grew 63% year on year. During the quarter, Meta announced plans for a new 1GW data center in Canada, and we are proud to have been selected as a delivery partner for this project. Beyond adding new hyperscaler customers and expanding our footprint internationally, we have widened our scope to include engineering and design... The Data Center Solutions business remains on track to exceed $1 billion annualized revenue run rate by the end of this year. As we broaden our offering and further diversify our customer base and geographic footprint, we expect to surpass $2 billion annualized revenue run rate as we exit 2027."

North America Data Center Solutions revenue increased 80% year on year in the quarter; the FY2025 10-K reports it grew 121% in 2025. All Other — which also contains APS and SLB Capturi — was $505M of revenue at a 28.2% pretax operating margin and 44.9% adjusted EBITDA margin in the quarter.

Two cautions, both stated. First, the $1bn and $2bn run-rate figures are management guidance, and this house half-weights guidance; they are not audited disclosures. Second, the business has no separate reportable segment, so its revenue, margin and backlog cannot be verified independently from the filings — a reader is dependent on prose. The single most valuable disclosure SLB could make in the next twelve months is a Data Center Solutions segment line, and it is written into the upgrade conditions in Section 11 for that reason.
