The Overview

Simon owns shopping malls — the good ones. Roughly 200 properties in America, Europe and Asia, including most of the premium outlet centres.

The most important thing that happened to this company this year is that its chief executive died. David Simon ran Simon Property Group from 1995 until his death on 22 March this year, at 64, from cancer. The board had planned for it: his son Eli, already the chief operating officer, was appointed chief executive the next day, and an independent director became chairman. The transition took one day and the business does not appear to have missed a step. But an investor should know that this company has been run by one person for thirty-one years and is now being run by someone with eight weeks in the job.

The business itself is doing well, and better than the "malls are dying" story suggests. Occupancy is 96%. Rent per square foot rose 5.2%. And here is the number that matters most: the shops in Simon's malls sold $819 of merchandise per square foot over the past year, up 11.8%. Retailers that are selling more can pay more rent.

Now, a warning about the data. Property companies report a measure called FFO — funds from operations — because the accounting rules make them charge depreciation on buildings that in practice are not losing value. Ordinary earnings per share for a property company is close to meaningless. The data file we were given contains no FFO figure at all. It shows earnings of $14.14 per share for last year, of which $7.56 was a one-off accounting gain created when Simon took full control of a partnership it already owned 88% of. No cash changed hands for that $7.56.

Taking the numbers from the filings instead: Simon's real cash earnings were $12.73 per share last year, and management expects $13.10 to $13.25 this year. At $225.90 you are paying about 17 times.

That is not obviously cheap. The shares are up 40.6% in the past year against a market up 24.3%. And unusually, the average analyst price target — $219.67 — is below the current price, with the median at $215. Twenty of the thirty-seven analysts covering it say hold.

The dividend was just raised 7.1% to $2.25 a quarter, which is $9.00 a year, or about 4.0% of the share price. That is real and it is well covered — it takes about 68% of the cash earnings.

Our estimate of fair value is $226. The price is $225.90.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, plus an independent, public-math cross-check further down the page (“Check our number”) so you can judge it for yourself.

1. The succession

This section exists because the vendor payload's only record of it is a name in a field.

From the 8-K filed 2026-03-24, reporting an event of 2026-03-23:

> "It is with profound sorrow and immense respect that the Board of Directors of Simon Property Group, Inc. (NYSE: SPG) announces the passing of David Simon, the Company's visionary Chairman of the Board, Chief Executive Officer and President — the singular leader who built Simon Property Group into the largest and most admired retail real estate company in the world. He was 64 years old. Mr. Simon passed away peacefully on March 22, 2026 surrounded by his family, after a courageous battle with cancer."

And on the transition:

> "The Board of Directors has always had a strong emphasis on succession planning for its executive leadership team... As a result of their hard work, effective today, the Board has appointed Eli Simon as its Chief Executive Officer and President. In addition, Eli will continue as Chief Operating Officer and Director... In addition, Larry Glasscock has been appointed by the Board to serve as Non-Executive Chairman of the Board consistent with these succession planning efforts and the Company's Governance Principles."

The investment-relevant facts, stated without commentary. David Simon was named Chief Executive Officer in 1995 and held the role for thirty-one years. The succession was executed within one day of his death, to an internal candidate who was already Chief Operating Officer and a Director. The chairmanship was separated from the chief executive role and given to an independent director. Eli Simon signed the first-quarter earnings release on 2026-05-11 as "Chief Executive Officer, President and Chief Operating Officer", and that release raised full-year guidance and raised the dividend — which is what a board and a new chief executive do when they are confident, and is the only evidence available so far.

One financial consequence is disclosed and quantified. The first quarter carried $40.0 million, or $0.10 per share, of accelerated stock compensation expense, of which $8.3 million ($0.02) is inside Real Estate FFO and $31.7 million ($0.08) is inside Other platform investments, net of tax. So the reported Real Estate FFO of $3.17 per share absorbed $0.02 of that charge, and the reported FFO of $2.91 absorbed the full $0.10.

What this means for the file. The key-person risk that hung over this company for three decades has been realised and, on the evidence of one quarter, absorbed. What cannot be assessed from any document in this archive is whether the capital-allocation judgement that built the company transfers. That is the reason the long-horizon confidence in this dive is Low, and it is the principal reason a strong operating quarter does not produce a Buy.

The transition was planned, executed within a day of David Simon's death on 2026-03-22, and has so far produced a quarter with Real Estate FFO per share up 7.5%, guidance raised and the dividend raised 7.1%. That is as good a first quarter as a new chief executive can deliver and it is one quarter. The specific things that cannot be assessed yet are the ones that made this company what it is: the willingness to buy distressed retail assets at the bottom, the discipline to sell them — the ABG stake went for $1.2 billion in 2024 at a $414.8 million gain — and the judgement about when to take equity in tenants, which produced an $86.1 million loss on Catalyst in 2025. The offsetting consideration is that Simon's assets are the least dependent on management genius of anything in this batch: 96.0% occupancy on irreplaceable A-quality retail real estate with tenants whose sales are rising 11.8% will generate cash under most stewardship. We are paying 17x for that, and 15x is where we would want to be paying it.
