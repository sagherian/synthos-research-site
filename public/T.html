The Overview

AT&T sells mobile phone service to about 120 million connections and, increasingly, home internet over fibre-optic cable and wireless. It is one of the two largest telephone companies in the United States.

The company's total sales grew only 2.3% last quarter, which sounds dull. But that number hides the actual story, and this year AT&T changed how it reports so the story is finally visible.

It now splits itself into three parts. The big part — 91% of the business, called Advanced Connectivity — grew 4.1%, and its profit grew 20.3% because costs barely moved and the accounting charge for wearing out old equipment actually fell. Its profit margin went from 22.2% to 25.7% in a single year. Within it, home internet revenue grew 27%.

The small part — 5% of the business, called Legacy — fell 26%, because AT&T is deliberately switching off its old copper telephone network. That decline is a choice, not a failure, and it is what drags the headline number down to 2.3%.

The shares are $23.38. The company is expected to earn about $2.34 a share this year and $2.56 next, so you pay about 10 times this year's earnings and 9 times next year's. It generated $19.4 billion of free cash last year — about 12% of the entire value of the company — and pays a dividend of $1.11 a year, a 4.75% yield, which the cash covers two and a half times over. Over the past twelve months the shares fell 15.7% while the American stock market rose 24.3%.

Two cautions. First, the data file we normally rely on is badly wrong here: it says AT&T owes $174 billion when the real figure is $144 billion, because it counts the company's building leases twice. That error makes the company look far more indebted than it is.

Second, and this is real: AT&T agreed last August to buy radio spectrum from a company called EchoStar for about $23 billion, paying with a mix of cash and new loans. That is a large commitment for a business already carrying a lot of debt, and it will push borrowings back up after several years of paying them down.

Our estimate of fair value is $26.90, about 15% above the price, plus the dividend. The average analyst says $27.13 — but the analyst community as a whole rates the shares a Hold, and six firms tell clients to sell.

  • Downside Risk 5/10. Enormous, stable cash generation and the least volatile share price in this group — against $23 billion of new commitments.
  • Growth Quality 5/10. Poor headline, genuinely good composition. Profit in the main business is growing five times faster than its sales.
  • Exponential Potential 3/10. A mature market. The fast-growing home-internet line is too small to change the arithmetic.

Putting a number on it: our fair-value estimate is $26.90 against a current price of $25.11 — real upside if our numbers are right.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, We don’t publish a reverse-DCF cross-check for pre-profit companies — negative or missing earnings break that math — so take this number on our modeling alone.

First, revenue grew 4.1% and operating income grew 20.3% — a five-to-one ratio. 2. Earnings quality — why the vendor's 7.7x P/E is wrong

The vendor reports priceToEarningsRatioTTM of 7.716x and netIncomePerShareTTM of $3.098. Both are arithmetically correct on trailing GAAP and both are misleading.

Trailing GAAP net income is $21,494M (Q3 2025 $9,314M + Q4 2025 $3,724M + Q1 2026 $3,829M + Q2 2026 $4,627M). The September 2025 quarter alone contributed $9,314M — more than double any other quarter in the series — on EBITDA of $17,670M against a run-rate of $11.2B to $13.0B. That is roughly $5 billion of non-recurring benefit in a single quarter, and it is what makes the trailing GAAP figure and every ratio derived from it unusable.

The adjusted series is clean and is what estimates are struck on. 10. Verdict, kill-criteria and flip conditions

Buy — Tactical.

What is genuinely good, and almost none of it is visible in the vendor payload: Advanced Connectivity — 90.7% of revenue — grew 4.1% while its operating income grew 20.3%, its operating margin went from 22.2% to 25.7% and its EBITDA margin from 40.5% to 42.0%, with advanced home internet up 27.3% and depreciation down 6.9% as a fully depreciated copper network rolls off; $19,442M of free cash flow, a 12.1% yield, covering a 4.75% dividend 2.5 times; 158.7 million shares — 2.3% of the company — retired in six months for $3,917M, a roughly 4.6% annual pace; funded net debt of $126,384M, not the $155,752M every screen shows; beta of 0.422, the lowest in this batch; the highest vendor composite rating in this batch (A- / 4), which we do not reject; and five consecutive adjusted-EPS beats, the latest by 10.2%.

What keeps this Tactical rather than Core: a ~$23 billion EchoStar spectrum purchase funded with "cash on hand and term loan borrowings" that takes funded leverage from about 2.60x to about 3.07x; a Legacy segment falling 25.9% with its operating margin down from 43.6% to 32.0%, so the runoff is getting less profitable; capital expenditure at 17.5% of revenue with no relief in sight; consolidated revenue growth of 2.3% that no amount of segment analysis changes; a Hold sell-side consensus with six sell ratings; a price 7.1% below its falling 200-day moving average; and a knowledge-base lane whose only name-level claim is bearish.

The distinction that matters. The market is pricing AT&T as a melting ice cube and the segment disclosure says it is not one — but the reason the market cannot see that is the same reason we can: the company reorganised its segments this year and the data vendors have not caught up. That is a genuine, temporary, mechanical information advantage, and it is the reason for a position. It is not a reason for a large one, because the thing the market IS pricing correctly — $23 billion of new spectrum obligations landing on a balance sheet with $144 billion of debt and negative tangible book — is also real and is about to happen. Buy the yield and the operating leverage; size it for the leverage step.

Pre-registered KILL criteria — what would take this to Watch or Avoid:

  • Advanced Connectivity operating margin below 24% in any quarter. It is 25.7% and the entire earnings-growth case rests on it.
  • Segment depreciation ceasing to decline. The −6.9% year-on-year fall is the mechanism producing 20% operating-income growth on 4% revenue growth; when it stops, so does the growth.
  • A ratings action or a funded-leverage ratio above 3.3x after the EchoStar close.
  • The buyback suspended to fund the spectrum purchase. That removes roughly 4.6 points of the 9.3% total shareholder yield and would be the clearest signal that $23 billion was too much.
  • Any impairment against the $129,123M of licences, which would directly refute the only genuinely useful claim in our knowledge base.
  • Advanced home internet growth below 15%, from 27.3% — the only fast-growing line in the company.

Pre-registered FLIP conditions — what would take this to Buy — Core:

  • The EchoStar transaction closing predominantly with cash, keeping funded leverage below 2.9x. This is the single most likely path and it may already have happened — management expected a close by the end of July 2026 and the archive does not confirm the outcome.
  • A third consecutive quarter of Advanced Connectivity margin expansion on 2026-10-28, confirming the operating leverage is structural rather than a depreciation artefact.
  • Consolidated revenue growth above 3%, which would mean Advanced Connectivity is finally outrunning the Legacy runoff at the consolidated line and would justify a multiple above 11x.
  • A price below approximately $21 (roughly 8.2x the 2027 consensus, near the 52-week low), which would put the base case at +28% with the dividend yield above 5.2%.
