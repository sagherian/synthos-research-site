The Overview

TJX runs TJ Maxx, Marshalls, HomeGoods, Winners in Canada and TK Maxx in Europe and Australia — a little over five thousand stores. Its business is buying merchandise that other retailers ordered and then could not sell, or made too much of, and selling it at a discount. Because the merchandise changes every week, shoppers come back often to see what has arrived. The company calls this the treasure hunt and it is the entire model.

It is an extraordinarily well-run business. Last year sales were $60.4 billion, up 7%, and sales at stores that had been open more than a year rose 5% — which for a retailer of this size is very strong. Profit margins went up in all four of its divisions. In the most recent quarter, sales at existing stores rose 6%, faster still, and earnings per share jumped 29%.

On money: TJX owes $2.9 billion and holds $5.6 billion of cash, so it is a net lender, not a borrower. It earns about 22 cents of profit on every dollar of capital it employs, which is roughly three times what a typical large company manages. It gave shareholders $4.3 billion last year through dividends and share buybacks.

So why not buy it? Because of what it costs. The shares are $157.55. The company is expected to earn about $5.23 per share this fiscal year, so you pay about 30 times earnings for a business whose earnings grow around 10% a year. That is a price that already assumes everything goes right.

There is also one bookkeeping point worth knowing. Last year's reported profit of $4.87 per share included 14 cents of one-off benefit from a legal settlement about credit card fees. Take that out and the real figure was $4.73, and last year's growth was 11% rather than the 14% the headline suggests. The data file we use carries the higher number without saying so.

Our estimate of fair value is $162, only about 3% above the price. The average analyst says $183 — and remarkably, none of the 53 analysts covering the company has a target below today's price, which means there is nobody left to turn more positive. The company reports again on 19 August, fifteen days away.

  • Downside Risk 4/10. Net cash, 103 times interest coverage, the lowest share-price volatility in this batch — but a 30x multiple with nothing underneath it.
  • Growth Quality 8/10. Growth is real, accelerating, and visible in every division. It is also being paid for.
  • Exponential Potential 3/10. This company grows by opening about 3% more stores each year. That is excellent and it is not exponential.

Putting a number on it: our fair-value estimate is $162 against a current price of $157.55 — real upside if our numbers are right.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, plus an independent, public-math cross-check further down the page (“Check our number”) so you can judge it for yourself.

2. The number that is not what it looks like: fiscal 2026 EPS

From the 10-K, verbatim:

> "Net income was $5.5 billion in fiscal 2026 compared to $4.9 billion in fiscal 2025. Diluted earnings per share in fiscal 2026 were $4.87 compared to $4.26 in fiscal 2025. The credit card interchange fees litigation settlement and related expenses resulted in a net benefit of $0.14 on diluted earnings per share in fiscal 2026. Foreign currency had a $0.01 negative impact on diluted earnings per share in fiscal 2026 compared to a $0.01 positive impact in fiscal 2025."

So the clean fiscal-2026 diluted EPS is $4.73, not $4.87, and the underlying growth rate was 11.0%, not 14.3%.

Two independent confirmations that this is right. First, the earnings calendar's reported actuals for the four fiscal-2026 quarters — $0.92, $1.10, $1.28 and $1.43 — sum to exactly $4.73, while the vendor's income statement quarters sum to $4.89. The gap is entirely in the fourth quarter, which is where the settlement landed. Second, the 10-K's segment commentary attributes margin improvement to it in three of four segments: Marmaxx's rise to 15.1% was "primarily driven by a net benefit from the credit card interchange fees litigation settlement and related expenses as well as favorable merchandise margin"; HomeGoods' rise to 12.2% likewise; while TJX Canada's margin FELL to 13.4% "primarily driven by expenses related to the credit card interchange fees litigation settlement" and TJX International's rise to 7.0% was "partially offset by expenses related to" it. The settlement was a net benefit in the US and a net cost abroad.

The vendor's income statement carries the gross $5,494M of net income and an eps of 4.89 with no flag whatsoever, and epsdiluted is NULL on every row. Every trailing multiple published on this company from that file is therefore struck on an inflated denominator.

What is genuinely excellent, and essentially none of it is in dispute: comparable-store sales of +5% in fiscal 2026 accelerating to +6% in the first quarter of fiscal 2027, driven by both basket and transactions; all four segments expanding margin, with Marmaxx at 15.1%, HomeGoods at 12.2% and TJX International closing from 5.9% to 7.0%; pre-tax margin from 11.5% to 12.1% with the cost-of-sales ratio down 40 basis points and SG&A down 30; return on invested capital 22.1% and return on equity 59.7%; a 28.6-day cash conversion cycle and 5.5x inventory turns; $2,710M of net funded cash and 103x interest coverage; $4,917M of free cash flow and $4.3B returned to shareholders; 1,155 US stores of stated headroom in the two largest concepts; and five consecutive EPS beats.

What we are declining to pay for: 30.1x the fiscal-2027 consensus and 27.3x fiscal 2028 for an earnings line whose clean growth rate was 11.0%, not the 14.3% the reported figure implies — because $0.14 of last year's $4.87 came from a credit-card interchange litigation settlement that will not repeat. A base fair value of $162 that is 2.8% above spot and 11.4% below the street's $182.75. A consensus with 47 positive analysts, one negative, and a lowest target already above the price — no dispersion, no informational edge. A knowledge-base lane of exactly one claim that does not name the company. And a second-quarter print in fifteen days.

The distinction that matters. This is not a criticism of TJX. It is the observation that everything good about this company is known, published, and in the multiple — and that our own arithmetic, run independently, lands below the crowd's on a name where our research library has contributed nothing to differentiate the view. In the Synthos frame, a name where the base case sits at spot, the fair value sits below consensus, and the conviction lane is empty is a Watch by construction, not by hesitation. We want this business. We want it at a price that has a return in it.

Pre-registered KILL criteria — what would take this to Avoid:

  • Comparable-store sales below 3% in any quarter. That is the number the entire multiple rests on, and it is reported every quarter.
  • Per-store inventories still up 10% or more against a decelerating comp at the 2026-08-19 print — markdowns follow, and gross margin follows markdowns.
  • Pre-tax margin below 11.5%, which would mean the interchange benefit's absence is not being offset by operating improvement.
  • Any quantified tariff or landed-cost impact appearing in the cost-of-sales ratio, which the 10-K currently discusses only as a risk factor.
  • A break below the 200-day moving average at $154.58 on company-specific news rather than market beta.
  • A material slowdown in unit growth below 2%, which would remove the mechanical half of the growth algorithm.

Pre-registered FLIP conditions — what would take this to Buy — Tactical or Buy — Core:

  • A price below approximately $138 (roughly 24x FY2028E, 26x FY2027E), which would put the base case at +17% and make the risk/reward genuinely asymmetric. This is the single most likely path to a Buy and it is 12.4% below today's close — well inside the range this stock traversed between last October and this February.
  • The 2026-08-19 print delivering comps at or above 6% with EPS materially above $1.19 — which, given consensus asks for only 2.4% EPS growth across the remaining three quarters after a 29% first quarter, is a realistic outcome. A beat of that shape would validate the fiscal-2027 line and justify revisiting the multiple assumption in 6b.
  • TJX International's segment margin reaching 9% or better, which on $8.0B of revenue is roughly $160M of incremental pre-tax profit and is the largest single identified source of upside to the base case.
  • A genuine name-level knowledge-base claim appearing. At present the lane contains one three-year-old generic retail principle with no named speaker, and that alone is worth roughly one tier of caution.

Where TJX fits in the Synthos Framework Portfolio. The consumer / defensive-retail sleeve, at 0% today with a 2-3% target on a fill near $138 or a confirmed 6%-plus comp on 2026-08-19. On batch overlap: TJX is the lowest-beta name in this batch (0.623) and the only one whose model is countercyclical in a specific, mechanical way — a weak apparel cycle is a supply opportunity, not a demand problem. That is a genuine portfolio-construction argument for owning it at some price. It is not an argument for owning it at 30x. Logged as a tracked Synthos call (Watch) as of 2026-08-04 at $157.55.

Single biggest risk: the multiple, and there is nothing under it. At 30.1x forward earnings and 2.78x sales for a business growing clean EPS at 11%, TJX carries no valuation cushion whatsoever. The balance sheet, excellent as it is, cannot supply one — $2.7B of net cash against a $174.0B market capitalisation is 1.6% of the price. A de-rating to 25x — which is where this stock traded as recently as its fiscal-2023 comp trough — costs 17% with no change in the business at all. That is the whole bear case, and the reason a 25% downside case sits against a 25% upside case on a name where almost everything is going right.
