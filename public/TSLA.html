The Overview

Tesla sells two things today: electric cars, and big batteries that store electricity for power grids and homes. Together those brought in about $104 billion over the last twelve months. The company is worth $1.29 trillion on the stock market. Those two numbers do not fit together for a car company, and they are not meant to — what people are buying is the belief that Tesla will turn its cars into self-driving taxis and build humanoid robots, and that both will be enormous.

Two weeks ago the company reported its June-quarter results and the stock has been punished. It is worth being precise about what happened, because the headline is misleading. Sales were better than expected — up 25% from a year earlier and above what analysts forecast. Profit was much worse than expected — 33 cents a share against an expected 50 cents. The gap is spending. Tesla is spending far more on research and on running the company than it was a year ago, and a chunk of that is the accounting cost of the huge share package granted to its chief executive.

There is one more thing about that profit worth knowing. Tesla owns a $2 billion stake in a private company that its own chief executive also runs. In the June quarter it wrote that stake UP by $1 billion, and that gain counts as profit. It also got a $274 million one-off tax break. Add those two together and they are larger than the entire $1.1 billion profit the company reported. Strip them out and the quarter was roughly break-even.

Now the good news, which is genuine and which almost nobody reports. Tesla earns money selling pollution credits to other carmakers. That revenue has essentially no cost, so it flatters the profit margin on cars — and it is disappearing, down 67% in a year as governments cut the programmes. If you take it out and look at the margin on actually building and selling cars, it went up, from about 15.0% to about 16.3%. The car business is improving underneath a reported number that is getting worse.

The balance sheet is strong: $43.5 billion of cash and investments against $9.1 billion of debt. But Tesla now plans to spend more than $25 billion this year on factories and computing equipment — up from a $20 billion plan made only six months ago — while generating around $19 billion from operations. That means the company is likely to burn cash this year, and its own filing says it may need to raise more money.

Our estimate of what the shares are worth is about $250, against a price of $327. To get there we are still crediting the self-driving and robot businesses with hundreds of billions of dollars that do not yet exist on any revenue line. If you credited them with nothing and valued the car and battery businesses as generously as the world's largest carmaker is valued, you would get about $65 a share.

  • Downside Risk 8/10. Not solvency — $34.4bn of net cash. It is 339x earnings, a beta of 1.827, and free cash flow about to go negative.
  • Growth Quality 6/10. Revenue reaccelerating hard (+25.5% in the quarter); operating margin down for four straight years, 16.8% to 2.6%.
  • Exponential Potential 8/10. The highest in this batch, and still not enough at this price.

Putting a number on it: our fair-value estimate is $250 against a current price of $327.35 — consistent with our call to stay away or wait for a better setup.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

Three observations.

First, regulatory credits are collapsing. Down 67% year over year in the quarter, down 49% across the first half, and down 28% across fiscal 2025 (from a derived ~$2.75B to ~$1.98B). The 10-K attributes it to governmental action restricting the programmes; the 10-Q adds that "recent governmental and regulatory actions have restricted certain regulatory credit programs tied to our products." This revenue carries essentially no cost of revenue, so every dollar lost is a dollar of gross profit lost — and its disappearance mechanically depresses the reported automotive margin even when the underlying business improves. That is Section 2.

Second, services and other grew 50% to a derived $4.62 billion, attributed in the 10-Q to "increases in used vehicle sales volume and average selling price, non-warranty maintenance services and collision revenue and paid Supercharging sessions." Note what is not in that list: autonomous ride-hailing. The robotaxi service launched in June 2025 and the 10-Q says it has been expanded, but no revenue from it is separately disclosed anywhere in the filing.

Third, energy is now a real business with a real backlog — $10.05 billion of remaining performance obligations at 2026-06-30, of which $4.56 billion is expected within twelve months, plus 22.3 GWh deployed through the first half of 2026 against 46.7 GWh in all of 2025. It also has a related-party problem: $318 million of Q2'26 energy revenue and $405 million of first-half revenue came from SpaceX buying Megapacks, an entity controlled by the same chief executive. That is roughly 10.5% of the quarter's energy revenue.

This is the single most important analytical issue on this name, and the popular version of it is wrong.

The 2026-07-22 print produced actual earnings per share of $0.33 against a $0.50 estimate — a 34% miss. It is being read as a demand failure. It was not.

The entire operating-income decline is operating expense. Gross profit rose $873 million; research and development plus selling, general and administrative rose $1.398 billion, exactly accounting for the $525 million swing. And $472 million of that increase was stock-based compensation: $189 million more in research and development and $283 million more in selling, general and administrative, the latter attributed by the 10-Q to the 2025 CEO Performance Award. That is 34% of the operating-expense increase, and it is non-cash.

Below the operating line, two items produced the profit.

1. A $1.00 billion net gain on the SpaceX equity investment. Tesla invested $2.00 billion in March 2026 (originally a preferred-share investment in a related artificial-intelligence venture, converted to SpaceX common stock), for an ownership interest of less than 1%. It is accounted for under the equity method with the fair value option elected, classified Level 2 with a $238 million discount for lack of marketability, and marked through Other income. In one quarter the stake was written up by half its cost. The counterparty is controlled by Tesla's own chief executive.

2. A $274 million discrete income tax benefit from releasing the valuation allowance on California deferred tax assets following the enactment of California Senate Bill 122. Effective tax rate fell from 23% to 15%.

Stated plainly: those two items total $1.274 billion pre-tax against $1.114 billion of reported net income. Even after tax on the investment gain, they are approximately the whole quarter. Our derivation — labelled as ours — is that ex-mark, ex-tax-benefit, the June quarter was roughly break-even to a small loss. Pre-tax income of $1.329 billion less the $1.00 billion mark is $329 million; the tax provision of $201 million would have been $475 million without the discrete benefit.

6b. The return bridge (why the multiple moves)

Expected return decomposes as earnings growth + multiple drift + shareholder yield, and on this name two of the three legs are negative.

Earnings growth is the strong leg. Consensus earnings per share go $1.738 (2026) to $2.280 (2027) to $3.123 (2028) — a 34% annual compound rate off a small base, on 20-25 analysts. We do not dispute it; the revenue reacceleration to +25.5% and the 133-basis-point improvement in automotive margin excluding credits support it.

Multiple drift is a large, structural negative, and our fair value assumes COMPRESSION. The market pays 208.8x fiscal 2026 consensus net income today. Our $250 base assumes it pays 89.1x fiscal 2028 net income and 43.0x fiscal 2029 — still extraordinary multiples in absolute terms, and still crediting the optionality with several hundred billion dollars, but a compression of roughly 60% from where it stands. This is not forward-multiple rolldown; the price would have to fall for those multiples to be reached at those earnings. The driver is not a re-rating of quality — it is that 34% annual earnings growth cannot outrun a 209x starting multiple over any horizon we can underwrite.

Shareholder yield is negative. No dividend (lastDividend 0, no common dividends paid in any year of the cash-flow file), no buyback in any year, and share count up 5.3% in six months to 3,949,547,394 with roughly 710 million further restricted shares already outstanding and awaiting milestones. 11. Verdict, kill-criteria and flip conditions

Avoid.

This is a de-rating in progress, not an opportunity — and the reason is not the one most people are giving.

The operating business is doing better than the print. Revenue grew 25.5% in the June quarter and beat the estimate by 6.9%. Automotive gross margin excluding regulatory credits improved approximately 133 basis points year over year and was flat across fiscal 2024-2025 while the reported figure fell, meaning the headline margin erosion is a regulatory-credit story that will exhaust itself. Energy has $10.05 billion of contracted backlog. The balance sheet holds $34.44 billion of net cash. The car company is fine.

The price is not about the car company. Corrected enterprise value is $1,258.44 billion against $103.6 billion of trailing revenue and $3.818 billion of trailing net income — 12.1x sales and 338.6x earnings. Value the entire automotive and energy operation at Toyota's $224.0 billion market capitalisation, add the net cash, and the fundamentals-only figure is $65 a share. Everything above that — 80% of the price — is an option on autonomy and humanoid robots that generates no separately disclosed revenue in the 10-K or either 10-Q, whose closest customer-validated proxy is $4.05 billion of driver-assistance deferred revenue growing 4.7% in six months, and whose loudest advocate in our own knowledge base is the chief executive, who accounts for 22.1% of the lane.

Meanwhile the funding profile deteriorated inside six months. Capital expenditure guidance went from "exceed $20 billion" to "in excess of $25 billion" between the January 10-K and the July 10-Q, requiring second-half spending above $16.7 billion against $8.28 billion in the first half. Trailing operating cash flow is $18.68 billion. Fiscal 2026 free cash flow is very likely negative, and the 10-Q says liquidity management "may include additional funding." There is no dividend, no buyback in any year on file, and share count rose 5.3% in under six months to 3,949,547,394 with roughly 700 million further restricted shares outstanding awaiting milestones. Shareholder yield is negative.

Our base fair value of $250 is 23.6% below spot and still credits the option with roughly $700 billion. We could not construct a defensible number above the price without adopting management's timeline as an input, which house rules forbid and the attribution data makes unwise.

  • Two consecutive quarters of negative free cash flow with the net cash balance falling below $25 billion.

  • Material share sales by the chief executive to fund the disclosed and unresolved tax obligation on the June 2026 exercise.

  • A sixth line in the revenue disaggregation table. Autonomous ride-hailing or robotics revenue disclosed separately, at any material size, in any 10-Q. This is the single cleanest falsifier of our entire thesis and it is publicly checkable every quarter.

On the exponential score of 8 sitting alongside an Avoid. These are not in tension and the distinction is the whole discipline. Tesla scores 8 because the assets — the largest camera-equipped over-the-air fleet in the world, a running autonomous ride-hailing service, custom inference silicon with booked capacity, a humanoid programme, and a scaling storage business — are exactly the ingredients a step-change requires.
