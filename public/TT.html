The Overview

Trane makes air conditioning and heating systems — the big chillers on top of office buildings, home units, and the refrigeration on trucks and trailers. It also services what it sells, and that servicing is now 34% of its revenue, which matters because service is steadier and usually more profitable than selling machines.

The business is growing well. Last quarter, sales rose 10.6%, and Trane helpfully told us why: about 9 points was genuine growth in the underlying business (mostly more units sold), about 1 point came from companies it bought, and a fraction from currency. In the Americas — five-sixths of the business — genuine growth was nearly 11%.

Here is the problem, and it is the only thing that matters in this report. Sales grew 10.6%. Profits grew 6.5%.

Normally a company like this makes more profit per extra dollar of sales, because the factories and salespeople are already paid for. This quarter the opposite happened. The gross profit margin — what is left after making the product — fell from 37.6 cents on the dollar to 35.6 cents. Every one of the three geographic divisions became less profitable. Europe was worst: sales actually shrank, and its profit margin fell from 18.3% to 14.7%.

The company's explanation is one clause: "inflation and investments." That may be right, and investments made today can pay off later. But it is not much of an explanation for losing two full points of gross margin.

Meanwhile, analysts still expect Trane to earn $15.14 a share this year. It earned $6.94 in the first half. So they need $8.20 in the second half against $6.74 a year ago — a 22% jump — from a company whose profits just grew 6.5%. That looks like a forecast that has not caught up.

The shares cost $472.24, or about 27 times what analysts expect for 2027. Twenty-six analysts cover it and only eleven say Buy; fourteen say Hold and one says Sell. Over the last twelve months the stock is up 7.5% while the market is up 24%.

Our estimate of fair value is $485 — about 3% above the price. That is a Hold.

  • Downside Risk 4/10. Little debt, strong cash generation, one-third recurring service revenue.
  • Growth Quality 6/10. 9% genuine growth — that is not reaching the profit line.
  • Exponential Potential 3/10. An excellent business with a linear slope.

Putting a number on it: our fair-value estimate is $485 against a current price of $453.64 — real upside if our numbers are right.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, plus an independent, public-math cross-check further down the page (“Check our number”) so you can judge it for yourself.

2. The margin problem, in the company's own words

The disclosure is short and it is the whole issue.

> "Gross profit margin for the three months ended June 30, 2026 decreased 200 basis points to 35.6% compared to 37.6% for the same period of 2025 primarily due to inflation and investments, partially offset by productivity and price realization."

> Americas: "Segment Adjusted EBITDA margin for the three months ended June 30, 2026 and 2025 decreased by 50 basis points to 23.5% compared to 24.0% for the same period of 2025, primarily due to inflation and investments, partially offset by productivity and pricing."

Three observations.

First, pricing was positive and it did not save the margin. The company's own revenue bridge gives pricing +0.3% as a contributor to the 10.6% increase. A business realising price and still losing 200 basis points of gross margin is absorbing cost faster than it can pass it on, and "inflation" is doing more work in that sentence than "investments."

Second, the offset came from below the gross line, not above it. Selling and administrative expenses grew only 3.9% against revenue growth of 10.6%, falling 100 basis points to 16.3% of revenue. So Trane held the line on overhead and still could not hold segment margin — 80 basis points of consolidated segment EBITDA margin was lost. That narrows the problem to cost of goods.

Third, EMEA is a separate and worse problem. Net revenues fell 1.5%, and the decomposition shows volume −4.1%, pricing +0.4% (organic −3.7%), acquisitions +0.1%, currency +2.1% — so EMEA is shrinking in constant currency and the reported decline understates it. Segment EBITDA fell 21.1% and margin fell 360 basis points to 14.7%, against the Americas' 23.5%. EMEA is 11.0% of revenue and 7.2% of segment profit.

What this archive does NOT contain and we do not estimate around: the earnings release itself. The 8-K of 2026-07-30 states only that the company "issued a press release announcing its second quarter 2026 results", and the exhibit is not in the extracted text. So there is no management guidance of any kind available for FY2026 — no revenue guide, no EPS guide, no margin commentary beyond the 10-Q's single clause. Every forward figure in this dive is consensus.
