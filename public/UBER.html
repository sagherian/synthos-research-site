The Overview

Uber does not own cars. It runs the app that connects people who want a ride, or a meal, with people willing to provide one, and takes a cut. It does the same for freight. That means it can grow a great deal without buying anything, which is why it generated $9.8 billion of spare cash last year while spending only $336 million on equipment.

The business is growing well. In the first three months of this year, 199 million people used it each month and took 3.6 billion trips. The total value of everything booked through the app rose 25%. Operating profit rose 57%.

Reported revenue only rose 14%, and the company explains why: a change in how the United Kingdom treats ride-hailing for tax purposes removed $1.0 billion of revenue in a single quarter. Without that, revenue grew about 23%. The same change has also produced a $1.8 billion tax assessment from the UK authorities covering earlier years, which is not settled.

The shares cost $71.99 — 28% below their high of the past year, and down 17% over twelve months while the stock market rose 24%. That is a large gap and it exists for one reason: self-driving cars.

The argument against Uber is simple. If a robotaxi costs much less to run than a human driver, and the company that owns the robotaxi can run its own app, why would it pay Uber? One well-regarded analyst in our research base puts the cost advantage at roughly 60%.

The argument for Uber is also simple, and there is a number attached to it. Fleets that plug into Uber's network are already reported to be about 30% busier than fleets operating alone, because Uber has the customers. If that holds, fleet owners join Uber for the same reason drivers do — more paid miles.

Nobody knows which is right. You can see that in the forecasts: analysts' estimates of what Uber earns in 2028 range from $3.82 to $7.25 per share. That is not a forecast; it is a shrug.

Here is why we are buying anyway. At $71.99, you pay about 16 times what Uber is expected to earn in 2027. The company is generating cash equal to 6.7% of its value every year and using it to buy back 4.5% of itself annually. Our estimate of fair value is $89, about 24% above the price, and if we are wrong the downside looks like 32% while the upside looks like 74%.

One practical instruction: Uber reports results tomorrow. We are not going to pretend we know what they will say. Buy after the number, not before it.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, We don’t publish a reverse-DCF cross-check for pre-profit companies — negative or missing earnings break that math — so take this number on our modeling alone.

2. The Delivery Hero offer — the full agreement, and the sentence that matters

On 2026-07-16 Uber entered into a Business Combination Agreement with Delivery Hero SE. The complete archive carries the full Item 1.01 text; the key terms:

  • Structure: a voluntary public takeover offer under Section 29(1) of the German Securities Acquisition and Takeover Act, made by Uber International Technologies II Corporation, a wholly-owned Delaware subsidiary, for all no-par registered shares of Delivery Hero SE.
  • Price: EUR 41.50 per share, in CASH.
  • Board support: both the management board (Vorstand) and the supervisory board (Aufsichtsrat) of Delivery Hero have unanimously resolved to approve entry into the agreement, and the management board has undertaken to recommend acceptance subject to fiduciary duties and a fairness opinion.
  • Conditions: specified competition approvals, specified financial-services regulatory approvals, and a minimum tender of at least 50% of Delivery Hero shares plus one share, excluding treasury.
  • Timing: "The Offer is expected to be completed in the second half of 2027." A long-dated transaction with an outside regulatory date of 2028-05-10 in the termination provisions.
  • Break fees: EUR 200 million payable by Delivery Hero if a competing offer emerges and its boards withdraw support, or if the boards fail to support as agreed; a reciprocal fee runs the other way.
  • And the sentence that matters most for this dive: "The Company currently anticipates that the transaction will be funded primarily with existing cash balances and debt."

Why that last clause is the operative fact.
