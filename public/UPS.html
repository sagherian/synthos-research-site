The Overview

UPS moves packages. For decades the way it grew was to move more of them. It has now decided, on purpose, to move fewer.

The reason is Amazon. Amazon was about one dollar in every nine that UPS collected, and it was low-margin work. UPS agreed to cut that volume by more than half, finishing in June 2026. It also handed a chunk of its cheapest delivery product to the Post Office to carry the last mile. And it bought two healthcare-logistics companies, because moving temperature-controlled medicine pays far better than moving a box of socks.

So revenue fell for three straight years and profit fell faster — from about $13 billion of operating profit in 2022 to under $8 billion in 2025 — because a network with fixed costs loses money quickly when volume leaves. The company's answer is to shrink the network to fit: closing buildings, cutting staff, and spending about $3 billion less per year on capital projects than it did in 2023.

In the quarter just reported, revenue finally grew again — up 7.6%. But reported profit fell by half, because of about $1.2 billion in costs from a programme paying drivers to leave. The company asks you to look past that. It has told you in advance it would happen, which is fair.

The problem is the dividend. UPS pays $6.56 a share, which at $109 is a 6% yield — one of the highest in the S&P 500. Last year it paid $5.4 billion of dividends and generated $4.8 billion of free cash. It made up the difference and, notably, stopped buying back its own stock — the 10-K says plainly that no repurchases are anticipated in 2026. That is a company protecting a dividend, not a company with spare cash.

We think the stock is worth about $112 against a $109 price. That is close enough to nothing that the honest answer is: hold if you own it for the yield and understand the yield is tight; there is no edge here for anyone else.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

1. What the business is, and what it is deliberately giving up

United Parcel Service operates through U.S. Domestic Package, International Package and Supply Chain Solutions, with approximately 460,000 employees, chief executive Carol Tomé, and $41,994M of net property, plant and equipment on a $73,090M balance sheet.

The strategic fact that governs everything else is in the 10-K, stated without euphemism:

> "In 2025, one customer, Amazon.com, Inc. and its affiliates, represented approximately 10.6% of our consolidated revenues, substantially all of which was within our U.S. Domestic Package segment. As previously disclosed, our strategy involves reducing volumes from this customer by more than 50% by June 2026 from 2024 levels."

Amazon was 11.8% of revenue in each of 2023 and 2024 and 10.6% in 2025, and 13.6% of accounts receivable. The reduction completes in the month before the quarter just reported, which makes the September 2026 print the first clean look at the post-Amazon revenue base.

Three other structural changes, all from the filings:

  • The USPS arrangement. "In December 2025, we entered into an agreement with the United States Postal Service ('USPS') to support final-mile delivery for a portion of our Ground Saver and Mail Innovations volumes starting in 2026." In Q1 2026 this raised facility and transportation costs by $341M through "higher fees paid to the USPS" while lowering direct labour by $423M — and it is the reason cost per piece rose 9.7% in the quarter, because transition costs and "excess operational staffing" ran alongside the handoff.
  • Healthcare. UPS completed the acquisitions of Frigo-Trans and Biotech & Pharma Logistics and Andlauer Healthcare Group ("AHG") in 2025, both reported within SCS. "In 2025, our global healthcare portfolio generated more than $11 billion in revenue." In Q1 2026, healthcare logistics revenue rose $188 million year on year "primarily due to our fourth quarter 2025 acquisition of AHG." Vendor cf_a records −$1,968M of acquisitionsNet in FY2025, consistent.
  • Network Reconfiguration and Efficiency Reimagined. "In the first quarter of 2026, we closed 23 leased and owned buildings, 22 of which have been permanently closed as of March 31, 2026. We have identified 27 additional buildings for closure in 2026... In the first three months of 2026, we achieved approximately $600 million of program cost savings, and expect to achieve approximately $3 billion in full year-over-year cost savings from this initiative in 2026." Cost to achieve: "between $1.3 and $1.5 billion in cost during 2026, primarily related to employee separation benefits and third-party consulting fees, of which $1.2 billion will be related to the Driver Choice Program." The programmes "are expected to conclude by 2027."

Note what that last disclosure does. It was filed on 2026-05-06 and it pre-announces the exact charge that produced the June quarter's GAAP/adjusted divergence. A $1.2 billion pre-tax Driver Choice charge is roughly $0.95 billion after tax at the trailing 22.07% effective rate, or approximately $1.12 per share on 851 million shares — against an observed GAAP-to-adjusted gap of $1.05 per share in the quarter. The arithmetic ties. This is the rare case where a large adjustment is fully explained by a prior filing, and it should be credited as such.

6b. The return bridge (why the multiple moves)

Expected return over the next twelve months decomposes as: EPS growth (+11.8% FY2026, +7.5% FY2027) + multiple drift (roughly HELD; our base of $112 is 13.95x FY2027E against 13.6x today, +2.5%) + dividend yield (+6.01%) ≈ +14% to +16% on the consensus path — of which more than 40% is the dividend.

Our base assumes the multiple holds. It does not assume expansion, and that is the reason the fair value lands where it does. There is a legitimate re-rating argument — a network that has finished shrinking, with a growing healthcare vertical and $3 billion less cost, ought to earn more than 13.6x. There is an equally legitimate de-rating argument — a company whose operating income has fallen 39% in three years and whose dividend consumes all of its cash ought to earn less. We resolve it by holding the multiple and letting the earnings do the work, which produces a fair value 2.7% above spot, which is a Hold.

11. Verdict, kill-criteria and flip conditions

Hold.

The arithmetic that produces it. At $109.095, UPS trades at 15.2x the FY2026 consensus of $7.18 and 13.6x FY2027's $8.03, with a 6.01% dividend yield. Our base fair value of $112 is 2.7% above spot — inside the band where this programme declines to have a view — with asymmetry of 1.23:1 ($88 bear, $135 bull). The street is at $119 with a Hold consensus label and twenty holds against twenty-one buys. Nobody has an edge here, including us, and the correct response to that is to say so.

What is genuinely good. The cost programme is real, disclosed, quantified and already delivering — ~$600M in Q1 against a ~$3 billion 2026 target, 23 buildings closed with 27 identified, capex down from $5,158M in 2023 to a guided ~$3.0 billion. The June quarter grew revenue 7.6% in the month the Amazon glidedown completed, which is better than the shrinking-network story predicted. Healthcare logistics — more than $11 billion of 2025 revenue after AHG and Frigo-Trans — is the one line improving on merit, and it drove SCS operating profit up $159M while both package segments lost 270-320 basis points of margin. And the cash-flow data, the field most often corrupt in this feed, is verified correct.

What keeps it at Hold.

First, the dividend consumes all of the cash. $6.56 per share is 91% of FY2026 consensus EPS, 122% of trailing GAAP EPS and ~102% of trailing free cash flow; FY2025 paid $5.4 billion against $4,765M of free cash flow. Net debt rose $6.86 billion in 2025. And the company has told shareholders in writing that it "do[es] not anticipate further share repurchases in 2026" with $1.3 billion of authorisation unused. That is what protecting a dividend looks like from the inside.

Second, the GAAP and adjusted numbers tell opposite stories and have for four consecutive programmes. Q2 2026 GAAP operating income −49%; adjusted EPS +13.5%. The $1.05 per share gap is fully explained by the pre-announced $1.2 billion Driver Choice Program, and we credit that. But Transformation 2.0 ran into Fit to Serve which ran into Network Reconfiguration which ran into Efficiency Reimagined, and $1.3-1.5 billion more is guided for 2026. At some point a permanent adjustment is a cost.

Third, the growth is not volume. International domestic volume −6.6%, export −5.5%; revenue per piece +10.7%. Revenue has fallen in three of four years and operating income in all four. The consensus path to $8.03 in FY2027 is a margin path, and margins in the two package segments are currently going the wrong way.

Fourth, the knowledge base is net negative — three bearish claims to one bullish, and the bullish one is conviction 48 about a mechanism the company has never claimed.

Pre-registered KILL criteria — what would take this to Avoid:

  • A dividend cut, or any Board language qualifying the commitment to $1.64 per quarter. The yield is the thesis for most holders; without it this is a shrinking industrial on 13.6x.
  • The ~$3 billion 2026 cost-savings target revised down, or Q3 cumulative savings tracking below ~$1.8 billion.
  • U.S. Domestic Package operating margin below 4% in the September quarter, i.e. no recovery after the Amazon glidedown completed in June.
  • A goodwill impairment in Global Freight Forwarding ($877M) or Healthcare Logistics and Distribution ($738M), both explicitly flagged in the 10-Q.
  • A fourth transformation programme announced for 2027-28, which would establish that the adjustments are structural.
  • Cost per piece still rising year on year in the December quarter — it rose 9.7% in Q1 2026.

Pre-registered UPGRADE conditions — what would take this to Buy — Tactical:

  • Free cash flow covering the dividend with room, i.e. FY2026 free cash flow above roughly $6.2 billion, which at ~$3.0 billion of capex requires operating cash flow near $9.2 billion.
  • The buyback resuming, which is the single cleanest statement the Board can make about coverage.
  • U.S. Domestic Package margin back above 7% with cost per piece falling year on year.
  • Separate disclosure of the healthcare portfolio's revenue and margin. More than $11 billion of cold-chain logistics valued at parcel multiples is the clearest mispricing candidate in this file, and it is currently invisible.
  • A price below roughly $95 on non-fundamental weakness, which at a maintained dividend is a 6.9% yield and would restore the asymmetry to better than 2:1.

Where UPS fits in the Synthos Framework Portfolio. No position. This is an income holding masquerading as an industrial, and the income is not covered. If the buyback resumes or free cash flow covers the dividend with a margin, it becomes a legitimate 1.5-2% yield sleeve position at these prices; until then there is no reason to own it in preference to a bond.
