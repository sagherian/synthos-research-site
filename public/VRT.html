The Overview

Every data centre needs two things besides computers: electricity delivered safely, and heat taken away. Vertiv makes both — switchgear, uninterruptible power supplies, chillers, and now the liquid-cooling systems that dense artificial-intelligence servers require.

Business is very good. Sales last quarter were up 24%, and — this is the useful bit, because Vertiv actually told us — about 18% of that was real growth rather than companies it bought or currency movements. Profit margins improved sharply: the company now keeps 37.7 cents of gross profit per dollar of sales, up from 34.0 cents a year ago. The Americas division, which is about two-thirds of the business, grew 29% and its profits grew 49%.

The company also cleaned up its debts in March. It paid off its old bank loan, replaced its secured borrowing facility with a much larger unsecured one, and issued bonds that do not mature until as late as 2066. It now has more cash than debt.

The shares cost $269.93. That is 29.7 times what analysts expect the company to earn in 2027, for a company they expect to grow earnings 36% that year. Twenty analysts cover it: nineteen say Buy, one says Hold, none says Sell, and their average target is $357.47 — a third above the current price. Even the most pessimistic target, $277, is above where the stock trades.

So why not Buy?

Because of one number. Last quarter Vertiv earned more than expected but sold less than expected — revenue came in 3.1% below forecasts. Management said the cause was temporary: shipping congestion and the way big projects get phased. That may well be right. But for a company valued on how fast it can grow, a revenue shortfall is the miss that matters, and the shares have fallen 28% from their high because of it.

Two more honest notes. The European business shrank 8.4% in the first half. And the shares are extremely volatile — they traded between $121.82 and $376.23 in the last twelve months, a range of 209%.

Our estimate of fair value is $310, about 15% above the price. That is well below the analysts' $357.47, and we would rather wait for one more quarter of evidence that the sales shortfall really was temporary. The price at which we would stop waiting is named at the end.

1. What the business is

Vertiv Holdings Co is a Delaware corporation headquartered in Westerville, Ohio, NYSE-listed since 2018, with approximately 34,000 employees, led by Chief Executive Giordano Albertazzi. It designs, manufactures and services the physical infrastructure of data centres and communications networks: power distribution, uninterruptible power supplies, thermal management (air and increasingly liquid), racks and integrated modular systems, plus a service and spares business.

The service line is the annuity underneath the equipment, and it grew $148.7 million in the June quarter and $282.1 million in the half. The archive does not disclose its margin, which is the most useful undisclosed number in this file — service revenue in an installed-base business is normally materially higher-margin than equipment, and 18.0% of revenue at a premium margin would be a meaningful share of profit.

What management says is changing, quoted because the specifics matter:

> "We continue to see very robust growth in demand for data centers supporting artificial intelligence ('AI') and high-performance compute applications and have strategically invested in expanding our global capacity in response to current and anticipated customer demand across key infrastructure segments. For the quarter, our capital investments were significantly higher than the spend in the same quarter of 2025."

> "Customer requirements are evolving toward higher-density, more complex infrastructure environments, including, but not limited to, hybrid air and liquid cooling architectures, converged physical infrastructure systems, and high voltage direct current power architectures."

> "Customers are increasingly prioritizing speed of deployment, scalability and execution certainty. We continue to invest in prefabricated, modular and factory-integrated solutions designed to reduce on-site complexity and accelerate time-to-deployment."

Half-weighted as management framing — but the three architecture changes named in the middle quotation are the substance of the exponential case and are worth separating out. Hybrid air-and-liquid cooling, converged physical infrastructure, and high-voltage direct-current power are each a change of specification rather than a change of quantity. Each obsoletes part of an installed base, each raises the equipment content per megawatt, and each favours a supplier that can sell the whole stack and then service it. None of them is quantified anywhere in this archive.

The organic disclosure is the best thing in this filing and it deserves to be stated as a headline. Vertiv separates acquisition revenue and currency in plain language: $636.2 million of growth, of which $129.7 million was acquired and $35.9 million was currency, leaves $470.6 million on a base of $2,638.1 million — 17.8% to 18.2% depending on the base convention. Very few companies in this programme give the reader that split without being asked, and PWR, covered in this same batch, does not.

Four observations.

First, the margin expansion is real and its cause is disclosed. Gross margin rose 370 basis points to 37.7% in the quarter, attributed to "the mix of product and service sales", and over six months to "the mix of product and service sales in addition to operational leverage and improved project execution." Meanwhile SG&A grew only 10 basis points as a share of sales. That combination — gross margin up 370 basis points with operating expense flat as a percentage — is what produces a 48.6% increase in Americas operating profit on a 29.2% revenue increase.

Second, the "other operating expense" line grew $44.0 million and its composition is unflattering. The increase was "primarily due to a $26.8 increase in amortization of intangibles and a $21.4 increase in other operating expense (income), primarily due to the contingent consideration related to our recent acquisitions." In the corporate line, this is explicit: total corporate costs of $79.6 million in the quarter, up $20.9 million, "primarily due to the $28.8 loss on the change in fair value of contingent consideration associated with the PurgeRite acquisition", and $173.0 million in the half including $62.0 million of the same. A contingent-consideration charge means the acquired business is beating its earn-out targets — good news arriving as a cost — but it is a real charge and it is growing.
