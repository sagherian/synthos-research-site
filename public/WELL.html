The Overview

Welltower owns buildings where old people live and receive care — retirement communities, assisted living, some medical offices — in America, Britain and Canada. Increasingly it does not just own them and collect rent; it runs them, which means it keeps the profit when occupancy and prices rise, and wears the cost when staffing gets expensive. Nearly 80% of its revenue now comes from buildings it operates rather than leases out.

The business is growing very fast. Revenue rose 36% last year and 41% in the three months to June. That is the fastest of any of the twelve companies in this group. Most of it comes from buying more buildings — and in July the company said it had bought or was about to buy more than $5 billion more in the second half of this year.

The reason to be interested is demographic and simple: the number of people over eighty is rising sharply in all three of its markets, and almost nobody has been building this kind of housing because it costs about $450,000 per apartment and financing has been hard to get. Fixed supply, rising demand.

The reason to be careful is how it is paid for. Welltower issues new shares to buy buildings. The number of shares has risen from 605 million to 732 million in two years — 21% more owners sharing the same pie. Sales grew much faster than that, so it has probably been worthwhile, but there is a measurement problem, and it is serious.

Property companies are not valued on ordinary profits, because accounting depreciation on buildings is enormous and largely fictional — buildings do not usually fall in value. The industry uses a measure called funds from operations instead. Our data source provides no such figure for Welltower. The company's own quarterly filing does contain it, but the table it sits in was not captured when the document was processed, so we do not have it.

So we can say the shares look expensive on everything we can measure — about 53 times operating cash flow per share, 13 times sales, and a dividend yield of only 1.3% when this kind of company usually yields 4-6% — but we cannot say what they cost relative to the number the market actually uses. We are telling you that rather than inventing an answer.

The shares are $231.57, up 39% over the past year — the best of these twelve. Our estimate is $232, essentially the current price.

  • Downside Risk 6/10. Real buildings, no accounting goodwill, conservative debt — against constant share issuance and a price we cannot measure properly.
  • Growth Quality 8/10. The fastest growth here. Bought, not organic, and diluted.
  • Exponential Potential 5/10. A genuine multi-decade demographic wave, captured through a vehicle that must keep issuing shares to ride it.

Putting a number on it: our fair-value estimate is $232 against a current price of $231.57 — real upside if our numbers are right.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, plus an independent, public-math cross-check further down the page (“Check our number”) so you can judge it for yourself.

Seniors Housing OPERATING at 79.6% of revenue is the single most important structural fact about this company and it is often misunderstood. In a triple-net lease the REIT collects a fixed rent and the operator takes the business risk. In an operating structure, Welltower consolidates the property's revenues and expenses — so it captures the upside when occupancy and rate rise, and it wears the staffing and cost risk when they do not. That is why revenue can grow 40% and why the operating margin is volatile.

seg_geo ties cleanly. United States $7,937M + United Kingdom $2,167M + Canada $734M = $10,838M against reported revenue of $10,667M — a 1.6% over-sum, which is within the tolerance created by intersegment and unconsolidated presentation. The United Kingdom at 20.3% of revenue is unusually large for a US-listed healthcare REIT and is not reflected in any commentary in this file.

The acquisition programme, from the 10-Q filed 2026-07-28 — no vendor field contains it:

> "In July 2026, we announced that we have closed or expect to close more than $5 billion of seniors housing acquisitions in the second half of the year. Expected acquisitions not yet closed are subject to customary closing conditions and regulatory approvals."

More than $5 billion in one half, against a $166.9 billion market capitalisation and total assets of $67.3 billion. That is a roughly 7.4% expansion of the asset base in six months, on top of a 35.8% revenue increase in the prior year.

Two further filing disclosures with no vendor equivalent. Construction conversions in the first half were $257,446,000 at $450,080 per unit — the replacement cost that constitutes the supply barrier. And impairments were $27,181,000 on 12 properties in the first half of 2026, down from $33.8 million a year earlier — very small against a $67.3 billion asset base, and a genuine sign of portfolio health.

Same-store net operating income is disclosed by segment across 980 same-store Seniors Housing Operating properties, 499 triple-net properties and a further pool in Outpatient Medical. The figures themselves sit in tables that were not preserved in extraction, so we can confirm the disclosure exists and cannot report the numbers.

Correction 1 — propertyPlantEquipmentNet of $2,158M against $67,303M of total assets is not a usable description of a REIT. Welltower's business is owning buildings. The vendor's PP&E line captures 3.2% of the asset base; the real-estate investments are evidently mapped to other line items the payload does not surface. No asset-composition conclusion in this dive uses the vendor's balance-sheet classification. What we can say from it, and it is genuinely creditable, is that goodwill and intangibles are BOTH ZERO — Welltower carries no acquisition premium at all on $67.3 billion of assets, which is rare and is a real quality signal.

Correction 2 — netDebt is arithmetically correct at its own date and the date is the problem. Welltower carries $0 of short-term investments, so the standard omission cannot occur: $21,380M less $5,034M = $16,347M. This is one of only three names in this batch where that field needed no construction repair. But the balance sheet is 2025-12-31, and the company has since disclosed more than $5 billion of second-half acquisitions on top of whatever closed in the first half. There is no filing-verified balance sheet later than 2026-06-30 available in extracted form, because the WELL archive preserved no tables.

Correction 3 — interestCoverageRatioTTM of 0.92x is a depreciation artefact and is REJECTED. It is computed on GAAP operating income of $355M against interest expense of $652M. But depreciation and amortisation was $2,139M in 2025 — six times operating income — and for a property company that charge is largely non-economic. Adding it back gives roughly $2.5 billion against $652M of interest, or 3.8x coverage. The vendor's 0.92x would, at face value, describe a company unable to pay its interest; Welltower generated $2,882M of operating cash flow in the same year. We reject the field.

Correction 4 — priceToEarningsDilutedRatioTTM of 122.5x, returnOnEquityTTM of 3.15%, returnOnInvestedCapitalTTM of 0.96% and dividendPayoutRatioTTM of 152.4% are all the same artefact and are all REJECTED. They divide by a GAAP net income that depreciation has crushed. For a REIT these fields are not merely unhelpful, they are actively misleading, and the vendor's own composite rating scores WELL 1 out of 5 on both price-to-earnings and price-to-book as a direct result, dragging it to B− / 3. We reject the composite.

What the balance sheet does support, on any reading: debt-to-equity of 0.425x, $5.034B of cash, zero goodwill, and impairments of $27.2M on 12 properties in the first half against $67.3B of assets. This is a conservatively financed property company.
