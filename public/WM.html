The Overview

Waste Management takes away rubbish. Households, shops, factories and city governments pay it to do so, on multi-year contracts with prices that rise automatically each year. It then buries most of what it collects in landfills it owns — and nobody is allowed to build new landfills, which is why the ones Waste Management already has are so valuable.

It is a very good business and it has been a very disappointing share for a year. The stock has fallen 1.6% while the market rose 24%.

Two things explain that.

The first is an acquisition. In November 2024 Waste Management paid about $7.5 billion for Stericycle, which collects medical waste from hospitals and clinics. The purchase was funded with debt, so the company now owes about $22.7 billion — roughly three years of its earnings before interest, tax and depreciation — and it has warned in its own annual report that credit rating agencies are watching. The medical waste business, meanwhile, actually lost money at the operating level in the first half of this year.

The second is volume. Read the fine print of the latest quarterly filing and the company explains that its fuel bill went down partly because it is driving less — "declines in collection volumes." It is collecting less rubbish. Revenue is still growing, but only because prices go up, not because there is more work.

What is genuinely working is cost. The company has cut headcount and overheads since buying Stericycle, and its selling and administrative costs actually fell compared to last year. That is where the profit growth is coming from. It has also restarted buying back its own shares — $1 billion in six months.

You pay about 25 times what analysts think the company will earn in 2027. For a business growing profits about 12% a year with debt at three times earnings and shrinking volumes, that is a fair price rather than a bargain. We think it is worth about $240 against $225.

That is not enough to buy. It is enough to keep on a list, with a price in mind.

  • Downside Risk 5/10. Extremely defensive operations; debt at 3.0x and no tangible asset backing.
  • Growth Quality 4/10. Growth is price and cost, not volume — and volume is falling.
  • Exponential Potential 3/10. Landfill gas converted to renewable natural gas is a genuine option on an otherwise linear business.

Putting a number on it: our fair-value estimate is $240 against a current price of $225.25 — real upside if our numbers are right.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, plus an independent, public-math cross-check further down the page (“Check our number”) so you can judge it for yourself.

1. What the business is, and the acquisition that reshaped it

Waste Management provides collection, transfer, disposal, recycling and — since November 2024 — medical-waste and secure-information-destruction services across North America and, newly, internationally. Approximately 60,500 employees, chief executive James C. Fish Jr., NYSE-listed since 1988, beta 0.437.

The Stericycle acquisition is the organising fact of the last two years. From the 10-K's risk factors: "Our November 2024 acquisition of Stericycle increased our exposure to international jurisdictions, primarily Western Europe, and we are subject to laws and regulations in the U.S. and internationally concerning transportation, management and disposal or destruction of medical waste streams, including regulations that govern the definition, generation, segregation, handling, packaging, transportation, treatment, storage and disposal or destruction of medical waste and controlled substances waste... Additionally, our secure information destruction services are subject to laws and regulations regarding proper handling and protection of personal and confidential information."

The two disclosures that define the medium-term question

On volumes, from the 10-Q's operating-cost discussion, verbatim:

> "The increase in fuel costs was primarily due to the increases in average market prices for diesel fuel previously mentioned. This increase was partially offset by reduced fuel consumption due to declines in collection volumes."

On Healthcare Solutions, verbatim:

> "Our Healthcare Solutions segment generated income from operations in the three months ended June 30, 2026 and a loss from operations in the six months ended June 30, 2026. The improvement across both time periods is primarily due to non-recurring integration costs incurred during the prior year periods along with lower costs in the current year from the realization of synergies, offset by lower volumes."

Read those two together and the situation is precise. Volumes are declining in the core collection business AND in the acquired medical-waste business. The earnings are being produced by price escalators and by cost removal. That is a perfectly legitimate way to grow earnings and it has a natural end point.

On the cost side the news is genuinely good, and it is the offsetting fact:

> "Selling, general and administrative expenses decreased for the three months ended June 30, 2026, as compared to the prior year period, primarily due to decreases in professional fees and lower labor costs resulting from synergies achieved from our acquisitions, particularly Stericycle, partially offset by higher bad debt expenses."

> "The decrease in labor and related benefits costs for the three months ended June 30, 2026... was primarily due to a reduction in workforce as we achieved synergies from our acquisitions, particularly Stericycle, and lower annual incentive compensation costs."

Note the one cautionary detail inside the good news: "higher bad debt expenses" appears in both the three-month and six-month SG&A discussions, and "increases in bad debt expenses" is named first among the drivers of the six-month increase. Rising bad debt in a waste business is a customer-quality signal and it is worth watching.

What keeps it at Watch rather than Buy.

First, volumes are declining and the filing says so twice. "[R]educed fuel consumption due to declines in collection volumes" and, for Healthcare Solutions, "lower volumes." Revenue growth has decelerated for three consecutive quarters — +14.9%, +7.1%, +3.5%, +3.9% — and revenue MISSED the estimate in three of the last four quarters, while consensus wants +5.5% in the September quarter. A fixed-cost network with falling volume is the exact configuration that has taken UPS's operating income down 39% in three years, elsewhere in this batch.

Second, the earnings growth is entirely cost. Consensus needs 400-600 basis points of margin-driven growth above a 5% revenue line, sourced from a synergy programme with a finite life.

Third, leverage is elevated by choice and the company has warned about it. Net debt $22,706M, 3.03x EBITDA, $201M of cash — eight days of revenue — and the 10-K's own words: "If we do not achieve our projected reduction in leverage following our acquisition of Stericycle, it could negatively impact our credit ratings." Interest expense rose 52.5% in FY2025 and was the principal reason EPS fell on 14.2% revenue growth.

Fourth, the asymmetry is 1.85:1 against a 2:1 requirement, and the base case needs a re-rating.

Fifth, the knowledge base is empty and the street's price-target block is one number in four fields.
