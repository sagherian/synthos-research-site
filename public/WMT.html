The Overview

Walmart is the largest retailer on earth. It sold about $713 billion of goods last year through supercentres, neighbourhood markets, Sam's Club warehouses and its websites, employs about 2.1 million people, and it is still growing sales at more than 4% a year — which at that size is genuinely hard to do.

Two things are going right. First, its online business is booming: online sales reached about $150 billion last year, up 24%, and are now more than a fifth of everything it sells. Second, it has started charging for things other than goods — Walmart+ and Sam's Club memberships brought in $4.4 billion in fees, and it sells advertising to the brands on its shelves and its website. That second point is the whole investment argument: selling groceries earns a few cents on the dollar, selling advertising earns a lot, so as the mix shifts the company's profitability should rise sharply — and that is what justifies paying a high price for the shares.

Here is the problem, and it is the reason this is a Hold rather than a Buy. After five years of that shift, Walmart's actual profit margin went down last year, not up — by a small amount, but down. It went down again in the most recent quarter. And when you look at why last year's reported profit-per-share rose 13%, most of it was not the shops at all: it was gains on investments the company happens to hold, which can just as easily go the other way.

Meanwhile, the price is high. You pay about $38.50 for every dollar of profit the company is expected to make next year. For a supermarket growing profits at about 11% a year, that is a lot. And the cash actually left over after Walmart rebuilds its stores and warehouses has shrunk — it now spends nearly twice as much on buildings and equipment each year as those assets wear out.

The shares have fallen 17.5% from their high, which sounds like an opportunity. But they had risen a very long way first, and a 17.5% fall from a very high starting multiple leaves you at a merely high multiple. Our estimate of what the shares are worth is $110 against a price of $111.55 — essentially the same number. Wall Street thinks $140. We do not. None of this means Walmart is a bad company; it is an outstanding one. It means that at this price you are being asked to pay in advance for an improvement the company has not yet delivered.

  • Downside Risk 5/10. The business is about as safe as a listed equity gets — beta 0.60, 1.3x leverage, 10.5x interest cover. The stock is not: the risk is the multiple falling, and it has been falling.
  • Growth Quality 6/10. Sales growth is excellent and real. Profit growth is flattered by investment gains and is not reaching the shareholder.
  • Exponential Potential 3/10. The advertising and membership layer is genuinely growing at 24%. It has not yet moved the consolidated number the price depends on.

Putting a number on it: our fair-value estimate is $110 against a current price of $111.55 — a premium price for a business we still like.

What does “fair value” mean?

Fair value is Synthos’s estimate of what one share is worth today, based on our model of the company’s future cash generation and the risks to it. It is not a price target or a prediction of where the stock trades next quarter — it’s the price where we think risk and reward are balanced. Above it, you’re paying for outcomes better than our base case; below it, the market is offering a margin of safety.

The model’s inputs and weightings are proprietary — they’re the product. What we publish is the output, plus an independent, public-math cross-check further down the page (“Check our number”) so you can judge it for yourself.

Walmart's entire premium multiple rests on the claim that a mix shift into high-margin non-retail revenue is re-rating the company's profitability. The filings say it has not happened yet.

Operating income as a percentage of net sales decreased in both of the last two reported periods. Management's own stated objective, in its own words, is "to achieve operating income leverage, which we define as growing operating income at a faster rate than net sales." It is currently doing the opposite. Fiscal 2026 net sales grew 4.7% and operating income grew 1.6% ($29.825B against $29.348B). Q1 fiscal 2027 net sales grew 7.3% and operating income grew 5.0% ($7.493B against $7.135B).

The gross margin is behaving as the bull case predicts. The operating margin is not. Gross profit rate rose in every period — the 10-K attributes it to "disciplined inventory management, as well as growth in higher margin businesses globally," and the 10-Q to "merchandise mix shifts and growth in higher margin businesses, including advertising." So the high-margin revenue is real and is showing up above the line. It is being consumed below it: fiscal 2026 operating expenses rose 20 basis points on $0.9B of higher self-insured general liability claims expense, the $0.7B PhonePe share-based compensation charge, and increased depreciation from the capital programme. Q1 fiscal 2027's Walmart U.S. operating expenses rose 56 basis points on depreciation, associate healthcare costs and business reorganisation charges.

Now the earnings-quality problem, which compounds it. Reported diluted earnings per share grew 13.3% in fiscal 2026 ($2.73 against $2.41) while operating income grew 1.6%. 6b. The return bridge (why the multiple moves)

Expected return over 24 months decomposes as: consensus EPS growth (+11-12% a year) + multiple drift (our base assumes COMPRESSION, from 38.5x forward to ~33.6x) + shareholder yield (~1.8%: 0.89% dividend, ~0.9% buyback).

Our base case assumes multiple compression, and that is why a stock with double-digit consensus earnings growth carries a fair value at spot. The arithmetic is stark and worth writing out: at an unchanged 38.5x on fiscal 2028 consensus earnings of $3.274, the price is $126 — a 13% gain requiring no re-rating whatsoever, only the absence of a de-rating. At our base multiple of 33.6x it is $110. At 27x it is $88.

So the entire disagreement between us and the street is a multiple assumption, not a forecast. We are not below consensus on earnings; we use consensus. We are below consensus on what those earnings are worth.

Why we assume compression rather than a hold. Three reasons, each numeric. First, the operating margin is contracting (−13bp, −8bp) while consensus requires it to expand (+37bp over three years) — the direction of travel and the direction of the assumption are opposed. Second, the earnings that grew 13.3% last year did so on a $2.9B non-recurring investment mark, and marks reverse; the operating line grew 1.6%. Third, at 1.41% free-cash-flow yield and 0.89% dividend yield, the stock offers no valuation support from cash returns if the growth narrative slips — there is nothing underneath it.

What would make us wrong, stated fairly: the bull leg needs no re-rating at all, only stability. If the multiple simply holds at 38.5x and consensus delivers, you make 13% over two years plus 1.8% a year in yield. That is a perfectly reasonable outcome and it is why this is a Hold rather than an Avoid. The asymmetry is roughly balanced, tilted slightly negative: −21% to +18%.

11. Verdict, kill-criteria and flip conditions

Hold.

The business is not the question and we should be explicit about that. Walmart grew total revenues 4.7% to $713.163B at a $700B base, ran Walmart U.S. comparable sales at 4.3% for five consecutive reported periods, compounded eCommerce net sales 24.4% to $150.4B — now 21.3% of net sales — grew membership fee revenue 15.8% to $4.4B, generated $41.565B of operating cash flow, and carries 1.32x net leverage with 10.5x interest coverage and a beta of 0.603. That is one of the best operating records available in a listed company of this size, and nothing in this dive disputes it.

The price is the question, and it answers itself. At $111.55 you pay 38.5x fiscal 2027 consensus earnings, 34.1x fiscal 2028's, 41.3x trailing adjusted earnings and roughly 71x trailing free cash flow for a business whose consolidated operating margin contracted 13 basis points in fiscal 2026 and 8 more in Q1 fiscal 2027, whose reported 13.3% earnings growth was ~85% supplied by below-the-line investment marks, and whose free cash flow conversion has fallen from 102.6% to 68.2% on a capital programme running at 1.88x depreciation.

Our base fair value of $110 lands 1.4% below spot. That is a no-edge finding, and under house convention no-edge defaults to Hold. The asymmetry is −21% to +18%, slightly negative. We are 21% below the street's $140.27 target, but we are not below the street's earnings — the entire disagreement is what those earnings are worth.

On the 2026-08-20 print, 16 days out. This is outside the five-day banner rule but far too close to establish a position ahead of. Consensus is $0.742 adjusted (+9.1% year over year) on $186.806B of revenue (+5.3%). At 38.5x forward, an in-line print is not sufficient — the multiple requires acceleration, and the specific line that matters is operating income growth against net sales growth, which has been negative on a margin basis for two consecutive periods. Any accumulation should be staged after the print, not before it; a staged entry ahead of a print is not a Hold decision but a Buy — Tactical one, and we are not making that call here.

Pre-registered KILL criteria — what would move this to Avoid:

  • Walmart U.S. comparable sales below 3% in any reported quarter. Five consecutive periods at 4.3% is the load-bearing operating fact; losing it while the multiple is still near 38x removes the only leg the price stands on.
  • A third consecutive period of operating margin contraction. −13 basis points in fiscal 2026 and −8 in Q1 fiscal 2027 can be explained by the PhonePe charge and self-insurance claims; a third makes it structural and invalidates the entire mix-shift thesis.
  • Other gains and losses swinging to a net loss, as they did in fiscal 2025 (−$0.8B). This would strip the flattering component out of reported earnings per share and expose 1.6%-type operating growth to a 38x multiple in the same quarter.
  • Fiscal 2027 capital expenditure guided or tracking above $27B, extending the free-cash-flow squeeze into a fourth year and keeping the price-to-free-cash-flow multiple above 70x.
  • A material adverse development in the litigation stack — the Asda equal-value third-phase hearing beginning 2026-11-23, the opioid matters, the US Attorney money-transfer investigation, the COFECE proceeding against Walmex, or the Flipkart Foreign Direct Investment show-cause notice. The 10-Q states no loss or range of loss can be reasonably estimated for several.
  • Consensus fiscal 2028 earnings per share revised below $3.10 on the 25-analyst line, which would take the base case below $105.

Pre-registered FLIP TO BUY (upsize) conditions:

  • Operating income growing faster than net sales for two consecutive quarters. This is management's own stated definition of operating leverage, it is measurable at every print, and it would validate the mix-shift thesis in a single stroke. It is the single most important flip condition in this dive.
  • A price below ~$95 (roughly 29x fiscal 2028 consensus earnings, near the 52-week low of $95.42), at which the base case becomes +16% and the asymmetry inverts.
  • First disclosure of advertising or marketplace revenue as a separate figure, at a scale — say, above $8-10B annualised with disclosed margin characteristics — that materially changes the consolidated margin arithmetic.
  • Fiscal 2027 capital expenditure landing at or below $25B with free cash flow conversion recovering above 85%, taking the price-to-free-cash-flow multiple from ~71x toward the mid-40s.
  • Membership and other income growing above 20% for two consecutive quarters as the Sam's Club fee increase ($50→$60, $110→$120, effective 2026-05-01) recognises ratably through fiscal 2027-28.

Where WMT fits in the Synthos Framework Portfolio. The defensive / low-beta consumer sleeve, as a hold-if-owned, 0-1% new-money position. The justification for the low weight is not the business, which would justify a core position at a different price; it is that our fair value is at spot, the conviction lane is thin and non-specific on valuation, and the multiple has more room to compress than expand. If the position already exists, there is no reason to sell a 0.60-beta compounder with a fortress balance sheet into a 17.5% drawdown. If it does not exist, there is no reason to establish it at 38.5x forward earnings 16 days before a print. That is what Hold means here, precisely.

Single biggest risk: multiple compression. At 38.5x fiscal 2027 consensus earnings and roughly 71x trailing free cash flow, a drift to 28x on fiscal 2030 consensus earnings of $3.953 produces $110.7 — three years of zero price return with consensus delivered in full. Nothing about the business has to go wrong. The 10-K concedes the exposure in its own risk factors: "We believe that the price of our stock generally reflects high market expectations for our future operating results."

Most fragile assumption in the price: that the mix shift into advertising, marketplace, fulfilment services and membership expands the consolidated operating margin. Consensus requires net margin to widen 37 basis points between fiscal 2026 and fiscal 2029. The two most recent reported periods delivered −13 and −8 basis points of operating margin. The company sizes exactly one component of the mix — membership fee revenue at $4.4B, or 0.62% of revenue — and discloses none of the others. If that shift is real and arriving, this stock is worth more than $132. If it is a story the market has already paid for, it is worth less than $88. We cannot tell from the disclosure available, and neither can the market. That uncertainty, priced at 38.5x, is the Hold.
